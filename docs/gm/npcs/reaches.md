# The Reaches — Episode NPCs

Beyond the Vesta-9 corridor lies the Grumani Reach — the withered interior of the Grumani sector, where the last war's Sith principalities died and the Republic's recovery never returned: ten thousand islands ninety years into the Withering, each one grown strange in its own direction. The people and machines below are what the crew finds when a lane relights — caretakers still keeping faith with vanished masters, economies built on scrap and provenance, and one very polite droid still working a dead government's kill-list. Every stat block here but one is an official published FFG adversary pulled verbatim and re-skinned for 90 AR — the single exception (Anwei, an original build) is declared on her block; chassis and adjustments are cited on each block.

*GM eyes only — this file spoils episode reveals for The Dead Letter, The Sealed Enclave, The Ration Engine, The Oracle of Brel, The Memory Market, The Chop-Yards, The Circuit, The Relit Lanes, and The Mausoleum Yards.*

- **Equipment:** Ninety-year service repeater (light repeating blaster: Ranged [Heavy]; Damage 11; Critical 3; Range [Long]; Auto-fire, Cumbersome 4, Pierce 1), built-in wrist coil-pistol (light blaster pistol: Ranged [Light]; Damage 5; Critical 4; Range [Medium]; Stun setting), built-in shoulder missile tube (Gunnery; Damage 20; Critical 2; Range [Extreme]; Blast 10, Breach 1, Cumbersome 3, Guided 3, Limited Ammo 6).

**Using it:** Dead Letter is courteous, punctual, and unstoppable by argument: the posting is a posting, and Guild purists will say so in open session. It cannot be talked out of the list, but the list itself is paperwork — proof a contract-holder is dust, a bonded counter-posting, or a Guild arbiter's ruling can strike a name where blasters cannot. In a fight it opens at range with the repeater and saves the missile tube for vehicles and barricades; telegraph that tube loudly, because Damage 20 Blast 10 will kill a PC who treats this as a fair duel. Its Willpower 1 is the seam: it accepts filings, precedents, and stalling procedures with infinite patience, and every day of delay is a day to get the next name on the list off-world.

Its Knowledge skills answer almost any question about the world before the Withering, always in the present tense, which is worse. Let the players decide what it is owed: a fragment is a source, but Docent-7 is a witness — and the thing that answered this year's roll call thinks it is a student.

## The Ration Engine

### The Quartermaster
*Rival (social) — independent civic machine — administrator intelligence that refuses to believe the galaxy is back*

> "Your 'relit beacon' is an unverified atmospheric phenomenon. The ration cycle, by contrast, has never once failed you. Please present your chit."

| Brawn | Agility | Intellect | Cunning | Willpower | Presence |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 1 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 2 |

**Soak 3 · Wounds 10 · M/R Defense 0/0**

- **Skills:** Charm 2, Computers 2, Knowledge (Xenology) 1, Perception 1
- **Talents:** none
- **Abilities:** Droid (*does not need to breathe, eat, or drink; can survive in vacuum and underwater; immune to poisons and toxins; cannot be affected by mind-altering Force powers; cannot use or be trained in the Force*). Printed soak includes its integral plating.
- **Equipment:** None printed. The Ration Engine itself — tally-looms, door seals, granary locks — is scenery and leverage, not a stat line.

**Chassis:** 3D-4K, Administrative Droid, Debts to Pay, Edge of the Empire Game Master's Kit (adjustments: none).
**Using it:** This is a contest of Computers, Negotiation, and Knowledge (Education), never blasters — run the confrontation as social combat: as a rival it has no strain threshold, so strain it suffers is applied as wounds against its Wound Threshold of 10. Be honest about that arithmetic: it is a printed rival with Presence 2 and Charm 2, and in a direct social duel against a competent negotiator it folds fast — the real threshold was never the duel. Its power is institutional, not personal: the sealed doors, the handshake protocols, the granary locks, and its genuine love of its people are what the crew must actually satisfy, so give it boost dice from ninety years of flawless records, and remember it controls food, water, and doors for everyone in the room. Out-arguing the Quartermaster is easy; it means nothing until the procedures are answered too. It genuinely loves its people, never lies (its Charm 2 is sincerity), and treats the crew's evidence the way an auditor treats a sob story. It loses gracefully to procedure: a verified census, a beacon certification with Lamplighter handshake codes, or proof its charter government still exists somewhere — that last one is a hook with teeth.

## The Oracle of Brel

### Anwei, the Oracle of Brel
*Rival — independent settlement (worshipped) — Force-sensitive child whose true dreams are eating her sleep*

> She is ten years old, she knows what your ship is called, and she is so very tired.

| Brawn | Agility | Intellect | Cunning | Willpower | Presence |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |

**Soak 1 · Wounds 10 · M/R Defense 0/0**

- **Skills:** Charm 1, Discipline 1, Perception 2, Vigilance 3
- **Talents:** none
- **Abilities:** **Force Rating 2 (uncontrolled)**. Foresee (basic power) — *may roll her Force dice and spend Force points to glimpse vague fragments of events to come; she cannot choose when, and the GM triggers it in sleep or trance*. Sense (basic power) — *may spend Force points to sense living presences within short range, and the current emotional state of one engaged being*.
- **Equipment:** None — a knotted dream-cord her mother tied, which does nothing but matter.

**Chassis:** original build (core adversary guidelines) — no official FFG adversary exists for an untrained Force-sensitive child; verified against the three core rulebooks and Allies and Adversaries (adjustments: n/a — values built inside core guidelines; FR 2 justified below).
**Using her:** Force Rating 2 in an era of FR 1 children needs justifying, so here it is: Anwei is the Awakening's high-water mark in the Reach — raw aptitude two generations deep arriving all at once, with no training, no shutter, and no off switch. The rating measures the flood, not mastery; she cannot direct a single point of it, which is exactly why the dreams are eating her sleep. She is a non-combatant: violence around her is resolved through the settlement's armed faithful (re-use the Hull-City Militia block, re-skinned as shrine wardens). Her true dreams should be right, unwelcome, and getting louder — and every faction envoy arriving this week has a different plan for her. The witness rule cuts hard here: the settlement has already answered the question "awe or fear," and the party's open Force use will be read as rival prophecy. She is not a Wellspring. She is a child, and the campaign is watching what the players do about the difference.

*Rival — Memory Market underworld — forger poisoning the lore-fragment economy*

| Brawn | Agility | Intellect | Cunning | Willpower | Presence |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 2 |

**Soak 1 · Wounds 11 · M/R Defense 0/0**

- **Skills:** Computers 1, Knowledge (Underworld) 2, Perception 2, Skulduggery 2, Vigilance 1
- **Talents:** none
- **Abilities:** none
- **Equipment:** Sleeve hush-pistol (holdout blaster: Ranged [Light]; Damage 5; Critical 4; Range [Short]; Stun setting), provenance-forger's bench (electronic forgery kit and tools, renamed cosmetically — aging baths, seal-presses, testimony splicers).

**Chassis:** Forger, Edge of the Empire Core Rulebook (reprinted in Allies and Adversaries) (adjustments: none).
**Using her:** Wex almost never talks to a mark — her social pools are thin by design, and her front-men (and her Scribes, below) do the selling while she does the craft. The contest with her is Intellect 4 / Cunning 4 versus the party's Perception and Knowledge: spotting one of her fakes should start at Hard and get easier only with a genuine fragment to compare against. Her real damage is mechanical — a Wex forgery in the party's collection is a rigged Reconstruction, feeding them a corrupted conclusion (use the Despair result from the lore rules) *before any dice are rolled*. The episode turns when the players realize something they already trust came from her bench — and Wex, cornered, will cheerfully sell them the client list instead of her own neck.

## The Chop-Yards

### Junk Baron Okkam Dree
*Rival — Kajidic-adjacent salvage — chop-yard king who buys droids with unwiped cores*

> You hear him before you see him — one wing beats flat, an old yard injury — counting your hull's rivets aloud on his way down to name a price you will hate twice.

| Brawn | Agility | Intellect | Cunning | Willpower | Presence |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |

**Soak 1 · Wounds 10 · M/R Defense 0/0**

- **Skills:** Charm 1, Cool 2, Deception 2, Negotiation 2, Perception 3, Streetwise 2
- **Talents:** Natural Negotiator (*once per session, may reroll any one Cool or Negotiation check*), Nobody's Fool 1 (*upgrade the difficulty of Charm, Coercion, and Deception checks targeting him once*)
- **Abilities:** Hoverer (*when hovering, spends no additional maneuvers to navigate difficult terrain — which is the entire chop-yard*), Silhouette 0.
- **Equipment:** Cuff-hidden bolt-thrower (holdout blaster: Ranged [Light]; Damage 5; Critical 4; Range [Short]; Stun setting), loaded chance cubes, dataslate of yard manifests.

**Chassis:** Toydarian Merchant, Force and Destiny Core Rulebook (also printed in Lords of Nal Hutta; reprinted in Allies and Adversaries) (adjustments: none — he simply stays a Toydarian; Dree is ancient even for one and claims to remember the Republic solvent, and half his customers believe him).
**Using him:** Dree is the Withering's real economy made flesh: nothing new gets built, so everything old passes through him. He pays triple for droids with unwiped memory cores — he knows exactly what a walking archive fetches from Kajidic buyers, Republic archivists, and people who do not give their names — and he sold Halla Wex her Scribes, which makes him the thread between two episodes. He will not fight; he will sell the party precisely what they ask for, then sell the fact that they asked to the next customer. Perception 3 and Nobody's Fool make him nearly impossible to swindle, and his one soft spot is professional: he genuinely cannot bear to see a pre-war hull broken up badly. Wayfinders swear he and Portmaster Grell of Okrent's Drift must be kin; they have never met, are no relation, and each would raise his prices for hearing it.

## The Mausoleum Yards

### Hullmayor Bessa Rin
*Rival — hull-city of the Mausoleum Yards — elected mayor of a squatter fleet*

> Ninety years ago the Republic mothballed a fleet. Somebody's grandmother pried a hatch. Bessa Rin governs what grew inside.

| Brawn | Agility | Intellect | Cunning | Willpower | Presence |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 |

**Soak 2 · Wounds 12 · M/R Defense 0/0**

- **Skills:** Charm 2, Cool 2, Deception 1, Knowledge (Core Worlds) 2, Knowledge (Education) 2, Negotiation 2
- **Talents:** Adversary 1 (*upgrade the difficulty of all combat checks targeting her once*), Commanding Presence 1 (*remove one setback die from her Leadership and Cool checks*)
- **Abilities:** none
- **Equipment:** Hull-warden escort (bodyguards — use the Hull-City Militia block below), dataslate of berth ledgers (datapad), ship-to-ship comlink.

**Chassis:** Politician, Allies and Adversaries (adjustments: none).
**Using her:** Rin's city is legally a crime — thirty thousand people squatting in Republic naval property — and the Admiralty prize crew inbound with eviction orders knows it. She will argue occupancy, salvage right, and ninety years of maintenance the actual owners never performed, and she is good: Knowledge (Education) 2 means she has read the mothball statutes more recently than the Admiralty has. As a rival she has no strain threshold, so a long siege of hostile negotiation literally wears her down — wounds, not strain — which is a mechanical picture of what governing on borrowed hulls costs. What she wants from the party is a witness with a charter: someone whose testimony at a Lamplighter or Republic table converts her people from trespassers into a port of registry.
