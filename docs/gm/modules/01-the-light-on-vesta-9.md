# Session One — The Light on Vesta-9

*The GM-screen module. Run the session from this page. Every marked name opens its stat block in [Vesta-9 & the Crew's Orbit](../npcs/session-one.md); subsystem links go to the mechanics pages the session introduces.*

## Overview

**The session's jobs**, in rising order of consequence: teach the [lore fragment](../../mechanics/lore-fragments.md) subsystem at the moment a single page is worth a ship's life; put three factions on the ground in front of the crew — the Vigil, the Admiralty, the Lamplighters — each wanting something real and paying for it; put the crew on the record — they are witnesses to a lighting nobody claims, holding evidence nobody else has, and what they say about it in front of three factions becomes their reputation; and open the corridor into the Grumani Reach — the first dead leg of the [Duros Space Run](../../setting/geography.md) — where [Act 1](../../campaign/act-1.md) lives. If the table drives home arguing about what they put on the paper — and what they kept off it — the session worked.

**What the players should be holding when it ends:** a certified corridor with their names on the paper; 1,200 scrip minus whatever Scene 1 cost them; at least one logged fragment and the Question it bears on; a standing witness-charter offer from the guild; a position, on paper, on who lit Vesta-9; and — if they earned it — Osk's original page 40, her claim that somebody has been flying the dead Run for years, and a standing invitation to come back for the rest of the book. That last one is the campaign.

**The spine** — five beats, about four hours with a break:

| Beat | Section | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| Grounded, then the sky changes | Cold Open | ~20 min |
| The scramble off-world | Scene 1 | ~50 min |
| The race up a dead lane | Scene 2 | ~40 min |
| The standoff at the light | Scene 3 | ~60 min |
| The record, and the way out | Scene 4 + The Out | ~55 min |

**Cut for time, never for weight.** If the clock runs hot: compress the fuel problem to one roll, hand the fragment over via whichever vector is nearest, and let the *Steadfast* arrive early to squeeze Scene 3. Never cut Osk's bedside, the moment the paper asks who lit it, or the closing image — the session is remembered by those three, in that order.

**Prep checklist** — seven items, half an hour:

1. Tie each PC's Obligation to the grounding at Okrent's Drift — one written sentence per PC. The table in the Cold Open does most of the work; personalize the names.
2. Print the [Reading the Dice](../../mechanics/dice-results.md) player sheets, one per player. This session leans on Advantage/Threat spends and the sheets keep the table fast.
3. Keep the [session one NPC file](../npcs/session-one.md) open — the whole cast is statted there, marines and minion math included.
4. Pick two or three [Act 1 episode seeds](../../campaign/act-1.md) to place along the Duros Space Run chain (see [the geography page](../../setting/geography.md) for the real systems, Enarc through Jutrand) and pencil them onto the [beacon map](../tools/beacon-map.md).
5. Make the fragment card: *Grandmother Osk's rutter, page 40* — form, tag, Question, and its load-bearing claim written on a physical card you can hand across the table.
6. Decide privately what @@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@ saw. She was at the *dark* station before it lit — praying at a dead shrine when the light came on around her — and she has no ship anyone can find. Neither she nor anyone on camera lit the beacon: the Sith apprentice did, tonight, within the hour of the boards firing, and was gone before the first hull from the Drift could arrive (Dial 3). Whether Vhail glimpsed a figure, a hull, or nothing at all is yours; her psalm about doors covers all three. The Inheritor observer is an unremarkable hull in the arrival swarm (see the danger box below) — decide only which hull.
7. Set the famous number: the posted Vesta-9 prize, unclaimed for years, that every broke spacer at Okrent's Drift knows by heart. **12,000 scrip** is the default. It is ten times the witness fee, and the difference between them is Scene 4.

!!! danger "GM only — Dial 3 is on the field"
    Vesta-9 was lit by the Sith apprentice, tonight, deliberately — the line's one exception to its own rule that **the Sith never light beacons** (they fly the dark lanes without them; beacons are for the galaxy, because the Scrub works behind public reopening). The exception was forced: ten years of the patron's money never bought the one chart that finds the station (Osk's; see Scene 2), so Mendac hunted it off-pattern for years, found it, opened the principate cache it was guarding — and the holocron was not there. So she lit the beacon on her way out: the search has to move up the lane, the only machine that moves it is the guild's advance, and the advance was stalled on exactly this beacon. Even her dead ends advance the plan — and the galaxy's comfortable wrong answer ("some prize-hunter who didn't live to file") was part of the calculation. (Optional body for the Question, for later acts: the hands who flew her search were hired and did not outlive it — a drifting hulk off the lane can one day answer "who lit Vesta-9?" with corpses and a worse question.) Nothing on the field knows any of this, and nothing should. What *is* on the field is an Inheritor observer — grave-robbers notice when a tomb opens — an unremarkable hull in the arrival swarm that files no claim and asks no questions. Do not cast them, do not name them, do not let the camera find them. They pay off silently in The Out. If the crew examines the station, the honest traces are these: the core was woken by hand, competently, by someone who knew the old protocols; one docking cradle is still warm; and the station is not where the guild's charts say it is — whoever lit it knew where it was. See [GM Truths](../truths.md).

!!! tip "Table talk before the dice"
    If session zero did not already do it, say the era's thesis line aloud before the cold open — *nobody broke the galaxy; everyone just stopped holding it up* — and remind the table that in this era the Force is not a skill, it is an event. Both lines pay for themselves by Scene 4.

## Cold Open — Okrent's Drift

The tableau: a boomtown that bet on the Run reopening and has spent years losing the bet. Bannistar's fuel gantries were raised new to feed the Lamplighters' advance up the Duros Space Run — four beacons relit in a decade, every prize paid — and then the next beacon, Vesta-9, refused to be found, and Okrent's Drift slid from boomtown into recession. The crew's ship is bolted, their cargo is impounded, and their Obligations have found the address. Let them stew in it for ten minutes of roleplay before the sky changes — offer each player one small vignette of being broke:

- Haggling at the commissary over a meal that costs more than it did yesterday, because everything does.
- Watching the twice-daily lane departure from the fence line, with somebody else's manifest on it.
- A courier packet arriving months old — news from home that was already decided before they could have answered it.
- The Obligation itself knocking: a collector, a cousin, a writ, a rumor. One knock each; use the table below.

> Okrent's Drift at dusk is the smell of fuel and the sound of somebody else's ship leaving. Your own sits under the depot lights with a port-authority bolt clamped to the landing gear like a brass tumor, and the cargo that was going to fix everything is behind a fence with your name misspelled on the seizure tag. The lane out of here still runs; you can watch it, twice a day, taking other people places.

**Why *you* are stuck here** — invoke each PC's Obligation as the specific reason the Drift has them:

| Obligation | The grounding, personalized |
|---|---|
| **Debt** | The impound *is* the collection. @@npc:Portmaster Grell@@'s ledger carries your marker's interest, and the Kajidic dues-taker at the gate knows your face. |
| **Family** | The cargo is the lifeline — medicine or parts consigned for your homeworld, seized with the hold. |
| **Oath** | The dying archivist's last commission is in those crates, and the deadline was last week. |
| **Notice** | An Admiralty conscription writ reached this port before you did. Lifting without clearance confirms everything it says about you. |
| **Secret** | Someone on this field knows — and the price of their quiet was the cargo. |
| **Obsession** | You spent the fuel money on a fragment lead that went nowhere. That is *why* you cannot pay Grell. |
| **Responsibility** | Your refugees are camped at the field's edge; feeding them is where the scrip went. |
| **Contract** | The Lamplighters hold your ship's papers, and their factor here grounded you pending arrears. Remember this in Scene 3: Chartmistress Nerra is your creditor's colleague. |
| **Posted** | A Guild hunter is drinking in the cantina, deciding whether the posting on you still bonds. Lifting fast is suddenly very attractive. |

Then the sky changes:

> It starts as a murmur on the depot's public band, then every nav board in the port lights at once. Vesta-9 — dark since your grandparents' time, a dead name on old charts — is broadcasting, clean and steady, like sixty years never happened. Across the field a freighter spins up its lifters without waiting for clearance, and then everybody is running.

In this port nobody needs the stakes explained — they have been the port's whole conversation for years — so put the *disbelief* in an NPC's mouth instead (any dockhand): *"Five years of surveys. Best chartkeepers in the guild. Nobody could find it. And it just — came on."* Then the famous number, said the way people say a lottery number: the posted prize, plus salvage rights and a corridor of worlds nobody has priced in ninety years — *for whoever gets there and gets it certified.* Nobody on the field has stopped to ask who could claim a prize for a beacon nobody lit. They will. Cut to the scramble.

## Scene 1 — The Scramble

Three problems between the crew and the sky: the impound, the fuel, and the fact that everyone else heard the same broadcast. Run it as an obstacle course with a clock ticking audibly — every ten minutes of table time, narrate another ship lifting.

**The field at a glance** — four stations; the party can and should split up:

| Where | What is there |
|---|---|
| The impound office | The bolt release, the ledger, and a portmaster who has never once torn up a ledger — only repriced it. |
| The fuel queue | 150-scrip lift-and-jump loads, a line out the gate, and the *Larksong* holding the head of it. |
| The cantina gantry | The rumor network at full boil: the chart-broker, the wager board, and word of who has already lifted. |
| The depot infirmary | Tama Osk, the vacuum-lunged old Duros who flew the Run when it was whole — and, tonight, the guild factor who got to her bedside first. See Scene 2's third vector. |

**The impound.** @@npc:Portmaster Grell@@ hovers at eye level behind his ledger-slate so nobody can look down on him. Listed price: **450 scrip** release plus a **60-scrip lift tax**. Assume the crew can raise about a third of that — say so out loud; the shortfall is the scene. He is venal, not cruel: every fee has a listed price, and he honors what he signs. His opening line, delivered while the beacon news howls outside his window: *"Everyone in this port suddenly has somewhere to be. Prices reflect demand. Yours reflects history."* If violence so much as clears its throat, he drops behind the desk and starts naming lower numbers — keep him alive; a portmaster who owes the crew goodwill is Act 1's best recurring door.

| Approach | Check | It works | The downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haggle his angle | **Average Negotiation**, upgraded once for his savvy (Natural Negotiator lets him force one reroll) | He would rather own a piece of a rumor than scrip today: the debt converts to a signed 15% of any first-lighting fee. Advantage shaves it to 10%. | Threat: the 60-scrip lift tax stays, cash up front. |
| Lie to him | **Average Deception**, upgraded once by his Nobody's Fool | The bolt comes off on the strength of a forged consignment or an invented patron. | Discovered — a failed check now, or any Despair in the port later — he doubles the release to 900, honors nothing unsigned, and says your names to the Kajidic dues-taker. Collections will find you (+5 to a Debt Obligation, or seed one). |
| Slip the bolt | **Hard Skulduggery** on the impound clamp | A quiet release and no paper at all. | Threat: the tamper logs — Grell knows before you clear the gravity well. Despair: the bolt's tracer fires on lift; the debt beats you to Vesta-9 in the guild post. |
| Pay | 510 scrip they do not have | The dockside Kajidic lender advances it in minutes. | Ruinous interest: Debt Obligation +5, and the young Hutt's clerk smiles as she files it. |

**The fuel.** The depot queue is suddenly the longest line on the planet. A lift-and-jump load runs **150 scrip**. Options: **Average Negotiation** with the depot clerk for deferral against the first-lighting fee; **Average Streetwise** for an off-book half-load at 90; or the kindness below.

**The rivals.** Holding the head of the fuel queue: the @@npc:Larksong crew@@, three spacers in patched vac-jackets who finish each other's sentences, and their captain — @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@, who waves at the crew from her boarding ramp like an old friend, because by the third race she genuinely will be. Everyone in the port knows the *Larksong*: hers is the crew that claimed the Triffis prize, the last one the guild paid before the Run stalled, and the wager board has her as the favorite for this one before she finishes waving. Play her fast, warm, and better-prepared: she names the wager (loser buys the winner's table a round, every port, for a season), and if the crew is short on fuel she splits her allocation without being asked — "I want to *beat* you, not lap you." She is the recurring foil; nothing in this scene should make her an enemy. Her ship is statted — @@npc:The Larksong, prize-money YT-1300@@ — and it is the crew's own pattern with the Triffis prize spent on it: Speed 4 to their 3, a Class 1 hyperdrive to their Class 2, and no backup hyperdrive at all. Let the players hear the turbine before they see the ship. And before the crew leaves the queue, she flips them a folded flimsi from her jacket: a fresh sheet of **jump-knots** — astrogation teasers off the cantina rack, the spacer's crossword, two bits at the commissary. *"Drop these with Grandmother Osk in the infirmary — I bring her the new sheet every time I'm through, and tonight I'm racing you. She's blind, so you'll have to read them to her. She does them in her head faster than you can read. Bring your manners."* It is a genuinely trivial errand, and Marrow thinks nothing of it. It is also the most important thing anyone hands the crew tonight — see Scene 2's third vector.

**Advantage/Threat menu** — tuned to this scene:

| Result showing | Spend it as |
|---|---|
| 1 Advantage | A dockhand throws in charged cells: remove a setback die from the crew's next shipboard Mechanics check. |
| 2 Advantage | Grell lets slip the count — how many ships have lifted, and that the *Larksong* is fueled but waiting on a chart. |
| 2 Threat | The Kajidic dues-taker at the gate writes the crew's names in her book. |
| 3 Threat | Fuel-line delay: the *Larksong* lifts first, visibly, with a cheerful wave. |
| Triumph | Grell smells profit and flips: release fee waived for a signed 10%, and he throws in the depot's sixty-year-old lane chart — a boost die in Scene 2. |
| Despair | The impound bolt scars the gear coming off: the ship starts Scene 2 carrying 2 system strain. |

!!! tip "If it drags"
    Marrow pays. She strolls into the impound office mid-haggle, counts out Grell's 510 from her winnings pouch, and tells the crew they owe her "one page's worth" — then races them anyway. It ends the scene and hands Scene 2 its fragment vector.

## Scene 2 — The Race

Frame it mechanically before anyone rolls: this session *is* the [beacon-relighting set piece](../../mechanics/beacon-relighting.md), run out of order. **Wake it** was done by an unknown hand — that mystery is the campaign's. The crew is now flying **Chart it**, and **Speak to it** waits at the station.

**The jump.** The Run's first dead leg — Bannistar Station toward Enarc — has decayed past its nav data: **Hard Astrogation**, exactly per the beacon rules. A rutter page bearing on the route adds a boost die — this is the fragment lesson, taught at the moment it matters. Jumping blind is legal but earns a setback die for sixty years of drift silt.

**While the navigator plots** — one supporting beat per other PC, so nobody watches one person roll:

| Crewmate | Check | On success |
|---|---|---|
| The engineer | **Average Mechanics**, coaxing the heirloom drives | Remove the setback from any Scene 1 hull damage, or clear 1 system strain mid-run. |
| The slicer | **Average Computers**, scrubbing sixty years of silt from the nav data | Add a boost die to the Astrogation check. |
| The lookout | **Average Perception** at the reversion points | The dead drift buoys read a heartbeat early — downgrade one Despair-shaped surprise to a Threat-shaped one, narratively. |
| The captain | **Average Leadership**, calling the run | Recover 2 strain across the crew; the ship flies like the crew believes in it. |

!!! tip "The house spends are live"
    This is the first scene where the [Ember Age spend table](../../mechanics/dice-results.md) earns its keep: 2 Advantage on a relight stage hands a boost to whoever tackles the next one, 2 Threat on decayed-lane Astrogation leaks the route to a rival, and a Despair means the relight broadcasts wider than intended. Let a player find the table themselves; approve generously.

**How they get the page** — three vectors, use whichever the fiction reaches first:

- **The Marrow trade.** @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@ owns a copy of Grandmother Osk's rutter — the *old* log, the living-days route every Run family traded around. She will trade page 40 for the crew's signature backing the *Larksong* as co-witnesses at certification — both crews on the guild paper, whoever docks first. (If she paid Grell in Scene 1, this is the "one page's worth" coming home.) The copy gives the boost die. It does not carry the claim. (Her jump-knot errand from Scene 1 is a separate, genuinely trivial thing — and it is what puts the crew at the bedside below.)
- **The cantina purchase.** A chart-broker under the depot gantry sells a fair copy of the same old log for 80 scrip — or for the full, embarrassing story of how the crew lost their cargo. He collects embarrassments. Boost die; no claim — and a hint, thrown in free: *"Mine's a copy. The original's owner is dying about forty meters from here. She doesn't sell."*
- **The bedside.** Tama Osk — the rutter's author, vacuum-lunged in the depot infirmary. Play her as a Duros somewhere past a hundred and fifty, blind these last two years, entirely unafraid: she flew the Run when it was whole, kept flying its dark stretches for decades after everyone else stopped, and speaks in a dry rasp that costs her something to use. (No stat block; she never leaves the bed.) If the table wants the quiet beat before the storm, give it to them: a crew member reads the first jump-knot aloud and the blind woman solves it before the sentence ends — the port thinks she is a relic; she is the sharpest navigator the crew has ever been in a room with, and now they know it before anyone mentions a chart. Under her pillow is the **original** — her dark-years marginalia: true positions, drift shadows, which buoys were warm — the one chart in the galaxy that finds Vesta-9 without a broadcast to home on. **What the crew walks in on.** However they arrive — Marrow's parcel, the broker's hint, their own reasons — they arrive mid-scene: a **guild factor** (unstatted; borrow the Beacon-Tender block from [Lanes & Ledgers](../npcs/lanes-ledgers.md) if dice are demanded) is at the bed, repeating the offer the guild has made for ten years, louder tonight, because the relight changes everything: the advance resumes, the surveys will need the dark Run's true positions for Enarc and every hop beyond, her book is worth more than it has ever been — and she is dying, behind on her infirmary tab, which is his leverage. **The deed** — no prompt, no promised reward: the crew can throw him out (he is a coward with paperwork), out-argue him (**Average Negotiation or Discipline**; he has quotas, not convictions), or kill his leverage by paying the tab — **90 scrip** they badly need tonight, or time they can hear costing them lifts. Doing nothing is allowed: he wins slowly, she says nothing, the parcel is delivered, and the crew never learns what was under the pillow — the copies still teach the fragment lesson, and the second audition (below) stays open. **The down payment.** If they act, she chooses — and says why, citing what this table actually did: *"Everyone in this port is running toward money tonight. You just spent yours on a stranger's bed-tab. Sit down."* What she gives is **not the book.** It is **page 40, the original** — margins no copy carries — plus the claim, said aloud, free: *"Somebody has been flying that lane for years. The buoys were warm when I went back. And the guild's prizes go exactly where those people wanted to go."* And the promise, both halves: *"When it answers you, tell it Osk finished the run. And don't sell my page to the people who were just in this room."* If the crew keeps the first half — says her name into the beacon's carrier during certification — give them a boost die on the Speak-to-it check, and note that Vhail hears it, and looks at the crew for the first time like they might be worth talking to. Then the hook: *"Come back and tell me what you found up there. Then we'll talk about the rest of the book."* **The inheritance (not tonight).** The whole rutter and the whole story — the coreward "antiquarians," the survey she flew before any prize was posted, the places that should have been empty, the people who did not come back — are a *return* scene, a session or two from now. She will already have heard what the crew put on the record, and whether a certain name went into the carrier; most crews never come back to a dying woman once they have what they need, and that is the second audition. When she hands the book over she has weeks left; her funeral is Act 1's first, and the port watches the nobodies carry Grandmother Osk's coffin and wonders what she left them. (Deliberate pacing: the crew should hold the only true chart of the dark Run by roughly the second corridor — when it becomes Act 1's engine, and the target on their hull.) **Why not Marrow:** Osk loves her, and the guild owns her — the Triffis prize made the *Larksong* the Lamplighters' poster crew, and Osk's one hard criterion is *owes the guild nothing*. The old log was her consolation gift years ago, and Marrow has never once asked about the original, which is part of why Osk loves her. The jump-knot errand is exactly what it looks like: a friend's small habit, handed off in a hurry — Marrow has no idea what it set in motion. If the crew ever tells her what happened at that bedside, she is quiet a moment, and then: *"Good."*

When the page lands, hand over the card and log it (if the crew has only a copy, log the card with the claim blank — and let them notice the blank):

@@action:fragment {"name":"Grandmother Osk's rutter, page 40","form":"rutter","tag":"Civic","question":"Who lit Vesta-9?","claim":"somebody has been flying the dead Run for years, and the prizes follow them","holder":"crew","status":"trusted"}@@

!!! tip "Teach the subsystem out loud"
    Say the rules as you hand the card: it has a **form**, a **tag** (Civic), and it bears on a named **Question** — *Who lit Vesta-9?* It boosts related Knowledge checks; three fragments on one Question unlock a [Reconstruction](../../mechanics/lore-fragments.md). Open the [fragment tracker](../tools/fragment-tracker.md) where the players can see it. Fragments from different tags always disagree on one load-bearing fact — write Osk's claim down now so the contradiction is loaded before anyone notices.

**Outcomes ladder:**

| The roll | What happens |
|---|---|
| Success | Clean jump. First ship in — except for the keeper already kneeling at the core. The crew gets perhaps an hour of quiet at the station before the sky fills. |
| Success with 3+ Threat, or failure | Slow jump: drift-silt reversions, two recalculations. They arrive to find the @@npc:BNS Steadfast, Bastion-pattern Patrol Sloop@@ already on station with its interdiction broadcast running. |
| Despair (regardless of success) | Misjump scare: reversion inside the shoal short of the station. The ship suffers 3 system strain and limps the last leg — the crew arrives *last*, standoff already assembled. |

**Neck-and-neck.** Mid-run, the lane forces a mid-course recalculation at a collapsed drift buoy, and the *Larksong* reverts alongside. The crew's fresh solution shows what the older copies miss: the buoy's mass shadow has drifted into the third leg, dead across the *Larksong*'s plotted line. The choice: **comm the warning** — lose the lead, arrive together or second, and cement a friendship the campaign will spend for years — or **stay silent** — arrive first while the @@npc:Larksong crew@@ limps in behind a scarred hull, and start the rivalry cold. Do not soften it; the table should feel the price of whichever they buy. And remember who Marrow is: the wager board's favorite, the crew that claimed the last prize the guild ever paid. Nobodies who beat the champion by warning her are a story the port tells for a season. Nobodies who beat her by letting her hull get scarred are a different story, and the port tells that one too.

## Scene 3 — The Beacon

> The station hangs where no chart promised — three light-hours off the guild's plot, exactly where Osk's page says — and it is lit: running lights, docking ring, the long lattice of the beacon mast all burning like a festival. Under everything, on every band, the carrier hums: one clear note repeating its name to a galaxy that stopped listening. Nothing about it looks sixty years dead. Something about it looks *swept*. And the swept places look very, very recent.

**Occupancy timeline** — who is where, keyed to the Scene 2 ladder:

| When | On station |
|---|---|
| Before the light | @@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@, kneeling at the beacon core, praying. She was here when it was dark. No ship anyone can find. She answers every "how" with a psalm about doors. |
| Within the hour | Whoever lit it — gone. One docking cradle on the ring is still warm to the touch, and nothing is in it. |
| The crew's arrival | Per the outcomes ladder — first, second, or last. |
| +40 minutes (or already there) | The @@npc:BNS Steadfast, Bastion-pattern Patrol Sloop@@ arrives off its fleet tender's clamps — the tender has been loitering off Bannistar for months, recruiting in a recession — runs out its guns, and broadcasts: all vessels stand to for inspection under the Corridor Security Act — a law the Admiralty passed for itself. |
| +90 minutes | @@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@'s guild packet arrives trailing the swarm from the Drift. She requests docking "as certifying observer," ink already wet. |
| Throughout | The arrival swarm: a dozen hulls from Okrent's Drift — and one that files no claim (see The Out). |

**Running order.** Let the scene breathe in this sequence, whatever the arrival math: (1) the crew and the empty, humming station — and Vhail, who does not look up; (2) the *Steadfast*'s broadcast, which turns every hopeful in the swarm into an audience; (3) the inspection; (4) Nerra's docking request, timed to interrupt whatever is tensest; (5) certification. Scene 4 detonates the moment the paper asks who lit it.

**The three-cornered standoff.** This is a social scene with a warship in it, not a combat with dialogue. Each corner wants something *from the crew*, and each has a lever:

| | @@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@ | @@npc:Commander Joss Reike@@ | @@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wants** | The core treated as a shrine — no slicing, no salvage claim on the station — and the crew's honest account of what they found. | All vessels stand to: manifests, inspection — and one signed line in his log that this corridor "requires naval protection." A stranger on the lane would do nicely. | To certify the relight with the crew signed as witnesses of record — *clean*: unknown hand, lane clear — and, if it exists, to buy the chart that found this place. |
| **Lever** | She knows more about the old network and the old Order than anyone else alive at this table. Answers are her currency; she trades them for reverence, never scrip. | The only warship in the sky, and the procedure that justifies using it. | No guild certificate, no first-lighting fee — and she decides whose names go on the paper. |
| **Red line** | Anyone cutting into the core housing, or claiming the Vigil blessed this lighting. | Armed defiance, or humiliation in front of his marines. | Forged guild marks, or claiming the fee around her. |
| **Compliance earns** | One true answer to any one question — a Jedi-tag lead — and the Vigil's doors marked friendly. | Correct treatment, a fuel chit drawn on the *Steadfast*, a clean record in the log. | The fee, the witness charter, accreditation — real value, honestly paid. Her Deception 3 is for what the offer *omits*. |
| **Refusal earns** | Your names in her psalter under a heading you cannot read; sealed places stay sealed to you. | Refuse *lawfully*, on the record, and he salutes — he respects people who beat him by the book. Refuse unlawfully and the ladder starts. | Perfect politeness, an "unwitnessed" filing, and every guild door in the Reach swinging politely shut. |

**The *Larksong* at the light.** @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@ docks per the race's outcome and parks the @@npc:Larksong crew@@ in visible, cheerful neutrality. If the Scene 2 trade was made, she backs the crew's account to Nerra and expects her names on the certificate; if the race ended cold, she bids against them at Nerra's table — graciously, and hard. She offers Reike nothing but compliance and offers Vhail a respectful nod she does not explain. Her testimony, not her pistol, is what she brought aboard.

**Advantage/Threat in the standoff** — a menu for the social rolls:

| Result showing | Spend it as |
|---|---|
| 1 Advantage | A hairline crack shows: Reike's marines trade a look at the word "protection"; Vhail's hand tightens on the psalter at "children"; Nerra's smile stalls, once, at "handshake codes." |
| 2 Advantage | A concession in the paper: strike one clause of Nerra's fine print, or win a named exemption in Reike's log. |
| 3 Advantage | A private audience: one of the three steps aside with the crew, off the record, and says one true thing. |
| 2 Threat | The swarm gets bold: a hopeful hull makes a move on the station, and Reike's posture hardens for everyone. |
| 3 Threat | The three corners agree on exactly one thing — the crew is the problem — and all three levers press at once. |
| Despair | The inspection widens, immediately: Reike wants the aft hold opened *now*. Cut to Scene 4. |

**Poking at the light.** Someone will examine the station — let them. **Average Mechanics or Computers** at the core establishes three facts, delivered flatly: the failsafes were released *from inside*, in the correct order, with no forced panels; the wake sequence used valid guild handshake codes; and whoever did it cleaned up afterward — within the last few hours. **Average Perception** on the docking ring adds a fourth: one cradle is still warm, and no ship anyone can find left it. And any navigator with Osk's page adds the fifth without a roll: the station is three light-hours from where the guild's charts put it, which is why nobody ever found it dark — and means whoever lit it *knew where it was*. Say them all and move on — do not explain. (GM only: these are the session's hard clues toward the Conclave rigging and the patron behind it. Vhail did not do this, whatever the players conclude; Nerra goes noticeably quiet if shown the handshake logs, and quieter at the name Osk.)

**Vhail's trade.** She will answer exactly one question truthfully and completely, for the price of visible reverence at the core — a moment of honest silence is enough. Sample answers, each a thread the campaign can pull:

| If they ask… | She answers |
|---|---|
| "Who lit it?" | "Not my order. We do not wake things. Ask instead who *benefits* from an opened door — and notice who arrives already knowing which doors come next." |
| "How did you get here?" | A psalm about doors — but if the crew told the truth on the record by session's end, she adds, quietly: "I was here before the light. I have not yet decided what that makes me." |
| Anything about the old Order, the Valley, or relic law | A true, careful answer worth a Jedi-tag lead — name where a fragment might be found in the Grumani Reach, keyed to one of your seeded episode worlds. Good defaults from [Keepers of the Flame](../npcs/keepers.md): a four-generation lightsaber sitting unrecognized on the Memory Market at Sanrafsix, or a dry hill on Veshet that the locals leave alone and will not say why. |

**The inspection.** Reike boards with four @@npc:Bastion Marines (Steadfast boarding party)@@ — one minion group: 20 wounds, 3 ranks in its group skills, weapons set to stun for a registered boarding. If the crew's papers are honest, no roll: he is correct to the last button and thanks them. Contraband or false manifests need **Hard Deception** (his Discipline 4 misses very little), or **Hard Skulduggery** *before* the boarding to re-stow. A failed check costs confiscation and a log entry, not blood. Either way, the inspection reaches the nav console — and if Osk's original is aboard, a marine finds a dark-lane chart annotated in a hand that is not the crew's, and Reike's eyebrows go up exactly once. He does not confiscate it. He *notes* it. **Cut to Scene 4 mid-inspection.**

!!! danger "If blasters come out"
    Be honest with yourself about the @@npc:BNS Steadfast, Bastion-pattern Patrol Sloop@@: a patrol sloop's gun ladder against a century-old heirloom hull is not a fight, it is an arrest. Do not stage a space battle the crew can win — the sloop cripples their engines with precise, throttled turbolaser fire, narrated as disabling rather than rolled, and the marines board under stun with Reike directing from cover (Tactical Direction; the fight is always the group, never the man, and his Adversary 1 punishes dueling him anyway). How everyone climbs down: any *lawful* proposal — "we submit under protest, log it" — stands Reike down instantly; Nerra interposes paper ("a certification hearing needs living witnesses, Commander"); Vhail kneels at the core and begins the psalm for the dead of Ruusan, loudly, which no Bastion crew will fire past; and a marine hesitating at an unlawful order is the crew's best lever — Reike would rather stand down than watch his boarding party's discipline crack. Remember also what the *Steadfast* cannot do: it has no hyperdrive and no navicomputer — it arrived clamped to a fleet tender, and until that tender returns it can hold the station, not chase the crew up the dead Run into the Grumani Reach. If they push through all of it, see Contingencies.

!!! tip "Pacing the standoff"
    Sixty minutes is generous and can still evaporate. Keep the *Steadfast*'s presence audible — proximity klaxons on the swarm, the marines' bootsteps on the ring — and whenever the table settles into comfortable negotiation, have one of the three corners raise the price. The scene should end because the certification and the inspection both resolved, not because everyone ran out of things to want.

**Certification — the "Speak to it" stage.** With Nerra aboard as guild observer, the relight's final stage is convincing her to certify: **Average Negotiation** or **Average Charm**, upgraded once for her practiced table manner — she *wants* to sign; the check is about whose terms. Honest testimony about the already-lit core adds a boost die. Without her — satisfying the old Republic handshake protocols cold — it is **Hard Knowledge (Education)**, and see Contingencies for what an uncertified claim is worth. Know the two numbers before you roll: the **witness fee** (1,200 scrip — what certified witnesses of record earn) and the **posted prize** (the famous number, 12,000 — the guild's price for finding and relighting Vesta-9, which technically nobody on this field can claim, because nobody on this field lit it). The swarm has not worked that out. Nerra has. Results: **Success** — the certificate of relight issues with the crew as witnesses of record, fee released; the prize stays on the guild's books. **Triumph** — she enters them as first-lighting claimants outright: the famous number, full honors, and priority at future guild tables — and a debt to the guild that has no line on the form. **Threat** — fine print: the certificate names the guild as charting authority for the whole corridor. **Despair** — the certification packet copies wider than intended; someone the crew did not invite now holds a clean copy (GM only: the mole — see [faction clocks](../tools/faction-clocks.md)).

## Scene 4 — The Record

Trigger it at the height of the standoff — ideally mid-certification, while all three factions are watching the core and each other. Nerra's certification form has a line on it. So does Reike's fleet log. Both say the same thing — *Lighting party / responsible hand* — and everyone in the chamber has just heard it read aloud, and everyone is looking at the witnesses of record.

> Nerra's stylus stops over the line. "Lighting party." She looks up, pleasant as weather. "You were first aboard. Who do I write?" Across the ring, Reike's adjutant has the same line open in the fleet log and is waiting with the patience of a man who is paid to wait. At the core, Vhail has stopped praying. She has not turned around.

**What the crew is holding** — take stock out loud, because the players may not realize it: Osk's claim that somebody has been flying the dead Run for years, and that the prizes follow them; the wake log — failsafes released from inside, valid guild handshake codes, cleaned up within the last few hours; a docking cradle still warm, and no ship anyone can find; and a station three light-hours from where the guild's charts put it, lit by someone who knew where it was. Nobody else in the chamber has all of it. Two of the three corners have already guessed at part of it.

**The choice belongs to the table, and it is not a roll:** what goes on the record? Frame it plainly. The certificate is the founding paper of the reopened Reach; it will be read for a century; and their names are on it as witnesses. There is no wrong answer. Every answer is the scene.

**Three wants, aimed now at the paper:**

- **@@npc:Commander Joss Reike@@ — the finding.** *"Unknown armed actor operating a dead lane with guild codes"* is the exact line his log needs to make this corridor "require naval protection." He will not lead them there; he does not have to. Say the stranger out loud and he thanks them, sincerely, and the Corridor Security Act gets its first justification in the Reach with the crew's names under it. Say nothing and he enters *"lighting party: undetermined; witnesses uncooperative,"* which is worse for them and better for him.
- **@@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@ — the clean line.** *"Unknown hand; lane clear"* is what she wants written, and she wants it enough to pay: strike her fine print, upgrade the witness charter, enter them as claimants for the famous number (see Certification). She goes quiet at the handshake codes and quieter at the name Osk — and the moment she learns an *original* page exists, with margins no copy carries, she offers real money on the spot: **3,000 scrip** for the page, rising toward the famous number itself for the book it came from, if the crew can produce it — or say where it lives. That is the sale Osk has refused for ten years, arriving at the crew's elbow hours after they watched the factor make it at her bedside. Her Deception 3 is for what the offer omits: where the book goes after. (GM only: to the patron's survey teams, within the month.)
- **@@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@ — the truth at the core.** She wants nothing written. She wants it *said*, at the core, honestly — and afterward she finds whoever spoke alone and asks exactly one question — *"Who opened this door — and did you say so?"* — and leaves without pressing. What she does after is the hinge: a crew that told the truth leans her report toward the half of her order that believes doors are being opened on purpose; a crew that certified it clean is entered in her psalter under a heading they cannot read.

**Decision matrix** — what the crew puts on the record, and what it seeds:

| If the crew… | Costs now | Seeds for Act 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Names the stranger on the record | Reike's finding — the Annexation gets its founding paperwork with their names on it — and the certification packet carries the codes to the guild's desks (GM: the patron now knows the crew *noticed*) | The *Steadfast*'s tender returns with orders; guild doors cool; and the Weeding takes an interest — a rival withdraws, a magistrate is helpful, luck the crew did not earn. *The Toll* and *The Mausoleum Yards* land with the Admiralty already holding a file. |
| Certifies it clean — "unknown hand, lane clear" | The fee, the guild's warmth, and a lie with their names on the Reach's founding paper | Every guild door opens, and the Conclave of Charts arrives with the crew as trusted witnesses — and the first person who reads the wake log carefully knows what they left out. *The Memory Market* lands personally: provenance is a thing they have already fudged. |
| Hands the evidence to Vhail | She must choose which half of her order she serves | The Vigil learns someone is opening doors on purpose; sealed places open to the crew, and both halves of the order come asking what else they saw. *The Sealed Enclave* gains a witness. |
| Sells Osk's page — or the book's address — to Nerra | Money now, at the price of a promise made hours ago; the port learns who sold it | The factor is back at the infirmary by morning with a probate lien; the guild — and the guild's patron — finally get what they waited ten years for, and the prizes stop stalling and start *accelerating*, in an order nobody at this table chose (Dial 3). Osk hears before she dies. |
| Keeps the page and says nothing | Perfect politeness from Nerra, an "undetermined" finding from Reike, a heading in Vhail's psalter | The crew holds the original's first page and a standing invitation to the rest — the return to Osk becomes Act 1's first milestone, and when the book comes home with them it is the campaign's engine, and the target on their hull. |

!!! note "The wake log is on the record"
    Whatever the crew said, the station's own logs carry the handshake codes and the cradle's temperature, and Nerra's certification packet carries the logs. Nobody in the standoff has read them carefully yet; do not spring it tonight. It is a slow fuse: whoever reads that packet later (an Admiralty analyst, a curious archivist, the reader Dial 3 put behind the guild's desks) will know that the witnesses of record stood next to the evidence and chose what to say about it.

!!! note "The witness rule, if the Force comes out"
    Nothing in this scene calls on the Force, and nothing should — the era's gift is an event, not a set piece, and it will find the crew on its own schedule. But if a PC *chooses* to use it openly here, apply the [witness rule](../../mechanics/force.md) — disposition shifts one step, awe or fear — and remember the "community" watching is *three factions' agents at once*: Reike's log acquires a second finding, Vhail stops being silent, Nerra's fine print grows a clause. Add the setback die to social checks with all of them until the crew addresses what was seen.

## The Out

> The corridor is open. On the boards it reads like a sentence finished after sixty years: VESTA-9 — LIT — RUN CLEAR TO ENARC. The Steadfast holds station like the corridor already belongs to it, the guild packet burns for home with signed paper aboard, and somewhere sunward a keeper is praying over a machine. Every one of them wrote down your names.

Before the bookkeeping, settle debts on-screen: Grell's cut (if signed) transmitted with bad grace and a note that his door is open; Marrow's wager paid at whichever cantina comes next; and the promise to Osk kept or broken, said aloud either way.

**Bookkeeping** — run the list in order:

**Next session's door** — open one before the table stands up, keyed to what they chose:

- They kept the page: the first leg is exactly where her margins say it is, and something in the margin — a warm buoy, a berth that should be empty — is waiting on their first Chart-it check.
- They kept faith: on the return leg the infirmary orderly flags them down — *"she wants to see you."* The rest of the book, and the whole story, is a scene next session — play it, don't summarize it.
- They named the stranger: a courteous letter from the Admiralty arrives at the next port, thanking them for their finding and asking them to carry something.

**The closing image**, said to the table in your own words: the crew came to Okrent's Drift as nobody. And someone nobody counted has a file too — and theirs is the only one that marks what the crew is carrying, and what they left off the paper.
