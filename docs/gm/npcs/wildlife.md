# The Feral Run — Wildlife of the Dark Country

Ninety years of darkness did not leave the Run empty — it left it **wild**. Dead stations grew mynock colonies; wreck-fields became reefs with resident predators; abandoned agri-worlds went over to feral herds; and things that like the dark had three generations to move into the infrastructure. Nobody broke the galaxy — the wilderness just moved back in when everyone stopped holding it up. The recovery is not only relighting beacons; it is evicting nature from the machinery, one cold surface at a time.

**The eviction rule.** Every relight displaces ninety years of dark-adapted tenants, and the displaced move *up the dark Run, ahead of the advance*. Spacers read the migrating swarms as omens. Sector folklore has spent a decade blaming ghost-traffic on mynock-waves and mynock-waves on ghosts — a confusion the campaign's hidden actors have enjoyed without ever arranging (the Scrub's cover, bought free; see [Keepers of the Flame](keepers.md)). When the crew relights a beacon, ask the beacon map's shadow question: *what left when the light came on, and where did it go?*

**Using wildlife — weather with teeth.** Star Wars beasts are rarely encounters; they are the environment demonstrating that space is old and alive. Three uses, in rising order of screen time: **the complication** (mynocks on the couplings mid-scene — never a whole scene), **the territory** (a dark station or sealed ruin that already belongs to something; entering wrongly is the mistake), and **the bigger fish** (the thing chasing the crew gets eaten — spend it once per act at most, and never on the villain the table has earned). Wildlife is also the comic engine on legs: a stampede through a certification ceremony beats three pages of dialogue.

**The rules kit.** Knowledge (Xenology) knows the beast; Survival reads and avoids it; Athletics and Coordination outclimb it. Anything **silhouette 6 or larger runs as a hazard, not an adversary** — terrain with intent: upgrade the Piloting checks, roll the water, never the teeth. And the era rule, absolute: **wildlife is never a villain.** No beast serves a faction, carries a grudge, or advances a clock. It is the recovery's honest obstacle — the one antagonist on the Run that is exactly what it appears to be.

**Beasts and the gifted.** The Force and fauna notice each other. A gifted PC can calm what a blaster only enrages (FaD's beast-influence rules apply as printed); Del Gormo's swamp is thick with creatures that behave strangely well around him; and a Dathomiri self-taught with a beast at her shoulder is a payoff worth building toward across an act. The vornskr (below) is this street running the other way — and note the GM irony: a beast that hunts the gifted is also a *detector* of them, and more than one faction would pay for a leashed one.

> **Sourcing note.** Per the pull-don't-build policy these entries are transcriptions of official printed FFG beast blocks — **transcribed from the library's index and NOT yet verified against print**: before a beast hits the table, check the cited book and use the printed wording; print outranks this file, then tell the archivist. The two silhouette-6+ entries are declared **hazards** (like the Scar's Echo): terrain with intent, no printed block owed.

- **Skills (group only):** Brawl, Coordination
- **Talents:** none
- **Abilities:** Silhouette 0; Flyer; **Vacuum Dweller** (does not breathe; lives on radiation and stellar energy); **Energy Parasite** (a mynock attached to a powered ship, vehicle, or device drains it: 1 system strain per round it remains attached; removing one is an Average Athletics or Coordination check, or a barrel roll)
- **Equipment:** Sucker-maw (Brawl; Damage 2; Critical 5; Range [Engaged]; Ensnare 1).

The **mynock-wave** — a displaced colony migrating ahead of the relighting — is an omen NPCs argue about in cantinas, and the mundane wrong answer to half the Run's ghost stories.

### The Reef
*Hazard — The Feral Run — a wreck-field become an ecosystem; declared homebrew, like the Scar's Echo*

> The war left a battlefield across the corridor. Ninety years made it a reef — salvage crust, mynock shoals, hull-barnacles — and a reef this old has a landlord.

Run the Reef as **terrain with intent**, never as a stat block: dense wreckage (setback dice and silhouette limits), mynock shoals (system strain weather), sensor ghosts — and the **anchor predator**, an exogorth-class thing grown fat on ninety years of drifting salvage, silhouette 7, patient as geology. It is a fact of the field, not a monster to kill: its presence upgrades Piloting checks in its third of the reef; its strike is narrated, telegraphed (the wreck-field *flexing*), and survivable by flying well. **The bigger fish:** the Reef is where a pursuing hull learns why the locals throttle down — the crew that baits a pursuer across the landlord's parlor ends a chase without firing a shot. The corridor the recovery needs runs straight through; the episode seed is *The Reef* ([Act 1](../../campaign/act-1.md)). **Placed:** the approach to Sanrafsix, the wreck-field of the Battle of Sanrafsix, worked by Dree's cutters under wreck-lamps — module 06, Scene 1 on the way in and Scene 7 on the way out, with whoever follows the crew off the Ring.

### The Aquilaris Deep
*Hazard — The Feral Run — the drowned resort's landlord; declared homebrew*

> The resort piers went under sixty years ago. Something moved into the shallows above them, and the fishing villages' first rule is: nothing on the water at dusk.

The leviathan of Aquilaris (hop 2) is the physical session's weather: run it as a **storm with intent** — wake-surges that swamp skiffs, a shadow under the glass-bottomed old promenades, pressure rules and Piloting (Planetary) upgrades on the water at dusk. Roll the water, never the teeth: it breaches on a Despair, it hunts by wake, and it can be *read* (Survival or Xenology — its circuit is regular, and the locals' calendar of safe hours is accurate, which is worth more than any harpoon). It is also the module of the Chadra-Fan's nightmare — Mikrés's drowning-fear is on his sheet, and this is the session that pays it honest screen time without cruelty: give him the engineering solution (the pumps, the pontoon, the winch) that saves the day from dry land.
