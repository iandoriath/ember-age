# The Road Back to the Hydian

*Where the campaign happens: real charts, dead lanes. Player-safe — this is the geography any spacer at Okrent's Drift could sketch on a napkin.*

## The country: the Duros Run

The crew starts in the dark interior of the Outer Rim, in the country spacers name for the lane that threads it: **the Duros Run**. You are *out on the Run*, and everything on it shares the name. Its deep half crosses the old **Grumani sector**, and a century ago that was Sith country: during the Republic Dark Age the sector shattered into warlord principalities — the Daimanate, the Bactranate, the Arkadianate, the Dyarchy — and the last war burned through it on the way to Ruusan. The Republic's recovery never reached this far back out. The principalities are dust; their worlds are still here, grown strange in the dark. Spacers still tell the old stories about them — the lord on Darkknell who believed he had created the universe and plated his statues in cortosis because someone kept cutting the heads off; the lord who locked every child in his realm in orphan-fortresses the size of moons; the ice-city queen who changed her people's jobs every season; the twins who ran a whole planet as one mind; the Hutt who tried to drown Aquilaris and then Darkknell with storm-ships. Nobody out here can tell you which parts are true. The Vigil can.

## The lane home: the Duros Space Run

The country's one real lane is the **Duros Space Run**, which once carried trade from the Mid Rim all the way to the **Hydian Way** at **Darkknell**. For most of the last century the Lamplighters' great work was elsewhere: after the Ruusan reformations it was the guild, not the Senate, that led the restoration of the **Corellian Run**, relighting that artery stretch by stretch, and everyone assumed the prizes would keep following it rimward toward Ryloth. Instead, ten years ago, the guild surprised the Core — it began posting first-lighting prizes down the old Duros Space Run, from the Corellian Run junction at **Churba / New Cov** into country the recovery had written off.

The prizes were claimed the way prizes are, one beacon and one corridor at a time: **Kalarba**, **Kyrska**, **Glom Tho**, and finally **Triffis**, whose prize the crew of the *Larksong* collected. The guild paid every one in full, and the reconnected worlds have slowly begun pricing themselves back into the galactic economy — though the Senate, whose Adjournment was worded *"until reconnection,"* has made no overture about restoring their seats.

Then the advance stopped. The next beacon up the lane — **Vesta-9**, serving the long hop beyond Bannistar — has beaten every survey sent after it: dark for sixty years and, apparently, not where the old charts say it should be. Its prize has hung unclaimed for years, and **Bannistar Station** — fuel gantries raised new on the momentum of the advance, with **Okrent's Drift** growing up around their footings — has slid from boomtown to recession, the frayed end of the living lane. Everything beyond is beacon-dark.

## The prize: Eriadu

At the far end of the chain, where the Run meets the living galaxy, sits **Eriadu** — an old, modest world best known for delicate shellwork jewelry, which happens to occupy the most valuable crossroads in the southern Rim: the Hydian Way crossed the Rimma Trade Route, plus the minor **Lipsec Run**. Every faction can read a chart. Whoever holds the relit corridor when Eriadu wakes up owns the front door of the next century — and there are already rumors of coreward money, old families from Corulag, taking an interest in the place.

## Why the lanes are dark: the two darkenings

The Run's beacons died **twice**, and the distinction matters to anyone reading old rutters:

1. **The first darkening (before Ruusan).** In the depths of the Dark Age, the old Republic — bankrupt and losing — *deliberately shut down* its hyperspace relays beyond the Core, to deny the Sith the network. Thousands of systems went dark by policy. Out here, most beacons never came back: the oldest rutter pages describe lanes no living pilot has run.
2. **The second darkening (the Withering).** After Ruusan the recovery relit beacons Core-outward — slowly, and only so far. Vesta-9's sixty dark years date from this lapse: it was revived in the early Reformation and abandoned again when the appropriations stopped. The Withering is not the war's darkness; it is the recovery giving up.

Both darkenings left more than silence: ninety years without traffic **rewilded the lane itself**. Dead stations wear mynock colonies like moss, war-wreck fields have grown into reefs with residents, and the deep worlds' abandoned farms belong to their livestock's feral great-grandchildren. Spacer wisdom on the living end of the Run is blunt about it — *check your intakes, light your holds, and never open a hatch that's been shut sixty years without standing to the side* — and when a beacon relights, the dark's tenants move up the lane ahead of the light: the wave of displaced mynocks is read as an omen from Bannistar to New Cov.

## The far end: the Dead Gate

Every spacer eventually asks it: Darkknell is alive, rich, and one hop from Jutrand — so why has nobody ever relit the Run from *that* end? Two answers, and every merchant house on Darkknell can recite both.

The first is arithmetic. From New Cov, every relit hop reconnects a living world that starts paying the day its beacon lights. From Darkknell, the first hops inward are Jutrand and the old principality cores — the deepest, strangest worlds on the Run — and a lane pays nothing in through-traffic until it is lit end to end. One direction is a ladder of small returns; the other is an all-or-nothing bet whose first rungs are haunted.

The second answer has a graveyard in it. The two darkenings were not symmetrical. The Mid Rim end's beacons were Republic-pattern relays, cleanly mothballed by policy — anyone with the codes can wake one, which is exactly what the guild's advance has been doing. But the deep end was principality country, and to the Daimanate and the Bactranate, beacon control was *war infrastructure*: they fortified their approaches from the Hydian, keyed their masts to their own courts, and when the principalities died, their network died with them — masts slagged in the last campaigns, cores under dead-man locks answering to dynasties that are dust, approach lanes still sown with the war's ordnance. From Darkknell inward you do not relight beacons; you *rebuild* them, under fire from a dead century. Early in the Reformation a chartered venture out of Darkknell — the **Jutrand Commission** — spent a fortune and several ships proving it, and no bond-writer on Darkknell has underwritten the dark Run since: no bond, no certificate, no claim. Darkknell spacers call the Run's mouth **the Dead Gate**, and the insurance rates are how it keeps the name.

And a quieter oddity, for the curious: now and then coreward money — Eriadu's new families, a Corulag consortium — talks about forcing the Gate anyway and meeting the guild's advance in the middle. Somehow the talk always dies: a syndicate loses its backers, a survey is cancelled, a prospectus goes missing. Nobody has ever explained why.

## The far road: toward the Valley

Every child knows the armies reached **Ruusan** seven times, so the road exists: up the **Hydian Way** the entire way coreward — Darkknell, Malastare (itself a raw colony barely older than the Withering), Denon — to **Brentaal**, where the Hydian crosses the **Perlemian Trade Route**; rimward down the Perlemian to **Lantillies**; then the **Randon Run** past **Kashyyyk** — and off the charts, into the Teraab stellar nursery, where the nebulae themselves have been quietly eating the last lanes to Ruusan ever since the war ended.

The crew is relighting beacons at one end of that road. The Valley is at the other.
