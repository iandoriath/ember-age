# Session Six — The Memory Market

*The GM-screen module for hop 5, Sanrafsix (module 06). Run it from this page, one scene at a time — every name is introduced and every number stated where you need it; marked names open stat blocks. **Branch-safe**: no particular outcome from Verdanth or Aplooine is assumed; the carry-forward table reads their Handoff flags by name. This is the session where the crew learn what their names are worth — because somebody is already selling them.*

## What you need to know (even if you read nothing else)

**Where we are.** Hop 5 of the dark Run: **Sanrafsix**, the great dead hub, where the Duros Space Run crosses the smugglers' Sanrafsix Corridor. A hundred and forty years ago it was the sector's cosmopolitan market, which is what killed it: the Candorian plague came up the lanes, the quarantine failed in its first year, and the orbital stations sealed the surface and let the city below them die. Nobody has been down since. The stations lived on — on the last war's economy, then on the dark's — and grew together into **the Ring**: forty thousand people on a chain of linked hulls tethered over a planet of the dead, running the one market the dark could not kill. **The Memory Market** sells the past. Rutters, relics, seals, testimony, the salvage of ninety years of worlds going quiet — and on this Ring a thing is worth exactly what its paper says. Provenance is the currency. The beacon the guild has posted **9,000 scrip** for is not on the Ring. It is in the city below, at the foot of the dead orbital elevator, in the old port authority tower — and the elevator's shaft is taped across by the Vigil, a lifetime ago, because of who is buried at the bottom of it.

**The twist of the night: the crew are already here.** On paper. The Verdanth temple's counterfeit relics reached the Market weeks ago, consigned by a fence who never met the seller (and who is now dead), and the Market's provenance house has *improved* them: every lot carries an attestation, in perfect institutional hand, that it was *witnessed in situ by the lighting crew of Vesta-9, Enarc, Aquilaris and Verdanth* — the crew's own names, which they did, after all, walk through that temple under. Three papers built it, and none of them is a forgery: the guild's certification record (the crew were the lighting party on Verdanth that week); the Inheritors' own file of the crew's temple visit, sold to the House by the remnant for food money the week after the culling; and the second edition of the biography **@@npc:Captain Ferro Skell@@** has been selling since Enarc. The forgery ring of **@@npc:Provenance-Broker Halla Wex@@** supplied the hand and the seals. The Inheritors wanted the crew's standing and could not forge it. The Market simply did. What the crew do about their names is the session.

**The other thing on this ring.** Two stalls on the lower concourse, side by side. One is the **remnant's** — the surviving Inheritor cell, a table of *accounts* run by **@@npc:Ostrel, the Third Clerk@@**, the clerk in guild grey who wrote nothing at Vesta-9 and was on this ring buying testimony the night the temple died; he sold the crew's file to survive, and he wants one thing from them now, badly. The other is a chandler's counter: **Sund & Daughter**, shoring and dig supply, a woman who gives exact change. *(GM: Odri Sund is Mendac in cover — [Keepers of the Flame](../npcs/keepers.md). The tell, once, unremarked. She never approaches the crew, never speaks to them first, and bids tonight only through a proxy who has never met her.)* And in the back room of the provenance house, among the lots being improved for the season's auction, there is a farm log in cipher from an estate sale on Veshet that nobody on the Ring has read. It is **Rade's rutter** — the route-log of every family that hid a gifted child across the Run — and the Scribes are turning it into a fake Jedi journal because that is what sells.

**Rules shorthand:** Average = 2 difficulty dice, Hard = 3, Daunting = 4. A **fragment** is the campaign's clue-card; log it in the [fragment tracker](../tools/fragment-tracker.md). **Provenance** on this Ring is a mechanic: a lot with the House's seal sells at its estimate; a lot without one sells as junk; a lot the *crew* attest to sells at ten times either. An attestation is worth **1,000 scrip a lot**, in either direction, and the House will take one as payment: *the House takes standing*. The relight runs by the [relight rule](../../mechanics/beacon-relighting.md): three stations over three rounds, a setback at every station each round past the second, any station not green at the end of round three fires the beacon wrong. The **witness rule** ([The Force](../../mechanics/force.md)) fires on the Ring like nowhere else: forty thousand people who buy and sell the past, and one of them ignites a lightsaber. **Ring law**, stated once so nobody argues with it later: any hull that touches the surface is impounded on return and never docks at the Ring again — the quarantine is the Ring's whole legitimacy — the tower's pads were slagged when the city was sealed, and the medics' decontamination exists only at the shaft head. The only way down is the elevator.

**Cut for time:** the Reef to one leg and the landlord's shadow; the Stalls to Ostrel's ask and one glance at the counter; the Bench to the client list and the log, no scriptorium fight; the Down to the crown alone, three rounds, the rite still the clock; the auction to Lots 41, 58 and 63.

## Scene 1 — The Reef: the arrival that teaches the Run's oldest lesson

**Clock:** 30 minutes. The corridor into Sanrafsix runs straight through the wreck-field of the Battle of Sanrafsix — a Jedi victory in the war's last years, and the Run's largest graveyard of hulls. Ninety years made it @@npc:The Reef@@: salvage crust, hull-barnacles, sensor ghosts, mynock shoals, and a landlord.

> Reversion. The board fills before the viewport does: hulls, thousands of them, cruiser-keels and fighter-shards fused into drifts the length of cities, and between the drifts a lane the locals have cut and buoyed with wreck-lamps. Something in the far third of the field moves the way a coastline does not. Then the Ring, beyond it, a bright chain across a dark planet, and a voice on the band: *"Inbound, throttle down. You're in the Reef. Fly it like it's watching you. It is."*

**Three legs.** Run it as terrain with intent, never as a monster: the crew's pilot flies, everyone else has a beat.

- **The drifts** — **Average Piloting (Space)**, two setback for the wreckage. *Success:* through clean. *Failure:* a scrape, 2 system strain. *Threat:* a mynock shoal takes the hull. *Despair:* a hull-drift shifts and the lane closes; find another (a fourth leg, Average again).
- **The shoals** — **Average Piloting (Space)** while the engineer handles the strain (**Average Mechanics** clears 2 system strain) and the lookout calls the ghosts (**Average Perception** removes a setback). *Failure:* a shoal attaches — strain weather per the @@npc:Mynock@@ card, 1 system strain a round each, until **Average Athletics or Coordination** on the hull, a barrel roll, or a wreck-lamp's glare clears it — and the landlord's third is upgraded once more. *Despair:* the landlord notices: the fast line closes, and the long way is the only way.
- **The landlord's third** — the buoyed lane skirts it; the fast line goes through it. **Fast line:** **Hard Piloting (Space)**, upgraded once, through the exogorth-class thing's parlor. *Success:* through, and the band goes quiet with respect. *Failure:* the strike — telegraphed (the field *flexes*) — 4 hull trauma and a Critical, never a swallow. *Threat:* 2 system strain as the field flexes. *Despair:* a second pass; roll again. **Long way:** **Average Piloting (Space)**, ten minutes more. *Failure:* a scrape, 2 system strain. Nobody has ever killed the landlord; nobody sane has tried.

**Dree's cutters** work the drifts under wreck-lamps — @@npc:Scavenger Clan Cutters@@ in a cutter-tug, hailing anyone who looks lost with a tow fee and a price for anything they are carrying. **@@npc:Junk Baron Okkam Dree@@** owns the yard on the Ring's outermost hull; his people throttle down at the third and will say why if asked: *the landlord eats what doesn't.*

**The bigger fish.** This scene runs a second time on the way out if anyone follows the crew off the Ring (Scene 7): a pursuer who does not know the field can be baited across the landlord's third, and the chase ends without a shot. Say nothing about that now. Let a player think of it.

## Scene 2 — The Ring: the crew find their names

**Clock:** 30 minutes. Docking is a fee and a question: *what are you carrying, and what's its paper?* The Ring is a chain of linked station-hulls a century and a half old, cramped, bright, loud, every concourse a market and every market a matter of seals. The port clerk's slate has the crew's names before they give them.

> The House of Seals takes the whole face of the third hull: a rostrum, galleries, and behind the rostrum a wall of lots under glass, each with a card. *Lot 41. Verdanth: temple furnishings, relic class. Attested in situ by the lighting crew of Vesta-9, Enarc, Aquilaris and Verdanth.* Your names, in a hand none of you have ever used, under a seal none of you have ever seen. There are nine more cards like it.

**What the cards are.** The sixteen fakes the Inheritors made and the Scrub left, across **ten lots (41–50)**; consigned by a fence who never met the seller; improved by the House's back room with an attestation built from three true papers (the guild's record, the remnant's file, Skell's second edition). The attestations are *true in every particular and a lie in the whole*: the crew were there; the crew attested to nothing. On the Ring that distinction is worth **60,000 scrip the set**, and **1,000 a lot** to strike or to confirm.

**Registrar Ammet Sull** is on the rostrum, and she does not pretend. Play her dry and quick; she has run this House for thirty years and has never once been surprised by anger. Her position, stated in full the first time it comes up, so the players can argue with it: *"The House seals provenance. Provenance is who was there. You were there. If you want it said that you saw nothing, that is a statement, and the House will seal it — for the fee."* The fee to seal a repudiation is 1,000 scrip a lot. The fee she will pay the crew to attest to *anything else* is the same, in the other direction. She is not bribing them. She is quoting.

**Ask for nothing yet.** Let the players decide what this is: theft, insult, opportunity, or the Run keeping score on them. All four are right. The three levers are all in later scenes — the bench that made the seals (Scene 4), the certificate the crew will sign tonight, which the House cannot improve (Scene 5), and the auction where the lots sell (Scene 6).

**Skell** is in the gallery. He raises a glass. If anyone goes over: *"You're worth more on paper than in person, Captain. I'm just the one who noticed first."* He has sold the second edition of their biography to the House; if *Backed Skell's lien*, his co-sworn founding claim of Vesta-9 is in his pocket, and he will sell it to anyone in the gallery who asks, including them, for 1,500. He is not looking for a fight on the Ring; the Ring does not allow them (Sull's house-guard, and the fact that everything here is somebody's collateral). Outside the Ring is another matter.

**Nerra** finds them within the hour, at the dock or the House, and says she is sorry — once, and nothing about files. If a talker wants the chain out of her: **Average Charm or Negotiation** against her Deception (her card: *for what the offer omits*). *Success:* the guild consigned the season and the House read the guild's own certification record; she names the other two papers if pressed. *Failure:* she is sorry, and she wants the beacon lit. She will not be sorry twice.

**Tull** is at the dock too, because the audit is, and says one thing about the Reef as the crew come off the pad, looking at the wreck-lamps: *"That is a grave. They light it to work it."* She does not say whose. The nine names she reads tonight came out of it.

## Scene 3 — The Stalls: the remnant asks, the counter counts

**Clock:** 30 minutes. The lower concourse: the cheap end, where the sellers who cannot afford the House's seal trade on their own word. Two stalls side by side, and the crew will be brought to one of them.

**Ostrel's stall.** @@npc:Ostrel, the Third Clerk@@ — thin, precise, in guild grey he has no right to, a table of slates and a sign that says *ACCOUNTS BOUGHT*. He was the third clerk at Vesta-9. He was on this Ring buying testimony the night the temple went silent, and he has not slept properly since. He sold the crew's file to the House the week after, for food money, and does not deny it if asked. He recognizes the crew before they recognize him.

**What he wants,** and he asks it plainly, because he has nothing left to be mysterious with: *"You saw him. You stood in the temple and he showed you what he is. The Ring says he was a fraud and we were theater. You have standing. Say what you saw."* He wants a sworn account — the crew's — that the Heir was what he claimed, filed with the House: it is the only provenance the remnant can survive on. He will pay what the remnant has (2,000 scrip in pre-war coin; the stipend he was holding for Anwei) and he will not pretend it is enough.

**Do not tell the players what they saw.** The Heir was a fraud with a real archive and true believers; the crew know exactly as much as they worked out on Verdanth (*Read the Emptied House right*, the rig, the earpiece) and no more. Whatever a player says here is a sworn account with standing, and it decides whether the Inheritors survive as a lineage or end as a footnote. If they swear he was real, the remnant lives and the Market believes it. If they swear he was a fraud, the remnant dies as a faith tonight and Ostrel thanks them for the truth, which is worse. ## Scene 4 — The Bench: where the seals come from, and what else is on it

**Clock:** 40 minutes. The House's back room is on the fourth hull, behind three doors and a bribe, and everyone on the lower concourse knows it: *Wex's bench*. Getting in is the heist; what is on the bench is the scene.

**Ways in:** the front (Sull will sell a viewing of the improvement process to anyone who pays 500 scrip and signs that they saw nothing); the trade door (**Average Deception** in a consignor's smock, or **Hard Skulduggery** on the lock; Dree's cutters know the door and will sell the knowledge); the ducts (**Average Athletics**, and a fyrnock nest — @@npc:Fyrnock@@, two of them — because the fourth hull's dark spaces are the Ring's Feral Run). **Failure** on any road is the house-guard: four of Sull's people with stun batons (use the @@npc:Vigil Wardens@@ minion print for the numbers, stun only) and a night in the House's cells, which Sull will *bill for*.

**The scriptorium.** Rows of secondhand droids in institutional hand — @@npc:Forgery-Ring Scribes@@, four to a row, three rows — transcribing provenance: the Verdanth attestations are on the second row, the crew's names being copied onto a new lot as they watch. **@@npc:Provenance-Broker Halla Wex@@** is at the bench in the corner and does not look up. She never talks to a mark. Her front-man does the talking; the Scribes do the work; a raised voice disperses a row. **Cornering her** is the talker's job: **Hard Coercion or Negotiation**, and the lever is the one thing the Ring punishes — the knowing-aloud. *Success:* she sells the client list for her neck. *Failure:* she sells it anyway, to Sull, first, and the crew read about it in the morning.

**Three things to find,** in whatever order the players look:

1. **The attestation's source.** Three papers under the second row's slates: the guild's Verdanth certification record, the Inheritors' file of the crew's temple visit (Ostrel's, with the House's receipt to him stapled on), and Skell's second edition. **Average Perception** on the bench, or simply reading the slates. This is the proof: the crew's names were never signed, only *copied*. Sull will accept it as grounds for a repudiation seal at no fee — she is fair by her lights — but only if the crew bring it to the rostrum before the hammer falls on Lot 41.
2. **The client list.** Wex's real product: who bought improved provenance, for two seasons, Run-wide. Two entries matter, and neither has a name on it. A **numbered House account** has bought every authenticated Sith piece on the Run for two seasons, paid in pre-war coin through a broker, and returned every one to the House within the week *as junk* — the returns, in Wex's hand, *collected at a chandler's counter*. A player must match that to the receipts in their own notes; the list files as Ladder rung 1 (pattern) and nothing higher. *(GM: the counter is an assay office; the line buys everything Sith to check whether anything real is left. Nothing is. A player who says "they are not collecting, they are checking" has the Scrub in a ledger — write that on the card when they say it, not before.)* The second entry is the **guild**: House seals sold on lots dated before their relight's certificate had cleared. Say nothing. Let a player find the dates. *(GM: Chartkeeper Denno Pike's product — the relighting order for sale, [Vigil & Inheritors](../npcs/vigil-inheritors.md).)*

## Scene 5 — The Down: the Fifth Relight, in the city of the dead

**Clock:** 40 minutes. The beacon is in the city, and the only way down is the dead orbital elevator: the first hull is its top station, the port authority tower its ground station, the ride two hours on a cable nobody has ridden in a lifetime, and the Vigil's grey tape across the car's doors. The Ring's law opens the shaft once a year, for the rite; **Tull** reads it, and she will open it for a relight — the beacon is the Republic's and the guild's, and the dead are not the beacon's — with the rite read at the top and witnesses at her side: the same witnesses whose names are on her audit. *"Come down with me. You are already on the slate."* The nine names are the Battle of Sanrafsix's — Jedi and Sith, laid together in the port authority at the war's end and carried down this shaft before the tape went across. It is why the grave-keepers hold a plague city's door at all.

**Suits, not for the plague.** The plague is a hundred and forty years dead; the Ring believes it is not, and the Ring's belief is a law: anyone who comes up the shaft comes up through a decontamination the Ring's medics run like a rite of their own. The suits are for the air, which the city has not made in a century. Say once that the plague is gone. Nobody on the Ring will believe the crew.

> The car stops at the tower's crown and the doors open on a city at noon under a dead sky. Nothing moves; nothing has. The concourses run away under the tower in every direction, and in every direction the dead are where they were — in doorways, on benches, in the long lines at the port gates, waiting for a ship that never came down. A century and a half of dust has made them the colour of the city. Above you the beacon mast rises off the crown, salt-white, its lattice intact: the only thing here that was built to wait.

**Why it is dark.** Not the plague: the mast drew the Ring's power feed through the whole of the war and went dark when the feed was cut in the Withering — the maintenance money gone, the switch thrown from above. The harbourmaster's last quarantine order is still in the carrier's buffer, a hundred and forty years old, because nothing since has been transmitted over it.

**Three stations, three rounds, nine names.** The relight clock is the rite: Tull reads three names a round at the crown, slowly. **Every round past the second, one setback at every station.** *All three stations green by the ninth name:* Tull holds the last name until the crew are in the car, reads it, and the shaft seals behind them — the image; let it land. *Any station red at the ninth:* the name is read, the mast fires wrong (below), and the crew are down here until morning, when Sull opens the shaft for a fee or Nerra for a clause.
