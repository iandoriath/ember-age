# Session Two — The Toll at Enarc

*The GM-screen module for hop 1. Every marked name opens its stat block; the Kajidic cast lives in [Lanes & Ledgers](../npcs/lanes-ledgers.md). This module is deliberately **branch-safe**: it does not assume any particular Vesta-9 outcome — the carry-forward table below collects whatever your table actually did, and nothing downstream breaks. Pin the branches after session one is played; run it the same night if the table is long.*

## Overview

**The session's jobs:** dramatize the Kajidic crack — @@npc:Sorulba the Anchor@@ versus @@npc:Jeeda the Younger@@, scarcity rents versus growth, with the crew's guild paper as the tiebreaker neither Hutt can print; turn the [beacon-relighting set piece](../../mechanics/beacon-relighting.md) political — at Enarc the beacon is *maintained, functional, and deliberately dark*, because somebody owns it; pay off Osk's claim with a second, independent source that disagrees on exactly one load-bearing fact (the Weighbridge ledger — the [fragment system](../../mechanics/lore-fragments.md) doing its job); and let the players notice, on their own, how *fast* the guild posted the Enarc prize — the order was already written.

**The situation in three sentences.** Enarc was never empty. It is the dark crossroads where the Duros Space Run met the **Enarc Run** (toward Naboo and the living Rimma) and the Hutt-ringed **Triellus** — and the Triellus went dark like everything else, but it never went *quiet*: Hutts hold rutters older than the Withering and lifespans long enough to remember every lane, and the Kajidics simply never stopped flying — darkness that stops other people is margin to them. Sorulba's kajidic has held the junction for forty years from **the Weighbridge** — a toll station built into a pre-Ruusan customs hulk — taxing the dark traffic, running the fuel monopoly, and keeping the beacon mast on its spine polished, oiled, and *off*: the Anchor knows an asset when he owns one, and the light is worth more unlit.

**Why this is the episode:** a relit Enarc doesn't just extend the Run one hop — it reconnects a three-way junction, which means charters, inspections, certified rates, and the end of the toll as a private arrangement. Sorulba wants it dark forever. Jeeda has run the numbers — more traffic at a lower legal rate beats scarcity rents over three Hutt lifetimes — and wants it lit *with the kajidic's name on the certificate as port authority*. And the third corner was already here before anyone arrived: **Enarc is the Naboo Admiralty's front door** — the Enarc Run is the fleet's only road out of ninety years of autarky, the Weighbridge is the only market on it, and Sorulba has been quietly fueling grey hulls for decades. The fleet wants the junction lit — settlers and trade genuinely welcome — *under naval law*: a customs house and protected-lane status written into the founding paper. All three corners need what only the crew carries: guild standing, witness signatures, and the pen that writes the corridor's founding paper. The diplomats and traders get their spotlight tonight; the smart play against kajidic power has always been the ledger, not the knife.

## Scene 1 — The Approach

**The jump.** Vesta-9 to Enarc is a dead leg nobody has certified: **Hard Astrogation** per the beacon rules; Osk's page or margins add a boost die. Mid-run, the lane crosses the Triellus's ghost: dark hulls on crossing vectors that do not answer hails — Hutt traffic that has never needed a beacon. No combat unless the crew forces it; the lesson is quieter — *the dark is not empty, and never was.* Osk told them. Now they can see it.

**Arrival.** The beacon mast reads cold on every band — and then the Weighbridge lights them up with targeting sensors older than anyone aboard, and a bored voice with a Huttese accent says the only sentence the junction has said to strangers in forty years:

> "Enarc anchorage. Declare cargo and account. Berth is two hundred scrip, five percent of declared value, fuel at posted rates. Or come about and go back where you came from. The scale does not care which."

The dues math is real and the guns are real; pay, talk your way to a waiver (**Hard Negotiation** — the scale-master has heard everything), or turn around. The dues-taker at the gate knows their names from Bannistar if the Kajidic wrote them in her book in session one — continuity the players will feel.

## Scene 2 — The Weighbridge

A customs hulk with a town grown through it: fuel bowsers like temple columns, a bazaar of black-lane goods, gun tugs on lazy patrol, and the beacon mast rising off the station's spine — polished, maintained, dark. Give the players a beat to notice that last part: sixty dark years, and *this* beacon has no rust on it.

**The three courts.** @@npc:Sorulba the Anchor@@ holds the throne room he has not left in forty years — stage him as terrain, never combat: he pays promptly, keeps his word to the letter, prices the letter, and can contradict any human myth about the old Republic with an eyewitness sneer. @@npc:Jeeda the Younger@@ keeps a small office near the scales, styled as the clan's auditor, which is technically true. @@npc:Kajidic Enforcers@@ are everywhere, commercial and unhurried — stun settings first, because a dead debtor pays nothing. And the third court is small and wears grey: **Purser-Lieutenant Ansa Vale**, the Naboo Admiralty's liaison at the scales (unstatted — borrow Lieutenant-at-Writs Enna Calloway's block from [Republic & Admiralty](../npcs/republic-admiralty.md) if dice are demanded), who has kept the fleet's fuel account here for years. After ninety years of autarky, Sorulba's scales are the only market on the Admiralty's road, and the family ledger carries the fleet's fuel contract as its oldest live entry — the Anchor's biggest customer, about to become his replacement. Vale is **treaty wing**: she wants the junction lit, tariffed low, and friendly. Her escort, **Lieutenant Dray Kollen**, is **Founding cadre**: he would rather the junction stay a chokepoint until the fleet can *hold* it — which puts him, ironically, on Sorulba's side of tonight's argument. Let the two of them disagree in front of the crew; that crack is the faction. All three courts will see the crew the same day, and the order the crew chooses is itself information everyone trades.

**What the grey court brings to the table.** Vale's briefcase holds three conversations; run whichever the fiction reaches.

- **The debt purchase.** If the crew carries Kajidic paper — the lease, a dues-book entry from Bannistar — Vale offers to buy it at face value: *"The fleet settles its friends' accounts."* It is how a navy-state actually recruits: not conscription, *refinancing*. The crew trades a Hutt creditor for a grey one with better manners and a service-is-citizenship philosophy about what debtors owe. Genuinely tempting; let the table argue.
- **The letter of marque.** At the Weighbridge cantina, publicly, Vale offers @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@ an escort commission and naval registry — the fleet wants its first poster privateer, and the *Larksong* is famous. The crew watches their likable rival being courted by the nascent empire. Warm Marrow asks the crew what they think, and means it; cold Marrow lets them wonder. Nerra, when she hears, is coldly furious: naval certification is the first competitor the guild's seal has ever had.
- **The banker question.** Quietly, once, to the crew alone: *"Your guild posted the Enarc prize before your Vesta-9 paper cleared. Who does your guild bank with?"* The Admiralty noticed the too-fast prize too, and the fleet's investigators are competent, patient, and genuinely useful. (GM only: the trap is structural — handing the patron-thread to the Founding cadre arms them with a perfect enemy. "A hidden hand funds the reopening" is rally material whether or not anyone ever says the word *Sith*. The players can hand the fascists their cause and believe they are doing detective work. They are. That is the problem.)

However it resolves — the crew intervenes, brokers, or just watches — it is the Annexation in miniature, performed a season early, one freighter at a time.

## Scene 3 — The Ledger

The Weighbridge has logged the dark years — every hull it stopped, taxed, and fueled for four decades. For anyone chasing *Who lit Vesta-9?*, it is the only independent witness on the Run, and it can be bought, stolen, or traded for — Sorulba sells anything except cheaply, and what he wants most is the crew's honest account of the beacon that lit itself.

Two standing "do not hail" orders live in Sorulba's hand, written years apart, and keeping them distinct is the whole game. **The first is old, priced, and mundane:** the fleet's transits — grey hulls down from the Enarc Run, fuel bought, passage charged at a contract rate, every crossing *logged*. The Weighbridge has never hailed the Naboo Admiralty for the same reason a bank does not frisk its depositors. (A careful reader can even date NAS *Steadfast*'s passage toward Vesta-9 in the transit pages — which superficially feeds the port's comfortable wrong answer, and actually refutes it: covert lighters do not pay tolls on the way to the crime.) **The second order is newer, unpriced, and unexplained.** The page that matters says this: **six years ago, Run-lane entries stop.** Not because traffic stopped — because the Weighbridge stopped *hailing* certain hulls, by standing order, after a boarding crew failed to report back and was never found. No contract. No rate. No log. There are ships you do not stop, and they are not the fleet's.

## The Out

**The suspicious speed, said out loud.** However tonight ended, the guild's Enarc prize paperwork was *dated before Vesta-9's certificate cleared* — Nerra's packet, or the guild post, shows it to anyone who reads closely. The advance did not resume; it was *waiting*. That is a player-visible fact about the relighting order, free, for whoever thinks to check a date.

**Doors onward:**

- **Aquilaris, hop 2** — the drowned resort world, and the first true deep-Run stop: dead pleasure piers under warm seas. The next prize posts immediately (of course it does).
- **The clan argument travels.** Whichever Hutt lost tonight writes letters; expect the winner's rivals — or the loser's collectors — at the crew's next port.
- **If the Admiralty finding exists:** the tender arrives at Enarc on schedule, and the junction becomes the Annexation's first test case with the crew's names in its founding file.
- **The wager stands.** Marrow pays or collects at the Weighbridge cantina, loudly, and the season's round-buying begins — the port watches who laughs.
- **If the gifted dream recurs** (optional, Dial 2): the canyon again, closer — and this time somebody in it is facing the dreamer.

**XP:** 15–20; **+5** for a resolution that used ledgers, paper, or testimony where blasters would have been easier.

**The closing image**, in your own words: the junction lit or dark by the crew's own hand — and on the Weighbridge's oldest wall, under forty years of tally marks, the scale-master chalks tonight's entry the way the toll has always recorded history: a hull, a weight, a price. Whatever the galaxy decides Enarc was worth, the Kajidics already wrote it down.
