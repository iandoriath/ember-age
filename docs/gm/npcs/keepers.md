# Keepers of the Flame — GM-Locked Force Users

Ninety years after Ruusan, the trained Force survives in exactly two places, and neither advertises: a hidden Jedi lineage down to one tired Master and one farmhand pupil, and the Banite line, two or three successions deep and patiently setting the table. This file stats both — plus the dark-side vergence the Inheritors are digging into — because Acts 2 and 3 turn on who reaches the new generation first. These five entries are the campaign's locked answers made playable.

**GM ONLY — SPOILER-CRITICAL. Nothing in this file is player-facing; it implements Part V (locked) of the planning document. Do not read aloud, do not summarize to players, do not leave open at the table.**

> **Sourcing note (updated after re-pull).** Per the pull-don't-build policy, all four NPCs below are now **verbatim pulls of official printed FFG adversary blocks**. Attestation: a book-and-page-cited transcription dataset (the MIT-licensed stoogoff/sw-adversaries index, carrying full characteristics, derived stats, skills, talents, and weapon lines per entry) was cross-checked so that every citation resolves to an official FFG book — **"Forsaken Jedi"** (Edge of the Empire Core Rulebook, p. 412), **"Jedi Padawan"** (Collapse of the Republic, p. 137), **"Fallen Master"** (Force and Destiny Core Rulebook, p. 412; reprinted in Allies and Adversaries, p. 81), and **"Emperor's Hand"** (Edge of the Empire Core Rulebook, p. 402). Every printed number is transcribed exactly; only names, flavor, and equipment cosmetics are re-skinned for the era, and every block's Adjustments line is "none." The first draft's original builds and their unverified-against-print flags are hereby retired. The Scar's Echo remains the file's one declared homebrew entry (a hazard, not an adversary — no printed block exists or is owed). At the table, print outranks this file: if one of the cited books is open in front of you and disagrees on a number, the book wins — then tell the archivist.

## The Hidden Lineage

The second fork of Dial 1, resolved: one Jedi lineage survives to 90 AR. It consists, in its entirety, of the two people below. Neither the Vigil, the Republic, nor the Inheritors know they exist. A living mentor is the rarest Wellspring in the era (Part VII) — which makes Yenna Sar, mechanically and politically, the most valuable being in the campaign.

**Using her:** Yenna is third student of Master Tolum Rade, who studied under a survivor of Ruusan — a complete transmission of the lineage, which justifies her Force Rating 3 in an era of untrained Awakened (era rule, Part VII). She avoids all open Force use (witness rule); if forced to draw the saber a third time, she fights to disengage and vanish, never to win. As a living-mentor Wellspring she can support Force Rating increases and new power trees — and as the only being alive with plausible standing to be called *Master*, she is one-third of a reconvened Council, which makes her a walking constitutional crisis for Act 3.

## The Unbroken Line

Dial 1, statted. Bane, then Zannah, then Cognus — and the record goes silent, because the line got better at silence. Cognus's recorded apprentice, Darth Millennial, rejected the Rule of Two and walked out on *her* to found the Dark Force cult on Dromund Kaas; Legends names no loyal heir after him, so the Master who actually inherited collides with nothing published. Here that Master is **Darth Haerus** — bent from *haereo*, to hold fast, to remain; say it aloud and it is also *heir* — and the apprentice, whose name nobody outside the line has ever heard spoken, is **Darth Tacet** — *is silent*, the instruction a score gives an instrument for the whole of a movement. Third succession from Bane: young as Sith dynasties go, consolidating, and in no hurry, which is precisely why patience costs them nothing. Their goals and machinations, and the one set piece that shows their work, follow the two stat blocks.

It is also the reason no encounter with them this campaign is winnable or intended. ### What the line wants, and how it works

**The strategy in one line: keep the dark side's inheritance in exactly one pair of hands until the galaxy is worth inheriting.** Seventy years after Bane, the line has no empire, no treasury and no army, and wants none of them yet. What it has is the only unbroken transmission of the dark side in the galaxy, and the Jedi's conviction that the Sith are extinct. Everything Haerus does protects those two facts. The Republic will be corrupted eventually; this century's work is making sure there is a Republic to corrupt and no rival heir alive when the time comes. Reunification is their harvest, but they do not pay for it — they weed it.

**What Haerus wants.**

- **Exactly one heir.** The Rule of Two is not a doctrine to Haerus; it is a census with a single line on it. Any third claim on the Sith inheritance — grave-robbers in costume (the Inheritors), a prophet in the Sith Worlds (Millennial), a reconvened Jedi Order strong enough to go looking — is not an enemy to be fought but a weed to be pulled before it seeds.
- **To own the genuine.** Haerus is a curator before anything else: every real holocron, teaching and relic the wars scattered across the Rim is to be found, verified, and removed from the galaxy before the Jedi or the grave-robbers reach it. Counterfeits are left exactly where they lie — nothing in the Plan is served by a galaxy that stops believing in fakes, and nothing is more useful than a rival who has trained on one.
- **To remain.** The heir who stayed when Millennial walked wants the record to show that patience, not prophecy, carried the line. Never a name in a dispatch; never a protagonist. The day anyone can point at Haerus is the day the century was wasted.

**What Tacet wants** is simpler and younger: to be good enough. Anonymity is the grade; the Inheritors are the exam; and underneath the doctrine is an apprentice who has begun to suspect that patience is the Master's weapon against *them* as much as against the galaxy — which is the Rule of Two working as designed, and not this campaign's problem.

**The machinations** (off-screen and ongoing — draw on them whenever you need the pattern to show from very far back):

1. **The Weeding.** Whenever reunification stalls — a beacon certification held up, a corridor claim contested, a magistrate who would rather the lane stayed dark — the obstacle removes itself. A rival withdraws without explanation; a hostile official retires young and quiet; a rutter that would have started a war goes missing from a dead man's cabin. Tacet's hands, Haerus's patience, never a trace. The players should feel the recovery is lucky. It is not.
2. **The Inventory.** The line keeps the only true catalogue of what the New Sith Wars scattered: which holocrons survived Ruusan, which teachings the principalities hoarded, which relics are real. Acquisition is by theft, inheritance and the occasional dead collector — never by purchase, which leaves a ledger. The provenance bazaars (Sanrafsix's Memory Market first among them) are watched, not bought from: the day a genuine piece surfaces, its seller has a short future. Improv hook: a scholar of impeccable manners who asks to *see* a relic and is visibly, quietly relieved to find it is fake.
3. **The Wasp Trap.** Millennial's heresy on Dromund Kaas is alive and tolerated. It is a sump for the loud: the ambitious, the visionary, the seer who would otherwise draw attention inside Republic space. Haerus's people never touch it — occasionally they *send* someone to it — and it will be culled the day it reaches Republic space or the day a Darth title out of the Sith Worlds is taken seriously. Dromund Kaas is on the far side of the Rim from the Reach; if the heresy ever touches this campaign, it arrives as a recruiter, a rumor, or a prophecy that is suspiciously accurate about the Valley.
4. **The Census.** The Awakening is the line's future. A generation of untrained Force-sensitives with no Order to claim them is either the greatest recruiting pool the Sith have ever had or the seed of the Jedi's return, and Haerus means to decide which. The line keeps its own watch on the gifted — which families, which worlds, which doctrines are reaching them first — and the Inheritors' harvested Vesta-9 file (Dial 3) completes it. The line now knows the crew, and which of them the beacon answered. **Their desire is not to kill them.** Gardeners are not pruned, and Tacet's successor has to come from somewhere: the Rule of Two recruits at generational distance, and if you want a sequel hook, the apprentice's apprentice may already be at your table.
5. **The Graduation.** The culling of the Inheritors (Dial 1) is Tacet's exam — see the set piece below. It is the Weeding, the Inventory and the Census done in one night.
6. **The Absence.** Never on camera. Haerus manifests as pattern and Tacet as aftermath; neither is ever provable, and no encounter with either is winnable or intended. If the players ever build a true galactic power, the line's attention is the sequel's opening scene.

### Set piece — The Emptied House
*Aftermath encounter — the culling, seen from inside — run it after the Unveiling, or earlier as the culling's first quiet strike*

> One night, one base, nobody alive, and the relic hall still full.

Use an Inheritor base the crew has already visited with courtesy — first contact is always helpful (Dial 3) — so that coming back is the point: the dig camp at the Scar, a Sanrafsix warehouse, a cell on the Veshet spur. They find it taken apart. Everyone is dead and it happened in one night. There are no blaster scorings and no cauterized wounds. The bodies that must never be identified are not there at all (the disruptor); the rest died of falls, of fire, and of each other (Influence). The archive rooms are bare — not burned, *emptied*: dust outlines where shelves stood. And the relic hall is untouched. Every "Sith artifact" the Inheritors ever displayed is still on its plinth.

**The truth.** Tacet took every genuine item and left every counterfeit, because Haerus's eye is the one thing in the galaxy that can tell them apart — and because a room full of fakes tells the galaxy this was robbery by people who did not know what they were looking at. Nobody sane concludes "Sith." The fakes are also a message, for the one reader in ten thousand who can tell.

**Reading the room** (optional clue ladder; RAW difficulties; a failed check says nothing, a despair says something false):

| Check | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **Perception, Average** | No blaster or saber marks; too few bodies for a base this size; the missing dead are exactly the senior faces the crew remembers. |
| **Medicine, Hard** | The survivors of the first wave killed each other and then themselves, in an order that makes no sense — unless they were *made* to (Influence). Force-sensitive characters may substitute a Sense check: the fear in the walls has a shape. |
| **Knowledge (Lore), Hard** *(Average if the character has handled a real Sith relic or holds a Sith-tagged fragment)* | The relics on the plinths are counterfeits — good ones, the Inheritors' own costume pieces. |
| **Knowledge (Lore), Daunting** *(Formidable without a Sith-tagged fragment)* | *Only* fakes remain. Whoever did this could tell — which means a living tradition, not grave-robbers. Success awards a **Sith-tagged lore fragment** ("Someone in the galaxy can still tell true from false") and the GM a problem: the players now know the thing nobody sane concludes. |
| **Streetwise, Hard** | The fakes will surface on the Memory Market within weeks, consigned by a fence who never met the seller. The trail ends at a dead fence. |
| **Vigilance (or Sense), Average, in the archive** | The house is *quiet*. Whatever was here that the Force could feel left with the thieves — the only honest inventory of what was taken. |

**Aftermath.** Fire-sale of the fakes; leaderless cells; half-trained adepts scattering with dangerous knowledge; if this was a senior base, the Vesta-9 file is gone with everything else. The galaxy blames the Vigil, the Admiralty, or an Inheritor purge, and each misattribution lights a different Act 3 fire (Part V). If the crew's gifted stands in the relic hall and feels nothing at all, let that land: the real thing was here, and it is with someone now.

## The Scar's Echo

### The Scar's Echo
*Encounter hazard — Episode 6, "The Scar" — NOT an NPC*

> A New Sith Wars battlefield where the dark side pooled and never drained: kilometers of fused glass under gray ashfall, an Inheritor dig site at its heart, and something underneath that has learned the shape of a listener.

> **DELIBERATELY HOMEBREW SECTION — the only one in this file.** The Scar's Echo is a dark-side vergence run as terrain, not a creature: it has no characteristics, no wounds, and cannot be fought. Its mechanics are assembled from two RAW subsystems — the fear-check rules (Discipline checks against a set difficulty, F&D core fear guidance) and Morality/Conflict as written — so nothing below requires a book lookup; the assembly, and the two declared overrides on the Adjustments line at the end of this entry, are the homebrew. The "Encounter hazard" typeline above is a sanctioned exception to the template's Nemesis/Rival/Minion types: this is the file's one declared non-NPC entry.

**Zones and fear checks.** Call for a fear check (Discipline) when a character first enters each zone, and again if the Echo does something overt (a voice using a dead parent's cadence, a glass field flexing like breath). Per the RAW consequence pattern: on a failure the character suffers 2 strain and adds a setback die to all checks for the remainder of the encounter; three or more threat add 2 further strain; a despair means panic — the character is staggered until the end of their next turn or must immediately move away from the heart. A character who succeeds cannot be forced to re-check in the same zone the same day.

| Zone | Fear check | Ambient effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Ashfall Rim** (the crash-ring and Inheritor base camp) | Hard Discipline | Ash-blind: add a setback die to Perception and Vigilance checks; ranged attacks beyond short range upgrade their difficulty once. |
| **The Glass Fields** (fused, uneven, wrong underfoot) | Hard Discipline; Force-sensitive characters upgrade the difficulty once | Difficult terrain throughout. Each hour spent here: Average Discipline check or suffer 1 strain (the whisper accumulates; this strain cannot be recovered until the character leaves the Scar). |
| **The Choir** (the heart; the dig itself) | Daunting Discipline; Force-sensitive characters upgrade the difficulty once | Both outer effects apply. All checks to recover strain here automatically fail. Sleeping in the Choir: 1 Conflict, and a true-but-poisoned dream. |

**Conflict pressure (RAW Conflict, tempted).** The Echo's offer is generosity. While anywhere within the Scar, a Force-sensitive character may use dark side results on Force power checks *without flipping a Destiny Point* — the strain cost per dark point still applies as written, and each dark point used this way awards Conflict as RAW **plus 1 additional Conflict** (the vergence's finder's fee). Additionally, award 1 Conflict for: sleeping in the Choir; deliberately opening oneself to the Echo's visions; or taking payment from the dig knowing what is being dug. Land the aftermath hard (Part VII): there are no counselors in this era, and the first person to explain the nightmares wins — make sure the players notice who is offering.

**The visions.** Seek, Sense, Foresee, and Farsight checks made within the Scar add both a boost die *and* a setback die, and what they return is true but curated — the Echo shows real fragments (it qualifies as a **Sith-tagged lore fragment source** for the Reconstruction subsystem) selected to make the viewer feel indispensable. A despair on any such check puts a corrupted conclusion in play that the character believes (mirror of the Reconstruction rules).

**The bargain (Wellspring).** The Scar is a functional vergence, and therefore a legal Wellspring (Part VII): a Force-sensitive character *can* raise Force Rating or open a new power tree here. Any advance bought with the Scar as its Wellspring carries a rider: the first time that power or rating is used each session, the character gains 1 Conflict. This is the campaign's first real temptation, and it should work — the offer is genuine, the training is real, and the price is on layaway.

**Running it.** The Echo has no agenda a stat block could hold; it wants *listeners*, and it prefers the young. The Inheritor dig crew (coin, answers, courtesy — first contact is always helpful, per Dial 3) treats the whisper as an occupational perk and is quietly further gone than they know. Never bring the crew's gifted here without intending the campaign to pivot on it: they dream of the Valley, which is loud, but the Scar is quiet and hungry, and it would very much like to be their second teacher.

**Chassis:** original build (encounter hazard — no adversary stat block exists or is owed; assembled from the F&D fear-check guidance and RAW Morality/Conflict). **Adjustments:** the +1 Conflict finder's-fee and the no-Destiny-flip dark-pip rule are the only overrides of RAW, both declared above.
