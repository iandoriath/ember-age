# GM Truths (Locked)

!!! danger "Players never see this page"
    Three dials, now set.

## Dial 1 — The line is unbroken, and very far away

A Sith Master and apprentice continue Bane's work somewhere in the galaxy — patient, hidden, and interested in ruins only for what they remember. The Rule of Two does not want a withered galaxy; it wants one worth taking — and it wants every true thing about the Sith out of that galaxy and into its own hands. The Reach was Sith country in the wars, and the Withering sealed its vaults; reunification is their harvest, which makes the party their unwitting gardeners: every relit beacon opens a door she means to be first through.

They will not surface until a true galactic power exists — likely not within this campaign's span at all — so **run them as an absence**: unexplained good fortune whenever reunification stalls, rivals who quietly remove themselves, a pattern visible only from very far back. If the players ever found that power, the Sith's attention is the sequel.

At 90 AR the line is only two or perhaps three successions deep — young, consolidating, and nowhere near ready, which is exactly why patience costs them nothing.

*(Stat blocks, statted for completeness only: [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md).)*

### What the line wants, and how it works

**The strategy in one line: every true thing about the Sith, out of the galaxy and into her hands.** Veritis is not building an empire, a treasury or an army, and wants none of them yet. Seventy years after Bane, the line's whole position rests on two facts — it holds the only unbroken transmission of the dark side, and the Jedi believe the Sith extinct — and both are facts about *knowledge*. So that is what she works on. Every holocron, teaching, tomb-record and court archive the New Sith Wars scattered is to be found, verified, and removed: the genuine into the line's keeping, the galaxy left with nothing but its fakes. The Republic will be corrupted eventually; this century's work is making sure that when anyone goes looking for what the Sith were, they find only what Veritis has left for them.

**Why she is looking at the Reach.** The Grumani sector was Sith country in the wars — the Daimanate, the Bactranate, the Arkadianate, the Dyarchy — and the Withering sealed it: courts, tombs, archives, the private libraries of Sith Lords, a century of beacon-dark silence over all of it. Nobody has read any of it since. And somewhere in it is the one thing she came for: **the holocron of Naga Sadow** — the Dark Lord of the Great Hyperspace War's own teaching, five thousand years old, looted during the New Sith Wars into a principality's collection and lost with the principality. The line's catalogue says it survived the Withering. It does not say which vault. (Legends records no holocron of Sadow's in this sector, so it collides with nothing — and a Sadow holocron is exactly the kind of thing a Sith principate would have killed a world to own.) So the Reach had to reopen, and the line could not be seen to open it: Mendac lit Vesta-9 (Dial 3), and a guild has been opening the rest of the doors for her since, one beacon at a time, in her order. Reunification is the line's harvest and the party its unwitting gardeners: they are not relighting a trade lane, they are unlocking a library Veritis intends to empty — so that only lies remain.

**The Patronage.** Through a coreward front the Lamplighters believe to be a private antiquarian consortium, the line funds the guild. The money comes with two conditions the Grandmaster of Charts took to be a collector's vanity: the relighting proceeds in the patron's sequence, and the patron's survey teams get first look at anything a relight uncovers. The sequence is the Sith search pattern — principality by principality, vault by vault — and the survey teams are the Scrub. Ophira Zel does not know who her patron is. She has begun to suspect that the registry leaks; she has not begun to suspect the money.

**What Veritis wants.**

- **The truth, all of it.** Verification is the point: she can tell a genuine relic from the Inheritors' best forgery at a glance, and the line's inventory of what survived Ruusan is the only honest one in the galaxy.
- **The galaxy wrong, not ignorant.** Absence invites questions; error answers them. Counterfeits are left exactly where they lie, fake lineages are allowed to flourish, and a rival who has trained on a forgery is worth more to her alive than dead — until he starts to find real things.
- **Exactly one heir.** Any third claim on the Sith inheritance — grave-robbers in costume (the Inheritors), a prophet in the Sith Worlds (Millennial), a Temple that starts looking outward — is not an enemy to be fought but a reader to be denied the book.
- **To be unnecessary.** Never a name in a dispatch; never a protagonist. The day anyone can point at Veritis is the day the century was wasted.

**What Mendac wants** is simpler and younger: to be good enough. She was a workhouse child who walked into a cave on a dare and walked out over a Sith Master's body; everything since has been the Mother teaching her what that was worth. Anonymity is the grade; the Inheritors are the exam; and underneath the doctrine is an apprentice who has begun to suspect that patience is the Master's weapon against *her* as much as against the galaxy — which is the Rule of Two working as designed, and not this campaign's problem. She has friends she left on a workhouse world seven years ago. She has never once checked on them. She thinks about it more than the Mother knows.

**The machinations** (off-screen and ongoing — draw on them whenever you need the pattern to show from very far back):

1. **The Weeding.** Whenever the relighting stalls — a certification held up, a corridor claim contested, a magistrate who would rather the lane stayed dark, a guild officer who questions the patron's order — the obstacle removes itself. A rival withdraws without explanation; a hostile official retires young and quiet; a rutter that would have started a war goes missing from a dead man's cabin. Mendac's hands, Veritis's patience, never a trace. The players should feel the recovery is lucky. It is not.
2. **The Scrub.** The line's real work in the Reach, and the reason the order matters. Behind every relit beacon, the patron's "survey team" — Mendac, or hands Mendac has hired and will not leave alive — goes in first: the principality vaults opened and read, the genuine removed, the forgeries and the garbled copies left on the shelves for the next scholar to find. Each vault that does not hold the holocron narrows the search to the next beacon on the guild's list. Run it as the crew arriving somewhere already visited — a tomb with fresh footprints and nothing in it, a dead principality's archive whose only surviving volumes are the ones a Daunting Knowledge (Lore) check reveals to be worthless. The Memory Market at Sanrafsix is watched, not bought from: the day a genuine piece surfaces, its seller has a short future. Improv hook: a scholar of impeccable manners who asks to *see* a relic and is visibly, quietly relieved to find it is fake.
3. **The Wasp Trap.** Millennial's heresy on Dromund Kaas is alive and tolerated. It is a sump for the loud: the ambitious, the visionary, the seer who would otherwise draw attention inside Republic space. Veritis's people never touch it — occasionally they *send* someone to it — and it will be culled the day it reaches Republic space or the day a Darth title out of the Sith Worlds is taken seriously. Dromund Kaas is on the far side of the Rim from the Reach; if the heresy ever touches this campaign, it arrives as a recruiter, a rumor, or a prophecy that is suspiciously accurate about the Valley.
4. **The Census.** The Awakening is the line's future. A generation of untrained Force-sensitives with no Order to claim them is either the greatest recruiting pool the Sith have ever had or the seed of the Jedi's return, and Veritis means to decide which. The line keeps its own watch on the gifted, and the Inheritors' harvested Vesta-9 file (Dial 3 — they watched the relight Mendac performed) completes it. The line now knows the crew, and which of them the beacon answered. **Their desire is not to kill them.** Gardeners are not pruned, and Mendac's successor has to come from somewhere: the Rule of Two recruits at generational distance, and if you want a sequel hook, the apprentice's apprentice may already be at your table.
5. **The Graduation.** The culling of the Inheritors (Dial 1) is Mendac's exam — see the set piece below. It is the Weeding, the Scrub and the Census done in one night.
6. **The Absence.** Never on camera. Veritis manifests as pattern and Mendac as aftermath; neither is ever provable, and no encounter with either is winnable or intended. If the players ever build a true galactic power, the line's attention is the sequel's opening scene.

### The fork is closed: the Inheritors are heretics

No connection to the true line, and they do not know it — real techniques scraped from artifacts, counterfeit lineage, no living tradition. **The Sith do not yet know they exist.**

The moment that changes — the Unveiling, a public claim to a Darth title, anything that makes them look less like grave-robbers and more like a rival tradition — **the culling comes**, and it is surgical, anonymous, and total: the senior circle dead across a dozen worlds in one night, the archives vanished rather than burned, and nobody sane concluding "Sith." The galaxy blames the Vigil, or the Admiralty, or an internal purge, and every misattribution lights a different Act 3 fire.

Two aftershocks matter:

1. The culling **shatters the Inheritors instead of ending them**: leaderless cells, fire-sale artifacts, half-trained adepts scattering with dangerous knowledge.
2. The harvested archives include **the Vesta-9 file** — whoever wrecked the Inheritors now knows about the crew, and which of them the beacon answered, and has never once been seen.

### Set piece — The Emptied House
*Aftermath encounter — the culling, seen from inside — run it after the Unveiling, or earlier as the culling's first quiet strike*

> One night, one base, nobody alive, and the relic hall still full.

Use an Inheritor base the crew has already visited with courtesy — first contact is always helpful (Dial 3) — so that coming back is the point: the dig camp at the Scar, a Sanrafsix warehouse, a cell on the Veshet spur. They find it taken apart. Everyone is dead and it happened in one night. There are no blaster scorings and no cauterized wounds. The bodies that must never be identified are not there at all (the disruptor); the rest died of falls, of fire, and of each other (Influence). The archive rooms are bare — not burned, *emptied*: dust outlines where shelves stood. And the relic hall is untouched. Every "Sith artifact" the Inheritors ever displayed is still on its plinth.

**The truth.** Mendac took every genuine item and left every counterfeit, because Veritis's eye is the one thing in the galaxy that can tell them apart — and because a room full of fakes tells the galaxy this was robbery by people who did not know what they were looking at. That is the Scrub's whole doctrine in one room: the galaxy is not to be left ignorant of the Sith, it is to be left *wrong*. Nobody sane concludes "Sith." The fakes are also a message, for the one reader in ten thousand who can tell.

**Reading the room** (optional clue ladder; RAW difficulties; a failed check says nothing, a despair says something false):

| Check | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **Perception, Average** | No blaster or saber marks; too few bodies for a base this size; the missing dead are exactly the senior faces the crew remembers. |
| **Medicine, Hard** | The survivors of the first wave killed each other and then themselves, in an order that makes no sense — unless they were *made* to (Influence). Force-sensitive characters may substitute a Sense check: the fear in the walls has a shape. |
| **Knowledge (Lore), Hard** *(Average if the character has handled a real Sith relic or holds a Sith-tagged fragment)* | The relics on the plinths are counterfeits — good ones, the Inheritors' own costume pieces. |
| **Knowledge (Lore), Daunting** *(Formidable without a Sith-tagged fragment)* | *Only* fakes remain. Whoever did this could tell — which means a living tradition, not grave-robbers. Success awards a **Sith-tagged lore fragment** ("Someone in the galaxy can still tell true from false") and the GM a problem: the players now know the thing nobody sane concludes. |
| **Streetwise, Hard** | The fakes will surface on the Memory Market within weeks, consigned by a fence who never met the seller. The trail ends at a dead fence. |
| **Vigilance (or Sense), Average, in the archive** | The house is *quiet*. Whatever was here that the Force could feel left with the thieves — the only honest inventory of what was taken. |

**Aftermath.** Fire-sale of the fakes; leaderless cells; half-trained adepts scattering with dangerous knowledge; if this was a senior base, the Vesta-9 file is gone with everything else. The galaxy blames the Vigil, the Admiralty, or an Inheritor purge, and each misattribution lights a different Act 3 fire (Part V). If the crew's gifted stands in the relic hall and feels nothing at all, let that land: the real thing was here, and it is with someone now.

### The second fork, resolved: two Jedi

The Order is alive on Coruscant and absent here, and the campaign's Jedi question is not extinction but **succession**: Coruscant's Order or the Rim's?

- **The Core's Jedi** — Master N'Kata Del Gormo, the Temple's envoy, arriving with the Republic's charter ships to find the gifted children and reopen the sealed temples under the Council's authority. Canon says he ends as a hermit on a swamp world who trains a crashed young Yoda and a human friend, outside the Temple's system — so his arc here is the campaign's own question, *is this really what the Force is about?*, answered by a man who came to the Reach for the Temple and stayed for the swamp. Verdanth is his swamp. The Republic ship that one day answers Yoda's distress call gets there on a lane your players relit.
- **The Rim's Jedi** — Yenna Sar, third student of a Master who refused the recall in the year of the Adjournment; a tired woman on a dry world who has kept vigil over the gift for a century the Temple spent counting children it could reach. To Del Gormo she is the withdrawn: a heresy with a lightsaber. To the Reach she is the only Jedi it has ever met.

The codicil — three Masters, met in a sealed temple, may reopen it and seat a chapter of the Order there — makes the third Master the prize of Act 3, and "who counts as a Master" a war with two faces on it. The crew's gifted adult, too old for the Temple by doctrine and too dangerous to ignore, stands between them. *(Both are statted in [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md).)*

## Dial 2 — The tide

The Force returns **because it returns**. The Vigil's theology, the Inheritors' doctrine, and the Republic's science are all wrong or unprovable; nobody gets to be right, and the mystery stays a mystery.

The Valley of the Jedi is therefore **not** the engine of the Awakening — it is purely the era's greatest choice. The Force-sensitive dream of it the way compasses find north: it is simply the loudest thing the Force has ever done.

## Dial 3 — Vesta-9 was lit by the Sith

The apprentice lit it. The line is hunting one object: **the holocron of Naga Sadow** — the Dark Lord of the Great Hyperspace War's own teaching, five thousand years old, looted during the New Sith Wars into the collection of one of the principalities that held this region, and lost with them when the Withering sealed the Reach. The Sith know it survived; they do not know which principality's vault it sits in, or where in the Reach that vault is. So the Reach had to be reopened, and the line could not be seen to reopen it. Three consequences are now in play:

1. **The Lamplighters are the Sith's scouting arm, and do not know it.** Through a coreward patron the guild believes to be a private antiquarian consortium, the line funds the guild — and the patron's money comes with the patron's *order*. That is why the guild is so insistent that beacons relight in **its** sequence: the relighting order is the Sith search pattern, and behind every certified relight a quiet survey team goes in first. Every first-lighting fee the crew earns is paid, two hands back, by the thing they are helping to find. (the Patronage, below.)
2. **An Inheritor observer was present at the relight anyway.** The Inheritors did not light Vesta-9, but grave-robbers notice when a tomb opens: an unremarkable hull in the arrival swarm filed a census — the crew, above all its gifted — and the Inheritors are now chasing the same vaults from the other side. First contact will be helpful, because it always is: coin, rescue, answers, no strings visible.
3. **The guild leaks twice.** The Inheritors own a mole among the Lamplighters (Chartkeeper Denno Pike) who sells them the handshake codes and the patron's relighting order — which quietly turns Act 2's Conclave of Charts into an auction rigged from two directions: the patron has already bought the order, and the Inheritors have already stolen it. The party can discover and expose either, and the second one they find is the dangerous one.

## Marginalia

**A calendar gift:** Yoda's canonical birth falls in 104 AR — fourteen years after session one. He is not a relic of the old Order; he is downstream of your players. Whatever galaxy they build at the Founding is the one he will be born into — and the Order he someday joins is their legacy.

**Canon is your epilogue, not your cage:** Legends guarantees that a Republic and a Jedi Order stand a thousand years from now, and that Bane's line reaches its final two. It says nothing about what the rebuilding cost, what kind of Republic came first, or whose names history kept. If your players' Founding becomes the seed of the thousand years of peace, the record's silence about them is not a plot hole — it is what recoveries do to their founders.
