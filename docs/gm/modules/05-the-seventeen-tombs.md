# Session Five — The Seventeen Tombs

*The GM-screen module for hop 3, Verdanth. Run it from this page, one scene at a time — every name is introduced and every number stated where you need it; marked names open stat blocks. **Branch-safe**: no particular outcome from Aquilaris is assumed. This is the session where the campaign's two secret engines touch for the first time — the costume Sith and the real one — and the players watch it happen without knowing what they saw. Its climax is not on-screen until the last scene, and then it is the only thing on-screen.*

## What you need to know (even if you read nothing else)

**Where we are.** Hop 3 of the dark Run: **Verdanth** — a rainforest world on the Duros Space Run and the Sanrafsix Corridor, canonically "frequently fought over by petty princelings and would-be Sith Lords" in the Dark Age, with the title *Conqueror of Verdanth* passing to a new lord every few years: **seventeen** self-declared Sith Lords in one century. Every one of them built a tomb, a temple, or a boundary-stone, and the jungle has been eating all seventeen for a hundred and twenty years. The Vigil taped every one of them a generation ago. The prize for Verdanth posted the hour Aquilaris's certificate cleared — **7,000 scrip** — and the beacon mast is a Republic-era relay on a ridge above the old landing field, intact, dark, and *easy*: this is the one relight on the Run that is not the session's problem.

**The twist of the night: the Inheritors live here.** The biggest Inheritor holding anywhere is on this world, in the sealed temple of one of the seventeen — and its **nameplate is chiseled away**, the only tomb on Verdanth without a lord's name. That lord was **Vhasla**, and the Inheritors' leader, known in rumor only as *the Heir*, means to claim descent from him: the defaced stone is the paternity test destroyed at the source. The base runs on *numbers*, like the volume-states it imitates: lore-clerks by the dozen, adepts in pairs, dig crews stripping the other sixteen tombs to feed it. It is theater over surveillance — black robes at distance, wax seals up close, and a census behind the costume that knows what the crew carries ([Vigil & Inheritors](../npcs/vigil-inheritors.md), *How the Inheritors work*). **The Unveiling is not public.** The Heir shows the crew the costume *inside the temple* and makes the offer. **The culling triggers after the crew leaves** — and the crew is still in the system when it does.

**The other thing on this world.** **Brel** — a settlement in the seventeen's country, a day's walk from the temple road, where a ten-year-old named **@@npc:Anwei, the Oracle of Brel@@** dreams true and is worshipped for it: the Awakening's high-water mark on the Run, the Census's target, and the recruit the Heir fears most, because a real one outshines a fraud. Three factions arrive at Brel the same week the crew does. One of them is the Inheritors, and they only had to walk.

- **@@npc:Meru Vane, the First Friend@@** — the Inheritors' face, warm and real, who met the crew at Aquilaris; she brings them to the temple. *(Vigil & Inheritors)*
- **@@npc:Inheritor Adept@@** and **@@npc:Unveiling Blade-Cadre@@** — the drilled rank-and-file, real-but-scraped; the cadre always in pairs. **@@npc:Inheritor Lore-Clerks@@** — the archive, in triplicate. *(Vigil & Inheritors)*

## Scene 1 — The Green

**The jump.** Aquilaris to Verdanth: **Hard Astrogation** (rutter boost). Reversion shows a green world under weather — rainforest to the horizons, rivers like veins, and clearings that are not natural: too square, too many, each one a dead lord's footprint.

**The approach.** The beacon relay reads cold on a ridge above the old landing field — a Republic mast, intact, and the crew can see from orbit that the relight will be the easy part. What is *not* easy: the landing field is busy. A dozen hulls, dig-crawlers on the apron, shoring stacked in cradles, and a road running up into the trees toward a clearing with a black stone temple in it. The Inheritors do not hide their base; they hide what it *is*.

**Landing.** The field is run by the dig crews' foreman — a Devaronian named **Sarl**, who treats the crew as customers: berth is a hundred, fuel is available, and *the temple road is private after dark.* On the apron, among the crawlers, a folding counter of refurbished shoring jacks and lamp cells — *Sund & Daughter* — a young woman selling to the dig crews, giving exact change. The crew can see the temple steps from her stall. *(GM: note where it stood tonight. One line. Nothing more.)*

**The seventeen, from the ridge.** Give the players the world before the plot: from the beacon ridge at dusk, seventeen clearings in the green, each with a stone in it, and grey Vigil tape on sixteen of them catching the last light. The seventeenth clearing — the biggest — has lamps burning, crawler tracks, and no tape at all. *"That one's the temple,"* Sarl says. *"They bought the tape off the wardens years ago. Paid in relics."* (He is wrong — the Vigil do not sell — but it is what the dig crews believe, and it is the first lie the base tells without meaning to.)

## Scene 2 — The Tape

**Warden Tull arrives** with the guild packet — she was always going to; Verdanth is the busiest circuit on the Run, seventeen sealed sites and a relight — and she walks the crew's landing with a slate and a question: *"You have relit two beacons and stood at a grave. I would like you to stand at a seal."* The chapter has decided, after Aquilaris, to open a Verdanth tomb **by the rite** — the first willing unsealing in eighty years — and it wants outsiders with certified names as witnesses. Which tomb is the crew's choice, or hers: any of the sixteen taped clearings. *(GM: this is the Unbroken Seal's setup, and the players are choosing the venue of the campaign's Scrub reveal without knowing it.)*

**The lore of the seventeen.** Tull knows them by name — the necrology has all seventeen — and if the crew ask, she reads them: *Conqueror of Verdanth* after *Conqueror of Verdanth*, most of them dead within three years of the title, half of them killed by the next. **Sixteen names on sixteen stones.** The seventeenth clearing's stone has no name — she has it on the necrology (*Vhasla*, dead 1029 BBY, "of the Menagerie's line") but the stone itself was defaced before her chapter's time. *"Somebody did not want that one read. We taped it anyway. Then somebody bought the tape."* She does not know who; she is a warden, not an investigator. The crew now hold the thing the Heir chiseled a stone to hide.

## Scene 3 — Brel

**The situation.** A day's walk from the temple road, through the seventeen's country, to the settlement of **Brel** — and the seed the beacon map has carried since Act 1 opened: a settlement worships a Force-sensitive child whose true dreams are eating her sleep, and three factions arrive the same week the crew does. **@@npc:Anwei, the Oracle of Brel@@** is ten, knows what the crew's ship is called, and is so very tired. The settlement's armed faithful guard her; her mother tied the dream-cord she wears.

**Three factions, this week:** the **Inheritors** — Meru Vane's people, warm and helpful, with a tuition stipend and a place at the temple for "a gifted child who needs teachers" (she is the recruit the Heir fears: a real one, who would outshine him; the Census's target); the **Vigil** — Tull, or the chapter's next warden, who wants Anwei *kept here*, taught restraint, and taped into the world's care like a grave (keys or children, in one child); and **the crew**, whom Anwei has dreamed. *"You're the ones with the light. You'll go to the black stone. Don't go in the second door."*

**The choice** is the Awakening's whole argument in a village: who reaches a gifted child first, and whether anyone asks her. Run it as three courtships and one exhausted girl. Whatever the crew decide, **Anwei's dream is the session's one true prophecy**, and it is about the temple: *the second door.*

## Scene 4 — The Unbroken Seal

**Run the set piece** — it is written once, in [Vigil & Inheritors](../npcs/vigil-inheritors.md) (*Set piece — The Unbroken Seal*), and this is its venue. What this page adds is the staging:

1. **The rite, at the tomb the crew chose.** Three hours of liturgy at a Verdanth clearing — the lord's own dead read by name, the wax and scripture peeled in order, Tull's hands unsteady with the honor of it. The dig crews come to watch from the tree line. So does a black-robed pair, at distance, saying nothing (theater; note it).
2. **The bare room.** Dust outlines where the relics stood; one counterfeit on the central plinth; the bored shaft behind the shelving, re-mortared decades ago. The seal is honest — that is the horror.
3. **Reading the room** — the set piece's table. The **Knowledge (Lore)** result awards the campaign's **first Sith-tagged fragment** to a crew that reads the plinth-piece right: *only fakes remain; whoever did this could tell.*
4. **Tull's crisis, and the audit.** She does the arithmetic in front of the crew: sixteen taped tombs on Verdanth, and if this one is bare, how many others? Advance the Vigil clock. The chapter's audit begins tonight — and its first finding is already visible from the ridge: the seventeenth tomb, the untaped one, is the only clearing on the world with *lamps in it.*

## Scene 5 — The Second Door

**The invitation.** It comes through Meru Vane, warm as ever, the evening after the seal: the Heir would like to meet the crew who relit three beacons and stood at two graves. *"He buys accounts. He would like to hear yours in person."* The temple road is private after dark; tonight it is lit.

**The temple.** Vhasla's sealed temple, opened and occupied: the outer hall a working archive (lore-clerks at long tables copying in triplicate; the crew's own Vesta-9 account is here, if they sold it), the dig crews' spoils from sixteen tombs catalogued on trestles, the **Blade-Cadre** in mirrored pairs at every door. The costume is perfect and it is everywhere. Then the inner hall: the stage.

**The Unveiling, private.** The Heir — masked, immaculate, seated — welcomes them as *Lord Vhasla*, seventeenth Conqueror of Verdanth reborn in his descendant. The cadre demonstrates (real, drilled, dangerous — the adept blocks stand). The rig performs: lamps dim on cue, a relic on the dais *lifts*, and the Heir delivers his one divination — the thing only the Vesta-9 census could know: what the crew carried off the station and never told anyone. It is spooky until a player realizes it is surveillance, and then it is worse. **He says *Lord*, never *Darth*.** An informed player may notice the costume got the vocabulary wrong; do not point it out.

**The offer.** *"You have the one thing we cannot forge: standing. Your names open sealed doors. Ours open sealed books."* Their archive in exchange — real fragments, real technique, a fake lineage — and if the crew holds Kerra's saber, he asks, with perfect courtesy, to *see* it, and the whole hall goes quiet when it is drawn. **The gifted PC** gets the other offer, privately, from Vane afterward: teachers. Real ones. The Heir means it; it is also the thing that would end him.

**The second door.** Behind the stage, a sealed inner chamber the Inheritors have never opened — Vhasla's own vault — because the seal is genuine Vigil work and they cannot afford to be seen breaking one. Anwei dreamed it. If the crew goes in (the Heir will *let* them, because he cannot, and their standing is exactly what he wants it for): **it is bare.** Dust outlines. The bore behind the wall. One counterfeit. The Scrub was here before the Inheritors ever were — they built their capital on a tomb the real Sith had already emptied, and they have been guarding a fake for years. *(GM: the gifted PC feels it here for certain — the grinding Anwei heard, under the black stone: the Last Muster's call has a direction on Verdanth, and it is not toward the temple. It is past it, up the Run.)*

**Decisions, and none of them roll:** take the offer (standing for lore; the crew is an Inheritor asset, and the file grows); refuse it (Vane is sorry, sincerely; the file grows anyway); expose the rig (the cadre does not draw — the Heir laughs, and *thanks* them: "then you understand what standing is worth"); or tell the Heir what is behind the second door. If they do, watch his face under the mask do nothing at all.

## Scene 6 — The Third Relight, and the Leaving

**The relight is easy** — say so, and let it be: a Republic relay on a ridge, **Average Mechanics** to wake it, **Average Computers** for the handshake, no stations, no clock. The first beacon the crew lights without a fight. Read the firing short: the mast catches, the seventeen clearings take the light one by one across the green, and sixteen stones and one temple stand in it. *Third light anyone here ever gave back.*

**Certification.** @@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@ on the apron: **Average Negotiation or Charm** (upgraded once); the clauses are the guild's, and one of hers — she wants the **dig crews' spoils** (sixteen tombs' worth) declared under guild charting authority, which is a polite way of asking the Inheritors' quartermaster to sell to her. Whether the crew back it decides whether the guild and the Inheritors are partners or rivals at the Conclave.

## Scene 7 — The Emptied House

**Why the crew sees it.** They are still in the system — the outer reversion point, the last jump calc, a day's burn from the ridge — when Verdanth's landing-field band goes *silent*. Then a dig crawler on the open channel, Sarl's voice, and the words nobody on the Run has said in ninety years: *"They're all dead. Every one of them. The temple — they're all dead."* The crew can turn back. They will.

**Run the set piece** — [Vigil & Inheritors](../npcs/vigil-inheritors.md), *The Emptied House* — in the temple they walked through last night. What this page adds:

1. **One night, one base, nobody alive, and the relic hall still full.** No blaster scorings, no cauterized wounds. The senior faces are *not there* (the disruptor); the rest died of falls, of fire, and of each other. The archive rooms are bare — emptied, not burned. The trestles of catalogued spoils are picked over: every genuine piece gone, every fake left on its plinth, *perfectly arranged*.
2. **Reading the stage, in reverse** — the set piece's second table, and the crew planted the normal *themselves* last night: the cadre's inseparable pairs are dead apart; the archive kept triplicates and no brawl destroys all three; the stage rig is untouched, its operator dead mid-cue; the relics arranged. Too many coincidences for a careful investigator.
3. **The Heir.** Not there. His mask is on the dais, set down, as if for the night. *(GM: the disruptor; the one face nobody must identify. If the crew ever wonders whether a fraud among true believers was worth a real Sith's attention — he was the one who called himself a Lord in public, and that is what the line came for.)*
4. **The second door** is open. It was bare before. Now the counterfeit on its plinth is gone too — *the one thing she took that was fake* — because the crew saw it, and a fake the witnesses can describe is evidence.
5. **The GM dial.** The harvested file's one loose page — *Sund's counter, Enarc* — is on the archive floor, or it is not. Your call, now, with the crew standing in the room.
6. **Warden Tull** arrives before the crew has finished reading it. The Vigil will be blamed for this within the week (a seal opened, a base dead) and she knows it. What she says to the crew is the campaign's question in one sentence: *"Was this the practice, or the veneration?"*

## The Out

**Up the Run: Aplooine, hop 4** — the Ash Sea, a breather with a buried mast and a race ([module 04](04-the-ash-sea.md)). The guild's next prize posts within the hour. *Of course it does.*

**Other doors:**

- **The galaxy's wrong answer.** Within the week the Run has decided who killed the Inheritors: the Vigil (a seal opened, a base dead), the Admiralty, or the Inheritors themselves. Each answer lights a different Act 3 fire ([Act 3](../../campaign/act-3.md)). The crew can correct the record — if they want the Weeding to notice.
- **The Vigil's audit** runs all season: sixteen tombs, and the early returns are bad.
- **The fakes** surface on the Memory Market within weeks, consigned by a fence who never met the seller. The trail ends at a dead fence. Sanrafsix is next.

**XP:** 20–25; **+5** for a crew that read the stage in reverse and said the word nobody says.

**The closing image**, in your own words: the beacon on the ridge burning over seventeen clearings — sixteen stones under grey tape, and one black temple with every lamp in it still lit and nobody left to put them out. On the apron, a square of flattened grass where a folding counter stood. Up the Run, one hop ahead, exact change in a drawer.
