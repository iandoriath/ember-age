# The Vigil

*The war's grave-keepers — the watch over the dead and the doors, and the standing peace terms with the bereaved.*

## Who they are

Founded at the war's end by the people who stayed to bury it: Ruusan's grave-tenders, the lay custodians the Order left holding its keys, and the locals of the Brotherhood's old country who dug both armies' graves. When the Jedi sealed their Rim temples in the year of the Adjournment and went home to Coruscant, the Council deeded the stone and the relics to the Vigil — **custody of property, though nothing in the minutes grants them custody of the calling** — under a single instruction: keep it locked. The Vigil kept it locked, and kept everything else besides: the battlefields, the tombs, the sealed academies of the dead principalities, and the lists of the fallen. An angry galaxy tolerates them precisely because they keep it all shut; their doctrine is not only faith, it is the standing peace terms with the bereaved.

They are not a priesthood of the departed Jedi, whatever a coreward visitor assumes at first sight of the veils. They are the people who cleaned up after the Force's last war, on the ground where it was fought — and they know that war the way the Core does not: not as a chapter title but as family memory with map references. The Temple's archive was never scattered; the Vigil's was never *edited*. Which lineages fought here, who refused the recall, what the principalities did to their subjects, where the bodies are — the Vigil holds the ledger the Core sanitized, and reads a page of it aloud at every funeral: the **necrology**, the war dead entered in one column, Jedi and Sith together, because a grave-keeper does not sort the dead by whose fault they were.

## What they believe

Not veneration of the Force — **respect** for it, the way a drowned-sailor town respects the sea: real, holy, lethal, never trusted. **Veneration without practice** is their whole law: tend the graves, keep the seals, teach the gifted restraint — and forbid, absolutely, the *use* of any of it, so that the fire can never spread here again. Eighty years of keeping those terms have made them the Rim's own answer to the Force, and they have had time to decide they prefer it to Coruscant's.

## What they want

The seals honored. The graves left alone by the prize-rush pouring up the Run. And the Awakened of this country kept *here*: the gift is a war orphan — they buried its parents, and they mean to raise its children — taught restraint, never an order, never a title, never sent coreward at five years old to a Temple that did not come to the funerals.

## What they offer a wayfinder crew

- **Sanctuary** — with no registry attached.
- **The war's true records** — authentication of war-era relics and [lore fragments](../mechanics/lore-fragments.md), and testimony the Core never wrote down. (They are the second-best authenticators in the galaxy. Nobody, including them, knows who is first.)
- **Access to sealed places** no one else may enter — including the temples the Temple now wants back.

## The crack

The newly Force-sensitive split them in half: sacrilege walking around in children's bodies, or the Order reborn. The Order's return splits them again: half the Vigil will hand over the keys and weep with relief; half will not hand over the children, and has begun to wonder whether eighty years of keeping the watch has *earned* something the minutes never granted.

And beneath it sits the deed itself: their authority covers stone and relic, not flesh — a distinction somebody from Coruscant is eventually going to read aloud, in a sealed temple, with two other Masters present.
