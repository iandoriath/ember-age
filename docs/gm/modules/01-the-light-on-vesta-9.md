# Session One — The Light on Vesta-9

*The GM-screen module. Run the session from this page, one scene at a time — each scene is self-contained: the situation, who is in it and what they want, the checks, the outcomes, and what they seed. Marked names open stat blocks in [Vesta-9 & the Crew's Orbit](../npcs/session-one.md); the [player recap](../../campaign/session-one.md) is the only player-safe summary of this session.*

## Overview

**The session in one sentence:** the beacon nobody could find for five years lights itself, a broke crew races the port to it, and what they *testify* about who lit it — in front of three factions and an enemy, one faction nobody knew existed this morning — becomes the founding paper of the reopened Run.

**The session's jobs**, in rising order of consequence: teach the [lore fragment](../../mechanics/lore-fragments.md) subsystem at the moment a single page is worth a ship's life; put three factions and an enemy on the ground — the Lamplighters, the **Naboo Admiralty** (whose existence is tonight's reveal), the Vigil (a familiar strangeness at the edge of the scene — the Run's spacers already know the grey veils as a weird religious sect), and @@npc:Captain Ferro Skell@@ with a bought debt in his pocket; put the crew **on the record** as witnesses to a lighting nobody claims, holding evidence nobody else has; and open the corridor up the dark Run, where [Act 1](../../campaign/act-1.md) lives. If the table drives home arguing about what they put on the paper — and what they kept off it — the session worked.

**The spine** — eight beats, about four hours with a break:

| Beat | Scene | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| The argument, then the sky changes | 1 — Grell's Office | ~20 min |
| Fuel, the champion, the errand | 2 — The Field | ~25 min |
| The factor, the deed, the page | 3 — The Bedside | ~25 min |
| Up the dead lane | 4 — The Race | ~35 min |
| The standoff at the light | 5 — The Beacon | ~55 min |
| Lighting party: ______ | 6 — The Record | ~40 min |
| The return, and the way out | The Out | ~25 min |
| Flags for session two | The Handoff | ~5 min |

**Cut for time, never for weight.** If the clock runs hot: compress the fuel problem to one roll, reach the page through whichever vector is nearest, and let the *Steadfast* arrive early to squeeze Scene 5. Never cut the bedside, the moment the paper asks who lit it, or the closing image — the session is remembered by those three, in that order.

**The numbers, on one line** *(all sector scrip)*: lease arrears **450** + lift tax **60** · fuel load **150** (off-book half-load 90) · Osk's bought debt, in Skell's pocket **250** · rutter copy **80** · witness fee **1,200** · the famous posted prize **12,000** · witness charter **250**/lighting.

**Prep checklist** — eight items, half an hour:

1. Tie each PC's Obligation to the grounding — one written sentence per PC; the table in Scene 1 does most of the work.
2. Print the [Reading the Dice](../../mechanics/dice-results.md) player sheets; this session leans on Advantage/Threat spends.
3. Keep the [session one NPC file](../npcs/session-one.md) open — the whole cast is statted there, marines and minion math included.
4. Pick two or three [Act 1 episode seeds](../../campaign/act-1.md) and pencil them onto the [beacon map](../tools/beacon-map.md) (real systems, Enarc through Jutrand).
5. Make the props — they beat exposition: the fragment card (*Grandmother Osk's rutter, page 40* — form, tag, Question, claim), and the certificate of relight with a blank line the players will physically write on: **"Lighting party: ______."** One sheet each.
6. Know the lien. @@npc:Captain Ferro Skell@@ bought Osk's medical debt this morning (250 — Scene 3), and under the Drift's ledger-law a bought debt is a lien on the debtor's property — arguably including the rutter under her pillow. If that paper is still in Skell's hand at certification, he has a *claim*: "the chart that found this station is collateral on a debt I hold; the finding is mine." Tender the 250 at the bedside and the lien never exists — his corner at the beacon collapses to hearsay. Leave it standing and Scene 5's third corner arrives holding it, where it can still be tendered or contested on the merits (*Contest it*, Scene 5). Decide nothing else in advance; the argument's strength is the table's to test.
7. Decide privately what @@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@ saw. She was at the *dark* station before it lit — kneeling at the cold core, reading the necrology, when the light came on around her — and she has no ship anyone can find. **Default answer** (override freely): a Vigil circuit ship dropped her here on the warden's round — seals checked, names read — and returns on schedule, meaning the Vigil knew where Vesta-9 sat all along and never told the guild, because doors stay shut; she is the campaign's second person who knew and said nothing, and both facts are discoverable later. And she *saw the lighter*: a shape that looked at her, and left her alive. The necrology covers all of it: she answers every question with the list of the dead.
8. Confirm the famous number (12,000 unless you've changed it) and know why nobody on the field can claim it: nobody on the field lit the beacon. That gap is Scene 6.

!!! danger "GM only — the Sith are on the field"
    The Sith apprentice lit Vesta-9 tonight — deliberately, calmly, and exactly per plan. The line flies the dark lanes without beacons; beacons are for the *galaxy*, because the search rides the guild's public advance, and the advance was stalled on exactly this beacon. For years the line did not even know a true chart existed — Osk's survey was assumed lost with the hands who flew it — until the guild's repeated failed surveys forwarded "the one pilot who won't sell" to the patron's desk. Then it took a season: Mendac visited the blind woman's bedside months ago as a kind stranger with a sheet of jump-knots and — Influence, the liar's power — walked her through the run in a voice Osk trusted; Osk does not know what she told her. The visit, the station, the emptied principate cache (no holocron), and the light on the way out: routine, to them. The galaxy's comfortable wrong answer ("some prize-hunter who didn't live to file") was part of the calculation. (Optional body for the Question, for later acts: the survey hands from ten years ago did not outlive the trip home — a drifting hulk off the lane can one day answer "who lit Vesta-9?" with corpses and a worse question.) Nothing on the field knows any of this, and nothing should. What *is* on the field is an **Inheritor observer** — grave-robbers notice when a tomb opens — a clerk in guild grey standing in the certification crowd who writes nothing down. Spend exactly one throwaway line of description on them tonight, no more; the docking manifest in Nerra's packet makes them findable later, which keeps the reveal fair. They pay off silently in The Out. The line's full machinery is in [Keepers of the Flame](../npcs/keepers.md).

!!! tip "This party (update as the roster settles)"
    Bind the generic hooks to the actual sheets. **Mikrés Kyríes** (Chadra-Fan Technician, Debt: predatory lease, Pursuit: Wealth): Grell's impound *is* the lease — the creditor has a face tonight; Nerra's 3,000-scrip offer for Osk's page is aimed squarely at Wealth-versus-a-promise; and Chadra-Fan are canonically terrified of drowning, which is worth remembering when hop 2 turns out to be a drowned world. **The Republic Trooper** (Human Soldier, Dutybound, Oath: the Republic): the best version of his story out here — his garrison's charter was suspended *sine die* in 12 AR and three generations kept the post anyway; he is a living Civic fragment, the verdict Question — *should the Run be reconnected at all?* — is on his sheet in the form of an oath kept to a Republic that adjourned him, and Reike's pitch ("the writ passed to those who kept it") is a personal attack; the Dutybound row in Scene 1 is his (confirm it with the player). Reike should court him; Nerra courts Mikrés; Vhail watches the gifted. **The Dathomiri PC** (the crew's pilot): self-taught, no tradition, no teacher — not a Nightsister; her gift is exactly what the Awakening makes and exactly what every faction tonight wants to register, buy, or protect.

!!! tip "Table talk before the dice"
    If session zero did not already do it, say the era's thesis line aloud — *nobody broke the galaxy; everyone just stopped holding it up* — and remind the table that in this era the Force is not a skill, it is an event. Both lines pay for themselves later.

## Scene 1 — Grell's Office

**The situation.** Okrent's Drift is a boomtown that bet on the Run reopening and has spent years losing the bet: Bannistar's fuel gantries were raised new to feed the Lamplighters' advance — four beacons relit in a decade, every prize paid, the last to the *Larksong* — then Vesta-9 refused to be found, and the port slid into recession. The crew opens the session mid-argument in @@npc:Portmaster Grell@@'s impound office, and the argument is a perfect circle: the ship stays bolted until the lease arrears are paid; the crew can't pay until they sell the cargo; the cargo can't sell until it clears inspection; inspections are backlogged for weeks; and impounded vessels can't even be *scheduled* for inspection until the lease is current. Every door in the loop is locked from the far side, and Grell built the loop. Let them argue in character for ten minutes — no dice needed yet.

> The office smells of fuel-tax stamps and someone else's lunch. Grell hovers at eye level behind the ledger-slate so nobody can look down on him, and he has just explained, for the third time, patiently, that the rule that would free your ship is behind the rule that would free your cargo, which is behind the rule that would free your ship. Somewhere out on the field, a lane departure you are not on lights the window.

**Why *you* are stuck here** — invoke each PC's Obligation as the specific reason the Drift has them:

| Obligation | The grounding, personalized |
|---|---|
| **Debt** | The impound *is* the collection. Grell's ledger carries your marker's interest, and the Kajidic dues-taker at the gate knows your face. |
| **Family** | The cargo is the lifeline — medicine or parts consigned for your homeworld, seized with the hold. |
| **Oath** | The dying archivist's last commission is in those crates, and the deadline was last week. |
| **Dutybound** | The impounded crates are the garrison's resupply, bought on a post's credit the Republic stopped honoring in 12 AR; Grell's ledger carries the requisition as private debt (confirm with the player). |
| **Notice** | A reconnected world's magistracy re-issued an old writ the moment the lanes reopened — paper that slept for years is awake, and it reached this port before you did. Lifting without clearance confirms everything it says about you. |
| **Secret** | Someone on this field knows — and the price of their quiet was the cargo. |
| **Obsession** | You spent the fuel money on a fragment lead that went nowhere. That is *why* you cannot pay Grell. |
| **Responsibility** | Your refugees are camped at the field's edge; feeding them is where the scrip went. |
| **Contract** | The Lamplighters hold your ship's papers, and their factor here grounded you pending arrears. Remember this in Scene 5: Chartmistress Nerra is your creditor's colleague. |
| **Posted** | A Guild hunter is drinking in the cantina, deciding whether the posting on you still bonds. Lifting fast is suddenly very attractive. |

The pilot's grounding waits on her sheet; until then the impound holds the ship, which holds her.

**Then, mid-sentence, the sky changes:**

> It starts as a murmur on the depot's public band, then every nav board in the port lights at once. Vesta-9 — dark since your grandparents' time, a dead name on old charts — is broadcasting, clean and steady, like sixty years never happened. Across the field a freighter spins up its lifters without waiting for clearance, and then everybody is running.

Nobody in this port needs the stakes explained — they have been the port's whole conversation for years — so put the *disbelief* in a dockhand's mouth: *"Five years of surveys. Best chartkeepers in the guild. Nobody could find it. And it just — came on."* Then the famous number, said the way people say a lottery number. Nobody on the field has stopped to ask who could claim a prize for a beacon nobody lit. They will.

**The deal.** The light changes the argument: the crew suddenly needs sky, and Grell — watching a port full of brand-new grift assemble outside his window — suddenly wants this conversation over. For the first time all morning, both sides of the desk want a deal. Listed price: **450 scrip** of lease arrears plus a **60-scrip lift tax** (the inspection backlog quietly evaporates the moment the arrears are "resolved" — the loop was never about the rules). Odds are the crew cannot come close to raising it — say the number out loud anyway; the shortfall is the whole scene. Every ten minutes of haggling his eyes drift to the window and the price of his patience drops. He is venal, not cruel: every fee has a listed price, and he honors what he signs. If violence clears its throat he drops behind the desk and starts naming lower numbers — keep him alive; a portmaster who owes the crew goodwill is Act 1's best recurring door.

| Approach | Check | It works | The downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haggle his angle | **Average Negotiation**, upgraded once (Natural Negotiator lets him force one reroll) | He'd rather own a piece of a rumor than scrip today: the debt converts to a signed 15% of any first-lighting fee. Advantage shaves it to 10%. | Threat: the 60-scrip lift tax stays, cash up front. |
| Lie to him | **Average Deception**, upgraded once (Nobody's Fool) | The bolt comes off on a forged consignment or an invented patron. | Discovered — failure now, or any Despair in the port later — he doubles the release to 900, honors nothing unsigned, and says your names to the Kajidic dues-taker (+5 Debt Obligation, or seed one). |
| Slip the bolt | **Hard Skulduggery** on the impound clamp | A quiet release and no paper at all. | Threat: tamper logs — Grell knows before you clear the gravity well. Despair: the bolt's tracer fires on lift; the debt beats you to Vesta-9 in the guild post. |
| Pay | 510 scrip they do not have | The dockside Kajidic lender advances it in minutes. | Ruinous interest: Debt Obligation +5, and the young Hutt's clerk smiles as she files it. |

**It seeds:** whatever paper the crew signs here follows them — Grell's cut resurfaces in The Out, the lender's book in Act 1, and a crew that walks out with *no* deal is down to the Skulduggery row or stealing another crew's ship, which is a different campaign.

## Scene 2 — The Field

**The situation.** An obstacle course with an audible clock: every ten minutes of table time, narrate another ship lifting. The crew's morning in Grell's office cost them their fuel-queue place — they join at the back, watching fueled ships climb.

| Where | What is there |
|---|---|
| The fuel queue | 150-scrip lift-and-jump loads, a line out the gate, and the *Larksong* holding the head of it. |
| The cantina gantry | The rumor network at full boil: the chart-broker, the wager board, word of who has already lifted. |
| The depot infirmary | Tama Osk — and, tonight, the debt-buyers who got to her bedside first. Scene 3. |

**The fuel.** A lift-and-jump load runs **150 scrip**. Options: **Average Negotiation** with the depot clerk for deferral against the first-lighting fee; **Average Streetwise** for an off-book half-load at 90; or the head of the queue, below.

**The champion.** Holding the head of the queue: the @@npc:Larksong crew@@, three spacers in patched vac-jackets who finish each other's sentences, and their captain — @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@, who waves at the crew from her boarding ramp like an old friend, because by the third race she genuinely will be. Everyone in the port knows the *Larksong*: hers is the crew that claimed the Triffis prize, the last one the guild paid before the Run stalled, and the wager board has her as the favorite before she finishes waving. Her ship is statted — @@npc:The Larksong, prize-money YT-1300@@ — the crew's own pattern with the prize spent on it: Speed 4 to their 3, a Class 1 hyperdrive to their Class 2, no backup hyperdrive at all. Let the players hear the turbine before they see the ship. Play her fast, warm, and better-prepared: she names the wager (loser buys the winner's table a round, every port, for a season), and when she sees where the queue has put the crew she splits her allocation without being asked — *"I want to beat you, not lap you."*

**The errand.** Waving off thanks, she flips them a folded flimsi: a fresh sheet of **jump-knots** — astrogation teasers off the cantina rack, the spacer's crossword, two bits at the commissary. *"Call it even: drop these with Grandmother Osk in the infirmary — I bring her the new sheet every time I'm through, and tonight I'm racing you. She's blind, so you'll have to read them to her. She does them in her head faster than you can read. Bring your manners."* A genuinely trivial errand, and Marrow thinks nothing of it — it is the fig leaf that lets a proud, broke crew take the fuel. Her deckhands walk the split load to the crew's pad while the errand runs — which is why the crew has ten minutes for a dying woman in the middle of a gold rush. The *Larksong* lifts before they're back, cheerful wave from the ramp.

**The other ways to a page** *(if the bedside never happens, the fragment lesson still can)*: @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@ owns a copy of Grandmother Osk's rutter — the *old* log, the living-days route every Run family traded around — and trades page 40 for the crew's signature backing the *Larksong* as co-witnesses at certification. The chart-broker under the gantry sells a fair copy for **80 scrip** — or for the full, embarrassing story of how the crew lost their cargo; he collects embarrassments — and throws in a hint free: *"Mine's a copy. The original's owner is dying about forty meters from here. She doesn't sell."* Copies give the Scene 4 boost die. They do not carry the claim.

**Advantage/Threat menu** — tuned to this scene:

| Result showing | Spend it as |
|---|---|
| 1 Advantage | A dockhand throws in charged cells: remove a setback die from the crew's next shipboard Mechanics check. |
| 2 Advantage | Grell lets slip the count — how many ships have lifted, and that the *Larksong* is fueled but waiting on a chart. |
| 2 Threat | The Kajidic dues-taker at the gate writes the crew's names in her book. |
| 3 Threat | The split load walks over slowly: the crew's lift slips ten more minutes, and one more rival hull gets ahead of them. |
| Triumph | Grell smells profit and flips: release fee waived for a signed 10%, plus the depot's sixty-year-old lane chart — a boost die in Scene 4. |
| Despair | The impound bolt scars the gear coming off: the ship starts Scene 4 carrying 2 system strain. |

!!! tip "If it drags"
    Marrow pays. She strolls into the impound office mid-haggle, counts out Grell's 510 from her winnings pouch, and tells the crew they owe her "one page's worth" — then races them anyway. The debt is real, not flavor: "one page's worth" is the co-witness signature (Scene 2's trade), and she collects it at certification, in front of Nerra, at the least convenient moment she can find.

## Scene 3 — The Bedside

**The situation.** @@npc:Tama Osk@@ — the rutter's author, vacuum-lunged in the depot infirmary. Play her as a Duros somewhere past a hundred and fifty, blind these last two years, entirely unafraid: she flew the Run when it was whole, kept flying its dark stretches for decades after everyone else stopped, and speaks in a dry rasp that costs her something to use. (No stat block — her card is prose; she never leaves the bed.) If the table wants the quiet beat before the storm: a crew member reads the first jump-knot aloud and the blind woman solves it before the sentence ends — the port thinks she is a relic; she is the sharpest navigator the crew has ever been in a room with, and now they know it before anyone mentions a chart. Under her pillow is the **original** — her dark-years marginalia: true positions, drift shadows, which buoys were warm — the one chart in the galaxy that finds Vesta-9 without a broadcast to home on.

**What the crew walks in on.** However they arrive — Marrow's errand, the broker's hint, their own reasons — they arrive mid-scene: @@npc:Captain Ferro Skell@@ and two of @@npc:Skell's crew@@ are around the bed, not shouting, which is worse. Skell bought Osk's medical debt off the infirmary's books this morning — **250 scrip** of it, purchased within the hour of the boards lighting — and he is explaining, reasonably, that the *Overdue* collects what it is owed, that a dying woman has nothing else of value, and that everyone in this port knows what is under her pillow. The arithmetic is honest and vile: the relight makes her book the key to every prize on the Run, and Skell means to hold her last weeks hostage for it. (He cannot actually win — Osk has out-stubborned richer and scarier people than him for ten years, and she would die first — but nobody in the room knows that yet, and her last weeks getting uglier is real.)

**The deed** — no prompt, no promised reward, and these are not people who fold to a raised voice: **tender the debt** — 250 scrip counted out before witnesses; under port ledger-law a registered debt tendered in full is discharged, and Grell will enforce his own rules with visible pleasure — but the money has to come from somewhere that hurts tonight; **face them down** — **Hard Coercion**, or Negotiation with something real behind it: Skell is violent, not stupid, and a crew that looks like more trouble than a book is worth gets a shrug and a *"see you on the lane"*; or **make it a fight** — they will happily oblige, two of them plus Skell in an infirmary ward, stun settings nowhere in their doctrine; the orderly screams, Grell fines everybody, and the grudge is now blood. Doing nothing is allowed: Skell keeps the squeeze, Osk gives him nothing, the errand is delivered, and the crew never learns what was under the pillow — the copies still teach the fragment lesson, and the second audition (The Out) stays open. Either way, remember what this scene is really for: Marrow is the rival the crew will love. **Skell is the other kind.**

**The down payment.** If they act, she chooses — and says why, citing what this table actually did (the tender, the stare-down, the thrown punch — make it specific): *"Everyone in this port is running toward money tonight. You just spent yours keeping wolves off a stranger. Sit down."* What she gives is **not the book.** It is **page 40, the original** — margins no copy carries — plus the claim, said aloud, free: *"Somebody has been flying that lane for years. The buoys were warm when I went back. And the guild's prizes go exactly where those people wanted to go."* And the promise, both halves: *"When it answers you, tell it Osk finished the run. And don't sell my page to the people who were just in this room."* Then the hook: *"Come back and tell me what you found up there. Then we'll talk about the rest of the book."*

Two threads for later, if the conversation opens: asked whether she ever told a soul where Vesta-9 sits, her rasp goes quiet — *"Once. A young woman. Right questions, kind voice. I couldn't tell you a single word I said to her."* (GM: the Sith bedside — a living-witness thread that pays off when the crew someday has a silhouette to match; do not explain it tonight.) And her old **navigation droid** — blind pilots need eyes — went to a chop-yard for debts years ago; its unwiped core holds fifty years of the Run. Recovering it is a quest hook with a technician's name on it (the buyer, if anyone ever chases it, was Okkam Dree).

One more buyer, for texture: the chart-broker on the Field mentions a rival bidder for rutter pages who pays in *pre-war coin* — "the antiquaries," people call them. It is the same word the guild's coreward patron hides behind. Two collectors of old things, one a costume and one the real thing; the mid-game twist is that there were always two. Say it once and move on.

When a page lands — hers or a copy — hand over the card and log it (copy only: log the card with the claim blank, and let them notice the blank):

@@action:fragment {"name":"Grandmother Osk's rutter, page 40","form":"rutter","tag":"Civic","question":"Who lit Vesta-9?","claim":"somebody has been flying the dead Run for years, and the prizes follow them","holder":"crew","status":"trusted"}@@

!!! tip "Teach the subsystem out loud"
    Say the rules as you hand the card: it has a **form**, a **tag** (Civic), and it bears on a named **Question** — *Who lit Vesta-9?* It boosts related Knowledge checks; three fragments on one Question unlock a [Reconstruction](../../mechanics/lore-fragments.md). Open the [fragment tracker](../tools/fragment-tracker.md) where the players can see it. Fragments from different tags always disagree on one load-bearing fact — write Osk's claim down now so the contradiction is loaded before anyone notices.

**It seeds:** keeping the first half of the promise — her name into the beacon's carrier at certification — gives a boost die on the Speak-to-it check, and Vhail hears it, and looks at the crew for the first time like they might be worth talking to. The inheritance — the whole rutter and the whole story (the coreward "antiquarians," the survey she flew before any prize was posted, the places that should have been empty, the people who did not come back) — is a *return* scene: The Out tonight if twenty minutes remain, session two's cold open if not. She will already have heard what went on the record; coming back at all is the second audition. When she hands the book over she has weeks left; her funeral is Act 1's first — and play it as a scene, not a line: in this era the war dead are read aloud at funerals, Jedi and Sith entered in one column, and if the Republic Trooper wears the uniform to it, the whole port notices. **Why not Marrow:** Osk loves her, and the guild owns her — the Triffis prize made the *Larksong* the guild's poster crew, and Osk's one hard criterion is *owes the guild nothing*. The old log was her consolation gift years ago, and Marrow has never once asked about the original, which is part of why Osk loves her. The errand is exactly what it looks like — a friend's small habit, handed off in a hurry; Marrow has no idea what it set in motion. If the crew ever tells her, she is quiet a moment, and then: *"Good."*

## Scene 4 — The Race

**The situation.** Frame it mechanically before anyone rolls: this session *is* the [beacon-relighting set piece](../../mechanics/beacon-relighting.md), run out of order. **Wake it** was done by an unknown hand — that mystery is the campaign's. The crew is flying **Chart it** now, and **Speak to it** waits at the station.

!!! tip "Weather with teeth (optional)"
    A cheap, perfect Threat spend anywhere on the leg: **mynocks** — the dead lane's pigeons — latched on at the last cold buoy and chewing the power couplings (1 system strain a round until somebody EVAs or barrel-rolls them off; [The Feral Run](../npcs/wildlife.md)). Sixty dark years put colonies on every cold surface out here, and the crew's running lights are the first thing to disturb them in a lifetime.

**The jump.** The Run's first dead leg — Bannistar toward Enarc — has decayed past its nav data: **Hard Astrogation**, exactly per the beacon rules. A rutter page bearing on the route adds a boost die — the fragment lesson, taught at the moment it matters. Jumping blind is legal but earns a setback die for sixty years of drift silt. *(Why the Larksong's Class 1 hyperdrive doesn't simply win: on a decayed lane the clock is recalculation, not multiplier — the legs between dead buoys are short, and every reversion resets the math. Open lanes are where she laps people; dead ones are won by charts.)*

**While the navigator plots** — one supporting beat per other PC, so nobody watches one person roll. Astrogation is Mikrés's; the Dathomiri flies.

| Crewmate | Check | On success |
|---|---|---|
| The pilot | **Average Piloting (Space)** at each reversion, flying the dead buoy's mass shadow by feel | Success removes the blind-jump setback or clears 2 system strain; Threat here is where the mynocks latch; a Despair here is the misjump scare. |
| The engineer | **Average Mechanics**, coaxing the heirloom drives | Remove the setback from any Scene 2 hull damage, or clear 1 system strain mid-run. |
| The slicer | **Average Computers**, scrubbing sixty years of silt from the nav data | Add a boost die to the Astrogation check. |
| The lookout | **Average Perception** at the reversion points | The dead drift buoys read a heartbeat early — downgrade one Despair-shaped surprise to a Threat-shaped one, narratively. |
| The captain | **Average Leadership**, calling the run | Recover 2 strain across the crew; the ship flies like the crew believes in it. |

!!! tip "The house spends are live"
    First scene where the [Ember Age spend table](../../mechanics/dice-results.md) earns its keep: 2 Advantage on a relight stage hands a boost to whoever tackles the next one, 2 Threat on decayed-lane Astrogation leaks the route to a rival, a Despair means the relight broadcasts wider than intended. Let a player find the table themselves; approve generously.

**Neck-and-neck.** Mid-run, the lane forces a recalculation at a collapsed drift buoy, and the *Larksong* reverts alongside. The crew's fresh solution shows what the older copies miss: the buoy's mass shadow has drifted into the third leg, dead across the *Larksong*'s plotted line. The choice: **comm the warning** — lose the lead, arrive together or second, and cement a friendship the campaign will spend for years — or **stay silent** — arrive first while the @@npc:Larksong crew@@ limps in behind a scarred hull, and start the rivalry cold. Do not soften it. And remember who Marrow is: the wager board's favorite, the crew that claimed the last prize the guild ever paid. Nobodies who beat the champion by warning her are a story the port tells for a season. Nobodies who beat her by letting her hull get scarred are a different story, and the port tells that one too.

**Outcomes ladder:**

| The roll | What happens |
|---|---|
| Success | Clean jump — first of the swarm proper. The station is not empty even so: the warden was here before the light, and if Skell ran his default race (paper first — see *Where is the Overdue?* below), the *Overdue* is already on the ring, mid-recital. Perhaps an hour of quiet before the rest of the sky fills. |

## Scene 6 — The Record

**The situation.** Trigger it at the height of the standoff — ideally mid-certification. Nerra's form has a line on it. So does Reike's fleet log. Both say the same thing — *Lighting party / responsible hand* — and everyone in the chamber has just heard it read aloud, and everyone is looking at the witnesses of record.

> Nerra's stylus stops over the line. "Lighting party." She looks up, pleasant as weather. "You were first aboard. Who do I write?" Across the ring, Reike's adjutant has the same line open in the fleet log and is waiting with the patience of a man who is paid to wait. He says one thing, to the room, before anyone answers: "A contested corridor is a naval matter, Chartmistress." At the rail, Skell has his own stylus out, smiling like a man offering to split a bill. At the core, Vhail has stopped reading. She has not turned around.

**What the crew is holding** — take stock out loud, because the players may not realize it: Osk's claim that somebody has been flying the dead Run for years, and that the prizes follow them; the wake log — inside release, valid guild codes, cleaned up within hours; a docking cradle still warm, no ship anyone can find; a station three light-hours off every chart, lit by someone who knew where it was. Nobody else in the chamber has all of it. Two of the three corners have already guessed at part of it.

**The choice belongs to the table, and it is not a roll:** what goes on the record? The certificate is the first paper of the reopened corridor; everyone who comes up the lane after them will read it; their names are on it as witnesses. There is no wrong answer. Every answer is the scene.

**Four wants, aimed at the paper:**

- **@@npc:Commander Joss Reike@@ — the finding.** *"Unknown armed actor operating a dead lane with guild codes"* is the exact line his log needs to make this corridor "require naval protection." He will not lead them there; he does not have to. His adjutant already said the only thing that needed saying, and the players heard it before they answered: testify to a deliberate unknown hand, and the corridor is contested, and a contested corridor is a naval finding. Say the stranger out loud and he thanks them, sincerely. Say nothing and he enters *"lighting party: undetermined; witnesses uncooperative,"* which is worse for them and better for him. And he has a personal stake: half the port already believes his own fleet lit it, and *"lighting party: not the Admiralty"* is the other line his log requires — witnessed, signed, preferably tonight.
- **@@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@ — the clean line.** *"Unknown hand; lane clear"* is what she wants written, and she pays for it: strike her fine print, upgrade the witness charter, enter them as claimants for the famous number. She goes quiet at the handshake codes, quieter at the name Osk — and the moment she learns an *original* page exists, she offers real money on the spot: **3,000 scrip** for the page, rising toward the famous number for the book it came from, if the crew can produce it — or say where it lives. That is the sale Osk has refused for ten years, arriving at the crew's elbow hours after they watched Skell try to *collect* it at her bedside — the same appetite, with better manners. Her Deception 3 is for what the offer omits: where the book goes after. (GM only: to the patron's survey teams, within the month.)
- **@@npc:Captain Ferro Skell@@ — the co-signature.** A fifth of the famous number, offered flat, to co-swear his finding: the lien stands, the chart was collateral, the claim is his — with the crew's names beside his as witnesses of record. It is Osk's promise sold hours after they made it, to the man who was at her bedside — and it is real money, tonight, no fine print. He watches them do the arithmetic and does not rush them. *(If the 250 was tendered, this bullet never happens: he has no paper, and he knows better than to bluff Nerra.)*
- **@@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@ — the truth at the core.** She wants nothing written. She wants it *said*, at the core, honestly — and afterward she finds whoever spoke alone, asks exactly one question — *"Who opened this door — and did you say so?"* — and leaves without pressing. A crew that told the truth leans her report toward the wardens who think the seals are being worked, not merely failing — the Unbroken Seal's argument; a crew that certified it clean is entered on her slate, in the margin for the living.
