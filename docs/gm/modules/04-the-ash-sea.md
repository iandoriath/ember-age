# Session Four — The Ash Sea

*The GM-screen module for hop 4. Run it from this page, one scene at a time — every name is introduced and every number stated where you need it; marked names open stat blocks. **Branch-safe**: no particular outcome from Aquilaris or Verdanth is assumed. This is the Run's* breather *— no faction runs this world, no grave is argued, nobody is saved. The world is the antagonist and the people are just people. Let the table race, dig, and get paid.*

## What you need to know (even if you read nothing else)

**Where we are.** Hop 4: **Aplooine** — canonically "smothered in black ash up to 300 meters deep," no breathable atmosphere, "only a scattering of stable landing points atop weathered mountains" — and inhabited anyway, continuously, since the Kymoodon Era thirteen thousand years ago: it is one of the two worlds from which this whole pocket of space was first settled. The peaks are islands in a black sea. A few thousand people live in a handful of sealed **peak-holds**, and their whole culture is one thing: **crossing.** Nobody governs Aplooine; the ash does. The holds are family outfits that trade by crawler across a sea that *moves* — the **ash-drivers** — and the only status on the world is having made a crossing nobody else would. No council. No law but the driver's rule: *never cross on a slide day.* No opinion about the galaxy beyond *does it pay for the crossing.* The tone is a frontier port, not a temple — loud, competitive, superstitious about the ash, and delighted that strangers have money.

**The twist of the night: the mast is on the wrong peak.** The beacon relay stood on a mountaintop the ash *swallowed* two generations ago — a slide took the old relay peak and its dome under, and the mast's top three meters still show above the surface like a snorkel, sixty meters of black between it and the dome. To relight it the crew must **drive the Ash Sea to a peak that no longer exists, dig down to a dome nobody has entered in sixty years, and light a beacon underground** — in a room that fills with ash as they work. At Aquilaris the mast was under water; here it is under earth. The guild's prize: **5,000 scrip**, posted the hour Verdanth's certificate cleared.

**What the ash gives up.** The same slide that buried the relay peak uncovered something older, a kilometer from the dig: a **Kymoodon-era colony hulk** — thirteen thousand years old, the ship that seeded this pocket of space — surfacing from the black like a whale. The drivers treat it as weather (things surface; things sink). The crew are the first people with the equipment to go *in*.

**The cast, one line each** *(stats in brackets)*:

- **Hesk Varrow** — the Run's crossing champion, driver of the crawler *Long Odds*; wants the crew's crawler in the season's run across the Sea, and their money either way. Unstatted — borrow @@npc:Swoop Ace "Kettle" Maro@@'s block for the race. *(Out on the Run)*
- **The Varrow hold** — Hesk's family outfit, the biggest on the world: ash-crawlers, dig-rigs, a dome with room to rent. The drivers who will haul the crew's dig crew if paid, or if beaten. Unstatted.
- **Old Mim** — the holds' ash-reader, who calls slide days by the color of the sky and has been wrong twice in forty years. Unstatted. The one person the drivers obey.
- **The Fyrnock colonies** — ninety years of dark under the buried dome. *(@@npc:Fyrnock@@ — the Feral Run)*
- **@@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@** — the certifier, a day behind, with 5,000 scrip and a question about what the crew found on Verdanth. *(Vesta-9 & the Crew's Orbit)*

**Rules shorthand:** Average = 2 difficulty dice, Hard = 3, Daunting = 4. The ash is a hazard, not an enemy: **breathing it** without a sealed suit is 2 wounds a round (ignore soak); a **slide** is a Hard Athletics or Piloting to clear, Daunting to turn a crawler through. A **fragment** is the campaign's clue-card; log it in the [fragment tracker](../tools/fragment-tracker.md).

## Scene 1 — The Black Sea

**The jump.** Verdanth to Aplooine: **Hard Astrogation** (rutter boost). Reversion shows a world that is *dark* — not unlit, black: a surface of ash under a sky the color of iron, and a scatter of lights on mountaintops, each one a hold.

**The approach.** The only landing points are the peaks, and the peaks are private. The Varrow hold's dome has the biggest pad, and the price is stated on the open band before the crew has finished the approach: *"Berth is two hundred a night, air is fifty a head, and if you're here for the mast you'll want to talk to Hesk before you talk to anyone."* Nobody asks who they are. Nobody has ever asked.

**The mast.** From the Varrow pad, on a clear evening, the crew can see it with a scope: three meters of corroded lattice standing out of the black sea eight kilometers off, where the relay peak used to be. The drivers call it **the Snorkel**. Everyone knows what is under it. Nobody has been down.

**The ash.** Give the table the Sea before anyone crosses it: a black plain to the horizon that *moves* — slow dunes walking, a slide on the far range going down like a dropped blanket, and the wind carrying grit that hisses on the dome. Old Mim, on the pad, reading the sky: *"Slide day tomorrow. Nobody crosses. Day after's good."* The drivers obey her the way sailors obey a tide table.

## Scene 2 — The Varrow Hold

**The hold, seen walking.** A pressure dome on a mountaintop, three generations deep: crawler bays, a dig-rig yard, a market where the holds trade across the Sea, and a *drivers' hall* where the crossing records are chalked on a wall that goes back two hundred years. This is a frontier port — loud, fast, fair in the way ports are fair: everything has a price and the price is posted.

**Three things to do here, in any order:**

- **The dig crew.** Digging sixty meters to a buried dome is a hold's work, not a crew's — rigs, drivers, a crawler-train. **Hesk Varrow** will sell it: *"Four thousand, or you drive."* The race (Scene 3) is the alternative. **Average Negotiation** knocks the price to three; **Hard** to two, if the crew can prove the certificate will pay.
- **The drivers' wall.** Two hundred years of crossings, chalked by hand — and the last sixty years of the Snorkel's column blank: *nobody has crossed to the dead peak since the slide.* A player who reads the wall (**Average Knowledge (Education)** or just curiosity) finds the entry for the slide itself: the relay peak's hold was **counted out** the day it went under — forty names, no bodies. The mast is standing on a grave the drivers do not talk about.
- **Old Mim.** The ash-reader sells slide days for a price and, for a story from outside, tells the crew the one thing the drivers believe: *the Sea gives things up when it wants them found.* Last season it gave up the hulk. *"Thirteen thousand years and it decided now. Ask yourself why."* (She does not know why. The crew's beacon is why — the ash moves to the Run's weather; a relight brings the prospectors, and the drivers have already noticed the season's slides are wrong.)

**Odri's absence — one line, never remarked on:** the hold's dock-log, if anyone reads it, shows a **chandler's crawler** that came across the Sea last month, sold shoring to the Varrow rigs, and left — and paid for *no air*. The only visitor in the log who did not buy breath. Say it once. Move on.

## Scene 3 — The Crossing

**The situation.** The season's run across the Sea is tomorrow — the day after slide day — and **Hesk Varrow** wants the crew's crawler in it. Stakes he proposes: win, and the Varrow rigs dig the Snorkel for free; lose, and the crawler is his. (The crew's ship has no crawler; the hold *rents* one — *"tuned exactly as well as I want it tuned"* — or the crew builds one from the ship's speeder and a lot of Mechanics.) A crew with no driver can hire one and ride as crew, which is the drivers' idea of cowardice, and they will say so.

**The race — the dune sea chase, honestly earned.** Four crawlers, three legs across a moving plain:

| Leg | The ash | The check |
|---|---|---|
| **The Flats** | Hard-pack, flat out, the whole hold watching from the dome | **Hard Piloting (Planetary)** |
| **The Walking Dunes** | Dunes on the move — the line that was clear at dawn is a wall by noon; drivers read the Sea or die in it | **Average Piloting (Planetary)** plus a setback for the shifting ground; a passenger may substitute **Survival or Perception** to read the dunes for the driver |
| **The Snorkel Turn** | Around the dead peak's mast and back — the one place on the Sea nobody crosses, and the reason Hesk wanted the crew: *he* has never rounded it either | **Hard Piloting (Planetary)**; a **Despair** here is a slide — the crawler goes *under* for a round (Hard Athletics to get out and up), and the Sea gives the driver a look at what is buried before it lets go |

Best of three legs. Hesk rolls three Piloting with a boost (home ground); Advantage carries forward as boost dice; a **Triumph** anywhere is a lead nothing answers; a **Despair** is a slide (survivable, and the drivers *love* it). Hesk cheats exactly once — a rig-rat loosens the crew's crawler's ash-skirt overnight; a **Hard Perception** at dawn catches it — and if caught, he pays the dig himself, grinning: *"Worth a try."*

**If the crew wins:** the dig is free, Hesk's respect is real, and he enters *their* crossing on the drivers' wall — the first new entry in the Snorkel's column in sixty years. **If they lose:** the crawler is his, the dig costs four thousand, and he offers to run the dig anyway on credit against the certificate, because he wants to see what is under there as badly as they do.

## Scene 4 — The Hulk

**The situation.** A kilometer from the dig site, the thing the Sea gave up: the **colony hulk** — a Kymoodon-era settler ship, thirteen thousand years old, canted out of the ash with its prow to the iron sky, its hull the pitted grey of something that was never meant to be seen again. The drivers give it a wide berth; Old Mim would not cross within a kilometer of it on any day. The crew have suits, lamps, and the only cutting gear on the world.

**Going in** — a ten-minute derelict crawl, with the ash as the clock (the Sea is *reclaiming* the hulk; every round inside past the fourth, one setback die as the hull shifts):

- **The hatch:** **Hard Mechanics or Skulduggery** — thirteen millennia of ash in a lock.
- **The hold:** a colony ship's cargo, entombed and dry: seed vaults (dead), water tanks (crystal), and the settlers' **founding log**, on a medium no living reader can play — **Daunting Computers or Knowledge (Education)** to coax a rendering out of it, *or* **Average** for a Technician with the right talent and a long night.
- **The dark:** the hulk is *inhabited* — a @@npc:Fyrnock@@ colony that came in through a hull breach ninety years ago and owns the lower decks. Light the hold and you herd them; light everything and you fight them in a canted corridor. Two or three, not a swarm; the point is the dark, not the body count.

**The founding log.** It has nothing to do with Sith or Republic — it predates both here by ten thousand years. A settler captain's voice, describing the ash sea from orbit on the day the ship arrived: *"No one sent us. No one is coming. We will count ourselves."* It is the oldest fragment in the campaign and the cleanest evidence for the verdict Question's third rung: *these people were here before the galaxy had an opinion.*

## Scene 5 — The Dig, and the Third Relight

**The dig.** The Varrow rigs cut sixty meters of black in a day, shoring as they go — and the crew works the face with them, because the rigs stop at the dome and the dome is the crew's. **Average Mechanics** to keep the shoring true; **Hard Athletics** to work the face; a **Despair** is a slide into the cut — 4 strain, a round buried, and the rigs pulling everyone out by the ankles.

**The dome.** Sixty meters down, the relay peak's dome: sealed, dark, and *dry* — the slide entombed it whole. Inside, ninety years of what moved in: the Fyrnock, which own the dark, and the hold's forty dead, where the slide left them. This is the session's one solemn beat and it costs nothing: let the drivers who came down with the crew go quiet, and let one of them start reading names off the hold's wall. Then work.

**Three stations, and the ash is the fourth** — the mast's foot is in the dome, its lattice runs up through sixty meters of black to the Snorkel, and every minute the dig is open the Sea is coming back in:

| Station | Where | The work | On a Despair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The mast root** | The dome's core, the beacon's foot, corroded solid | **Hard Mechanics** to free the seating and seat the igniter | The lattice shifts in its sixty-meter sleeve of ash; someone has to *climb the inside of the Snorkel* to re-seat the head — **Daunting Athletics**, in the dark, in a tube. |
| **The capacitors** | A gallery off the dome, sealed, breathable — and the Fyrnock's nest | **Average Computers** for the handshake, then **Hard Mechanics** to sequence stacks that have never fired; the colony must be *herded out with light* first (the Feral Run's territory rule) | Arc-flash and a dead lamp: the gallery goes dark with the colony still in it. Fight in the dark, or finish by feel. |
| **The cut** | The dig itself — the shoring that keeps sixty meters of black off everyone below | **Average Mechanics** each round to hold the shoring, by the drivers or the crew | The cut slides: the dome is sealed again with the crew inside, and the only way out is up the Snorkel — the tube everyone else has been avoiding. |

Two or three rounds, the ash as the clock: **every round past the second, one setback die at every station** as the black comes in. Support: comms from the surface (**Average Leadership**, a boost), a spotter on the sky (**Average Vigilance** — Old Mim's slide warning), a driver holding the rigs at the cut.

**Threat menu:**

| Result showing | Spend it as |
|---|---|
| 2 Threat | A slide on the far range — the Sea is moving; one more setback everywhere below. |
| 3 Threat | The Fyrnock come *up* the cut, into the drivers; the rigs pull back for a round and the shoring goes unheld. |
| 3 Threat | The Snorkel's head is under — the surface has shifted; the beacon will fire *buried* unless someone clears three meters of ash off the top by hand. |
| Despair | **A slide day, early.** Old Mim was wrong for the third time in forty years: the whole cut goes, the dome seals with everyone inside, and the Third Relight finishes underground with the mast as the only exit — **Daunting Athletics** up sixty meters of lattice in a tube, in the dark, one at a time, with the beacon humming under their hands. Nobody dies who keeps their head. |

**The firing** — read it slowly:

> The charge runs up the lattice through sixty meters of black, and for a moment nothing shows — then the Snorkel catches, three meters of light on a dead sea, and the whole Ash Sea around it goes silver as the grit in the air takes the glow. On the peaks, every hold's dome has its people at the glass. On every band, in the oldest voice in the sky, Aplooine says its own name — a world that has been here thirteen thousand years and never once asked to be found — *because you lit it.* Third light anyone here ever gave back. Under your feet, the dome is filling with ash, and it is time to climb.

**Certification.** @@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@, on the Varrow pad with the sunset and the paperwork: **Average Negotiation or Charm** (upgraded once). The clauses tonight are small and strange — Hesk's crossing record on the certificate (it costs nothing and he will remember it forever), the Varrow hold's **dig rights under the dead peak** (a precedent, if the crew named the harvester stations at Aquilaris — Nerra notices the pattern and says so), and the founding log's custody. Nerra asks, once, lightly, what the crew found in the temple on Verdanth. Answer or not; she writes something either way.

| Lit it and left, clean | 5,000 scrip and a corridor | The prospectors come to a world with no room for them, and the drivers get rich hauling them across a Sea that is starting to slide wrong. |

**The chase, if it comes to one:** nobody chases anyone across the Ash Sea. The Sea does that.

## The Out

**Up the Run: Sanrafsix, hop 5** — the great dead hub, the Memory Market in orbit over a plague-emptied city, the Inheritors' recruitment proper, and a chandler's counter beside theirs. The guild's next prize posts within the hour. *Of course it does.*

**Other doors:**

- **The slides are wrong.** Old Mim will say it to anyone who listens: the Sea is moving to a weather she has never read, and it started the season the beacons began. (GM: the Feral Run's eviction rule, written in ash — and, further off, the vergence pulling at everything; the gifted PC felt the hulk *surface*.)
- **The forty names.** The relay hold's dead are on a wall sixty meters down. A Vigil warden will want them on the necrology; a driver will want them left where the Sea put them.
- **Hesk's wall.** A crossing record on a guild certificate is a thing the drivers have never had — paper that leaves the world with their names on it. Whether it was worth the Sea is theirs to say.

**XP:** 15–20; **+5** for going into the hulk — this session rewards the crew for doing the thing nobody sent them to do.

**The closing image**, in your own words: the Snorkel burning three meters above a black sea gone silver in its light, and behind it, canted to the iron sky, a ship thirteen thousand years old sinking a hand's breadth an hour back into the ash that gave it up. On the Varrow pad the drivers chalk a name on a wall. The Sea moves. Nobody on this world will ever ask the galaxy for anything — and tonight, for the first time, the galaxy can find them.
