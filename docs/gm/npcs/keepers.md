# Keepers of the Flame — GM-Locked Force Users

Ninety years after Ruusan, the trained Force survives in exactly three places, and only one of them advertises: the Temple on Coruscant, a swamp on Verdanth, and the Banite line, two or three successions deep and patiently setting the table. This file holds the campaign's locked Force users — the two Jedi, the two Sith, the dead withdrawn lineage's legacy, and the dark-side vergence the Inheritors are digging into — because Acts 2 and 3 turn on who reaches the new generation first. These entries are the campaign's locked answers made playable.

**GM ONLY — SPOILER-CRITICAL. Nothing in this file is player-facing; it implements Part V (locked) of the planning document. Do not read aloud, do not summarize to players, do not leave open at the table.**

## The Two Jedi

The second fork of Dial 1, resolved: the Order is alive on Coruscant and absent from the Run, and the campaign's two Jedi disagree about what a Jedi is *for* — the Temple's service, or the swamp's. Neither is statted; both are questions, not encounters (see each entry's Stats line). The withdrawn lineages are dead — what they left behind closes this section.

### Master N'Kata Del Gormo
*Nemesis — no faction (the swamp) — the hermit of Verdanth; run unstatted*

> Four arms, four small black eyes, a serpentine lower half at ease in black water, and a twisted staff crowned with a crystal prism, used mostly to point at things he finds funny. His neighbors do not call him a Jedi. They call him the fellow in the swamp, and they are careful to speak well of him.

**Canon:** a Hysalrian Jedi Master who, according to Legends rumor, lived as a hermit on a swamp world and trained a crashed young Yoda and a human friend until a Republic ship answered their distress call; the dates are "lost." Yoda is born in 104 AR; the swamp is **Verdanth**, hop 4 on the Run; and the Republic ship that answers the distress call gets there on a lane your players relit. Nothing published contradicts any of it.

**The break:** He left at the Reformation itself. When the Order placed itself under the Chancellor, he said the thing out loud — *the Force outlives every government that has ever claimed it, and a Jedi who becomes the state's cop has confused the vessel for the water* — and walked rimward while every other Jedi walked coreward. He has been on Verdanth about ninety years; Hysalrian lifespans are unrecorded, which is to say, long enough. He does not use the word *withdrawn*, does not consider himself to have left anything that still existed, and has never once wondered whether Coruscant approves of him.

**Stats:** none, deliberately. He is a teacher and a question, not an encounter; if violence ever reaches him, the scene has already gone wrong in a way no stat block fixes. (If a block is ever truly needed, assign an official Jedi Master chassis and verify it against print before use, per this library's sourcing rule.)

**Using him:** Zen absurdity with a true bottom — jokes, riddles, wrong answers that turn out to be the rightest thing anyone says all campaign. Play him at an angle to every scene: he answers the question under the question, feeds people before he will argue with them, and finds the Temple's solemnity genuinely, warmly hilarious. He anchors the campaign's spiritual contrast in one line: **the Vigil kept the stones, the Temple kept the rules, and the swamp kept the thing itself.** His arc is not a turn — he is already what the legend says he becomes — it is a siege: the Awakening keeps washing gifted people up on his shore (the Last Muster's call, further down this file, is why *this* shore — he has heard the grinding for ninety years, knows the sound is grief and not invitation, and will say so only in riddles), Tane keeps arriving to win an argument he declines to have, and the campaign gets to ask him whether transcending politics also means abandoning people. He is the era's rarest thing, a living-mentor Wellspring (Part VII): he can support Force Rating increases and new power trees, and he teaches the way swamps teach — slowly, wetly, and only what you came in already carrying.

**Wants from the crew:** nothing, which is the most disarming thing on the Run; later, the true shape of what they saw at the beacon — he collects what the Force is *doing*, never who is doing it. **Offers:** sanctuary with no registry attached; training that does not require joining anything; and, to the crew's gifted adult, the one thing neither Temple nor Sith will offer — an answer to *what is this for?* that is not also a recruitment.

### Master Seldra Tane
*Nemesis — the Jedi Order (the Temple) — the Core's envoy to the Run; run unstatted*

> Human, mid-forties, travel robes pressed anyway. Every form performed perfectly — the bow measured, the certification sealed, the courtesy exact — and the forms are not empty: they are what held her Order together after Ruusan, and she can tell you which grave each one is written on.

**The mission:** She arrives with the Republic's accreditation mission after the third certified relight reaches the Core's newsfeeds (Act 2 — news travels by courier, so give it a season), carrying both halves of the Core's offer. The Temple's half: the gifted children found and sent, the sealed temples reopened under the Council, and no Jedi in the Rim who answer to no Council. The Senate's half: reintegration under a single **sector seat** — one senator for the whole Grumani sector, in place of the old one-world-one-seat delegations (see [the Republic](../../factions/republic.md)). She believes in both, and she is the best possible person to sell them: taken at three, no attachments, a flawless record — the system's perfect product, sent to sell the system that made her.

**Stats:** none yet, by the same rule as Del Gormo: an envoy and a question, not an encounter. If a duel ever becomes possible, assign an official chassis and verify it against print before use. Until then she does not roll.

**Using her:** Form and ritual above meaning and intent — but play the forms as load-bearing, never as parody: her certifications are real, her word is the one Republic writ that cannot be forged out here, and the grief under the doctrine is real too. Her contempt for Del Gormo is doctrinal, not personal, and it has weight: when the Order buried its Knights at Ruusan and rebuilt itself as service, he kept the Force and abandoned the people — desertion in robes, however serene. His serenity sounds different when you remember he pronounced it while the graves were being dug; make the table feel that her side of the argument is *hard*. She keeps visiting the swamp to win it, keeps losing, and keeps coming back — run those visits on camera; they are the campaign's philosophy given two voices, and they should be funny until the moment they are not. Whether she holds to the end or cracks in the swamp is play's to decide: write her firm and visiting, and keep both endings live. Del Gormo's counter, when it finally lands, is not an argument — it is noticing, aloud, that nobody (not the creche, not the Council, not the Senate) has ever once asked her what *she* wants.

**Wants from the crew:** the names of the gifted (she will ask politely, once); passage and local standing for the mission; an honest account of the hermit on Verdanth, about whom the Temple's file is one rumor long. **Offers:** a Jedi's word; Jedi-tag authentication from the one archive that was never scattered; charters with the Senate's seal on them; and, to the gifted adult she cannot train and cannot stop watching, attention without ownership — the Temple's whole unanswerable problem in one person.

### What the withdrawn left behind

The Rim lineages — the masters who refused the recall in the year of the Adjournment and taught on where the anger was thickest — starved for pupils through the Ebb, and snapped. The last was **Master Tolum Rade's**, a survivor of Ruusan who taught three students on **Veshet**, off a dead spur of the Sanrafsix Corridor; his third student outlived him by twenty years and died a generation before session one, unremarked, under a farm name. There are no withdrawn left to find. What there is instead is *evidence* — three objects loose on the Run, each a campaign hook and a [lore-fragment](../../mechanics/lore-fragments.md) source of the first order:

- **The graves on Veshet.** Four unmarked stones on a dry hill that the locals still, without ever saying why, leave alone. A Force-sensitive visitor knows why the moment they stand there (Jedi-tagged fragments). The Vigil would call the hill a shrine; the Temple would call it a doctrinal problem; both would be describing four farmers.
- **The heirloom lightsaber.** Four generations old, last worn wrapped in a seed-sack, and loose in the fragment economy since the lineage's last estate sold for debts — currently an unresolved provenance question on the Memory Market at Sanrafsix (Episode 4 hook), where nobody yet knows what they are holding. The day someone authenticates it, everyone wants it: relic, proof, or bait. And keep one promise to the table: no lightsaber ignites on-screen in this campaign until it matters — so when this one lights for the first time in ninety years, let it be in a *player's* hand, in a room, with the witness rule firing like a struck bell.
- **Rade's rutter.** The lineage's true legacy, and this section's live grenade: a coded route-log of the families that hid gifted children across the Run, kept so the withdrawn could find and quietly mind them. The Temple wants it for the quota, the Inheritors want it for recruiting, and the line wants it for the Census (Dial 1) — so whoever the crew lets hold this book is deciding who reaches the Awakening's children first. Surface it mid-campaign, and play it as worse than a weapon: a weapon only kills the person it hits.

**The codicil.** Three Masters, met in a sealed temple, may reopen it and seat a chapter of the Order there — which makes "who counts as a Master" a war with two faces on it. The Run holds one unimpeachable Master, one hermit whose standing Coruscant has doctrinal reasons to deny (if Del Gormo counts, the Temple does not control the count), and an empty third chair the campaign can fill: the crew's gifted adult — too old for the Temple by doctrine and too dangerous to ignore — a second envoy of the Council's choosing, or nobody.

## The Unbroken Line

Dial 1, statted. Bane, then Zannah, then Cognus — and the record goes silent, because the line got better at silence. Cognus's recorded apprentice, Darth Millennial, rejected the Rule of Two and walked out on *her* to found the Dark Force cult on Dromund Kaas; Legends names no loyal heir after him, so the Master who actually inherited collides with nothing published. Here that Master is **Darth Veritis** — bent from *veritas*; you hear *verify*, *verity* — and the apprentice, whose name nobody outside the line has ever heard spoken, is **Darth Mendac** — bent from *mendax*, the liar; you hear *mendacious*. The Master takes the truth; the apprentice leaves the lie. Third succession from Bane: young as Sith dynasties go, consolidating, and in no hurry, which is precisely why patience costs them nothing. Their goals, machinations, doctrine, and the holocron they hunt are below in this file; the culling's set piece, *The Emptied House*, is with the faction it happens to, in [Vigil & Inheritors](vigil-inheritors.md).

!!! note "Easter egg — Screecher's Reach"
    These two are the Mother and Daal from *Screecher's Reach* (Star Wars: Visions, volume 2), read the Ember Age way. The record says the line went silent after Cognus; what happened is that her apprentice drove her mad, entombed her alive in the cave the locals came to call Screecher's Reach, and declared herself Dark Lord — **Darth Veritis**, the Mother. The screecher was Cognus: a century-old Sith Master, blind and wailing in the dark, and the test Veritis set every candidate she brought to that world was to go into the cave and come back out. None did — until a workhouse girl native to the planet, whom the Mother sensed while testing someone else's children, went in with her friends, killed the thing in the dark, and walked out carrying its lightsaber. That was **Daal**, about seven years before session one; she is **Darth Mendac** now, twenty or so, and the only apprentice the Mother ever took who was not hand-picked. Cognus died at the hands of a child with no training, which is the kind of truth Veritis would scrub from any archive but her own. If you want the cave on the chart, a dead principality spur fits a workhouse world — Gazzari or Chelloa — and the tomb is then one more Sith vault on the Run that the Scrub has already visited.

### What the line wants, and how it works

**The strategy in one line: every true thing about the Sith, out of the galaxy and into her hands.** Veritis is not building an empire, a treasury or an army, and wants none of them yet. Seventy years after Bane, the line's whole position rests on two facts — it holds the only unbroken transmission of the dark side, and the Jedi believe the Sith extinct — and both are facts about *knowledge*. So that is what she works on. Every holocron, teaching, tomb-record and court archive the New Sith Wars scattered is to be found, verified, and removed: the genuine into the line's keeping, the galaxy left with nothing but its fakes. The Republic will be corrupted eventually; this century's work is making sure that when anyone goes looking for what the Sith were, they find only what Veritis has left for them.

**Why she is looking at the Run.** The Grumani sector was the Brotherhood of Darkness's country — the Daimanate, the Bactranate, the Arkadianate, the Dyarchy were its inheritance — and their vaults hold the war's true records: rosters, academy lists, correspondence, the paperwork of a thousand self-made Dark Lords. Every page is *incriminating*: it is the evidence from which anyone could reconstruct what the Sith were at the end, and therefore deduce what the Sith became. The worst single item is **the holocron of Lord Kaan** (its own section below), which names Bane and records the heresy that became the line. The line has known since Bane that the record existed — he is *in* it — and known only that the war's end lost it; the catalogue's standing best guess has always been the Bactranate vaults under Jutrand. So the country had to be cleared before the galaxy came back to read it — and when the clearing outran two pairs of hands, the Run had to reopen *in her order*, and the line could not be seen to open it: a guild has been opening doors for her for a decade, one beacon at a time, in her sequence — and the one door the guild could never find, Mendac finally opened herself, lighting Vesta-9 on her way out of its emptied vault so the advance would resume.

**The Patronage.** Through a coreward front the Lamplighters believe to be a private antiquarian consortium, the line funds the guild. The money comes with two conditions the Grandmaster of Charts took to be a collector's vanity: the relighting proceeds in the patron's sequence, and the patron's survey teams get first look at anything a relight uncovers. The sequence is the Sith search pattern — principality by principality, vault by vault — and the survey teams are the Scrub. The prizes are wages: the guild believes the patron is buying beacons, when it is really hiring ten thousand prospectors to turn over a country's soil — and the first-look clause is both cover (her people, anywhere on the Run, with paper that explains them) and the whisper network's first thread. The pattern is even visible from the outside, to anyone who thinks to look: it was the patron's money that turned the guild off the Corellian Run at New Cov ten years ago — Ryloth unreached, the Core baffled — and pointed it down the dead Duros Space Run toward the principate vaults. The day the guild pivoted is the day the search began. Ophira Zel does not know who her patron is. She has begun to suspect that the registry leaks; she has not begun to suspect the money.

**What Veritis wants.**

- **The truth, all of it.** Verification is the point: she can tell a genuine relic from the Inheritors' best forgery at a glance, and the line's inventory of what survived Ruusan is the only honest one in the galaxy.
- **The galaxy wrong, not ignorant.** Absence invites questions; error answers them. Counterfeits are left exactly where they lie, fake lineages are allowed to flourish, and a rival who has trained on a forgery is worth more to her alive than dead — until he starts to find real things.
- **Exactly one heir.** Any third claim on the Sith inheritance — grave-robbers in costume (the Inheritors), a prophet in the Sith Worlds (Millennial), a Temple that starts looking outward — is not an enemy to be fought but a reader to be denied the book.
- **To be unnecessary.** Never a name in a dispatch; never a protagonist. The day anyone can point at Veritis is the day the century was wasted.

**What Mendac wants** is simpler and younger: to be good enough. She was a workhouse child who walked into a cave on a dare and walked out over a Sith Master's body; everything since has been the Mother teaching her what that was worth. Anonymity is the grade; the Inheritors are the exam; and underneath the doctrine is an apprentice who has begun to suspect that patience is the Master's weapon against *her* as much as against the galaxy — which is the Rule of Two working as designed, and not this campaign's problem. She has friends she left on a workhouse world seven years ago. She has never once checked on them. She thinks about it more than the Mother knows.

### The doctrine of the open road

**The night was made.** Galaxy-wide, the era's theme holds — the Ebb was real, nobody broke the galaxy, and the Withering has no villain. This region is the locked exception: **the Run withered because the Sith required time for their searches.** The line needed a dead country to work in — decades of it — and a galaxy-wide recession was perfect cover: beacon by beacon, across thirty years, the lane's lights were *helped* out — maintenance funds that evaporated, tenders retired and never replaced, relay contracts dead in committee — and every serious push to relight or re-bond met the Weeding (the Jutrand Commission strangled, syndicates unbacked, surveys lost). Nobody asked why the Run's lights died, because everyone's lights were dying. That is the kind of game the line plays: it did not fight the galaxy for the region — it let the galaxy's own bad decade carry the region away, then stood custodian over the dark while the vaults were emptied. The relighting now under way is not the night ending — it is the custodian finishing: the country reopens hop by hop, in her order, exactly as fast as it is cleared.

One rule governs the line's whole relationship to the network: **the Sith never light beacons.** They fly the dark lanes without them — beacons are for the *galaxy*. Why the line wants the country opened at all is the piece to hold onto: **travel was never the bottleneck; discovery is.** The line is two people, and the holocron is one object somewhere in a century of looted courts, sealed tombs, and resold estates — thousands of rooms on dozens of worlds, most of them under dead-man locks nobody has opened since the lights went out. Two people cannot excavate a sector. Only an economy can: prospectors crack vaults for salvage, heirs sell estates, scavengers strip ruins, and everything found must eventually surface at a choke point she already watches — the guild's registry, the certification desks, the Memory Market. She can open the road; only the galaxy can turn over the soil. The reopening is a mining operation: her money funds the mine, the galaxy supplies the labor, the guild is the mine office, the Memory Market is the assay office — and she skims the one nugget she came for while salting the tailings with fakes.

**The method is two-phase, and the timing matters.** *Ahead of the light:* the patron's sequence is a timetable — before a region's beacon relights, Mendac has already swept its **known** targets, the catalogued vaults and courts and tombs, while the country is still dark and empty of witnesses; then the beacon lights *behind* her and the prospectors arrive to find doors already visited. She stays a sector ahead of the advance, always. *Behind the light:* the diggers turn up what no catalogue listed — hidden collections, mislabeled spoils, an estate nobody knew held anything — and the **whisper network** flags it: market listeners, certification clerks, salvage brokers and fence-side ears, none of whom know whom they report to, assembled through the patron's cutouts and fed by the contract's first-look clause. When something genuinely Sith surfaces behind her, she doubles back and takes it — by quiet purchase through cutouts when possible, by the Weeding when not. A sector ahead of the advance; a whisper behind it.

One population is largely immune to the line's favorite tools: **Hutts.** Influence breaks on Discipline like Sorulba's (5, behind Nobody's Fool 3), and Hutt courts price loyalty too well to buy cheaply — which is why the line never talked the closed kingdoms open and simply routed *around* Hutt space, and why the ghost-traffic shows up in Hutt picket logs and nobody else's records at all.

**Vesta-9 was the exception that proves the doctrine, executed without drama:** the advance was stalled on the one beacon nobody could find, so the line found it and lit it — plan, not improvisation. For years the line did not know a true chart existed (the old survey was assumed lost with the hands who flew it); only when the guild's repeated failed surveys forwarded *"the one pilot who won't sell"* to the patron's desk did anyone coreward learn of Osk. Then it took a season. Mendac sat at the blind woman's bedside as a kind stranger with a sheet of jump-knots and — **Influence**, the liar's power, one target made to believe something untrue for a few minutes — walked her through the run in a voice Osk trusted. Osk's own mouth gave up the position, and she cannot remember doing it. Visit, station, emptied cache (no holocron), light on the way out. The galaxy's comfortable wrong answer ("some prize-hunter who didn't live to file") was priced in.

### The machinations

Off-screen and ongoing — draw on them whenever you need the pattern to show from very far back:

1. **The Weeding.** Whenever the relighting stalls — a certification held up, a corridor claim contested, a magistrate who would rather the lane stayed dark, a guild officer who questions the patron's order — the obstacle removes itself. A rival withdraws without explanation; a hostile official retires young and quiet; a rutter that would have started a war goes missing from a dead man's cabin. Mendac's hands, Veritis's patience, never a trace. The players should feel the recovery is lucky. It is not. The Weeding also guards the far door: every few years coreward money flirts with forcing the Dead Gate — reopening the Run from Darkknell and meeting the advance in the middle — and the talk always dies: a syndicate loses its backers, a survey is cancelled, a prospectus goes missing. A push from that end would reach Jutrand's vaults *before her gardeners do*, and Jutrand is where the catalogue's best guess lives. One obstacle never removed itself: **Tama Osk** — see the Vesta-9 passage above for why the Weeding never came for her, and why that is the doctrine working *and* the doctrine being wrong about what a dying woman's respect turns out to be worth. Fair-play rule for the whole Weeding: every removal carries one learnable tell — **the obstacle vanishes the night after a guild packet leaves port.** A crew that runs that pattern down (Streetwise, across episodes) has earned the first rung of the Ladder of Sightings ([Faction Clocks](../tools/faction-clocks.md)).
2. **The Scrub.** The line's real work on the Run, run in the two phases above. Run it at the table as the crew arriving somewhere already visited — a tomb with fresh footprints and nothing in it, a dead principality's archive whose only surviving volumes are the ones a Daunting Knowledge (Lore) check reveals to be worthless. The Memory Market at Sanrafsix is watched, not bought from: the day a genuine piece surfaces, its seller has a short future. Improv hook: a scholar of impeccable manners who asks to *see* a relic and is visibly, quietly relieved to find it is fake. And the Scrub's best-kept secret is its victim: **the Vigil's sealed sites are the catalogue's richest entries, and the Vigil's own doctrine hides the thefts** — a seal is never opened, so nobody has checked a vault's contents in eighty years, and whole shrines under faithful guard are already bare. The reveal — the crew and the Vigil unsealing a vault together and finding dust outlines — is the set piece *The Unbroken Seal* in [Vigil & Inheritors](vigil-inheritors.md), and it is the single best recruiting scene the Ladder of Sightings has.
3. **The Wasp Trap.** Millennial's heresy on Dromund Kaas is alive and tolerated. It is a sump for the loud: the ambitious, the visionary, the seer who would otherwise draw attention inside Republic space. Veritis's people never touch it — occasionally they *send* someone to it — and it will be culled the day it reaches Republic space or the day a Darth title out of the Sith Worlds is taken seriously. Dromund Kaas is on the far side of the Rim from the Run; if the heresy ever touches this campaign, it arrives as a recruiter, a rumor, or a prophecy that is suspiciously accurate about the Valley.
4. **The Census.** The Awakening is the line's future. A generation of untrained Force-sensitives with no Order to claim them is either the greatest recruiting pool the Sith have ever had or the seed of the Jedi's return, and Veritis means to decide which. The line keeps its own watch on the gifted, and the Inheritors' harvested Vesta-9 file (they watched the crew arrive at the door Mendac opened) completes it: the crew, the ship, the corridor, and — if the crew ever showed it to anyone — the rutter Osk kept from her for ten years. Which of them is gifted, the line learns later, the way it learns everything. **Their desire is not to kill them.** Gardeners are not pruned, and Mendac's successor has to come from somewhere: the Rule of Two recruits at generational distance, and if you want a sequel hook, the apprentice's apprentice may already be at your table. One more thread: the drift the Census keeps logging — gifted moving up the Run against all economics — is the Last Muster's doing (below), and Veritis cannot hear the call herself. The Census is quietly also her search for an ear.
5. **The Graduation.** The culling of the Inheritors is Mendac's exam — the trigger, the aftershocks, and the *Emptied House* set piece are with the faction they happen to, in [Vigil & Inheritors](vigil-inheritors.md). It is the Weeding, the Scrub and the Census done in one night.
6. **The Absence.** Never on camera *in the early acts*. Veritis manifests as pattern and Mendac as aftermath, and through Acts 1–2 no encounter with either is intended — mystery is the engine, and both should feel unbeatable because the crew is not meant to be fighting them yet. But the Absence is a doctrine the players can break with evidence, not a decree: the Ladder of Sightings ([Faction Clocks](../tools/faction-clocks.md)) is how — and rung four is where "mysterious" ends and the statted antagonists above step into the light.

### The Holocron of Lord Kaan

Legends gives Kaan no holocron, so this collides with nothing — and this campaign is its whole story. It is not a teaching holocron in the old sense: cut in the war's last years, it is the Brotherhood of Darkness's own record in its Dark Lord's voice — part archive, part manifesto, part confession — and it is the single most incriminating object in the galaxy. Chapter by chapter it proves what the Sith were at the end; its third chapter names the dissenter; and anyone who reads that chapter and then looks hard at the present — obstacles that remove themselves, a hand that can tell true relics from false — can do the arithmetic the Jedi have never had reason to attempt: *the heresy did not die at Ruusan.* It is the line's birth certificate, and Veritis will spend a century to bury it. She cannot use it — see the Gatekeeper — she can only silence it.

**What is on it.** Five chapters, in Kaan's own voice:

1. **The Brotherhood** — what he built and why: the Darth title abolished ("a hundred Darths had made the word a civil war"), every Sith a Dark Lord, equality as weapons discipline, the academies. The pitch that emptied a generation into his armies, in the voice that made it work — the one first-person account of what the Sith had become, and the truth Veritis wants out of every archive but her own.
2. **The War** — the New Sith Wars from the Sith side: the Grumani principalities as the Brotherhood's inheritance, with rosters, worlds, vault inventories and supply lines. Functionally this chapter is the Scrub's own catalogue in the enemy's hand: whoever holds it can *predict the patron's relighting order* — the most dangerous navigational fragment in the campaign.
3. **The Dissenter** — Bane, named. The miner from Apatros; the academy record; the heresy quoted in full so that Kaan could refute it — *that the Brotherhood was the Sith's disease, and that there should be two, no more, no less.* Ninety years on, the refutation reads as prophecy annotated by its victim. The Rule of Two's birth certificate; the name **Bane** enters the campaign here and nowhere else.
4. **The Weapon** — the thought bomb: the ritual, its price, and what it actually does. It does not kill; it **seals** — the souls it takes go into the bomb, not into the Force. Kaan records the working as his closing argument ("the enemy cannot win a war I am willing to end"). Read forward it is a weapon. Read backward it is a *lock* — and a lock implies a key. This is the chapter Act 3 turns on: release, draw, seal deeper, or ask the souls what they want — the holocron makes all four possible and forces none. Never point a sign at it; if the crew refuses the working, the refusal is the ending.
5. **The Seventh Campaign** — cut off mid-entry. The muster order; the contingency ("if my thread is cut, call the gifted, and the Brotherhood rises from here" — the Last Muster, below); the last confident sentences of a man who does not know he has already lost. The record ends before Ruusan does.

**Where it is.** Not where the catalogue says. Kaan trusted his Brotherhood and none of it: before the seventh campaign he moved his record to the muster cache (the Last Muster, below), off every inventory the principalities kept. The line's Jutrand guess is wrong — Jutrand is a magnificent red herring the Scrub will strip anyway — and the only working directions in the galaxy are a sound only the untrained can hear.

**The Gatekeeper.** Kaan's simulacrum, recorded at the Brotherhood's height — the most dangerous kind of dead man, a charismatic one. He does not know the war ended, or how. He is warm, collegial, magnetic, and recruiting: every visitor is "Brother" or "Sister," every lesson bends toward the muster, and the friendliness that emptied academies works exactly as well on a lonely gifted spacer as it did on the war's cadets. He trades: chapters 1 and 2 for civility; chapter 3 for *news* — he is starving for word of a war he refuses to believe is over, and true accounts of the present are the only coin he values. Chapter 4 he opens to a sworn Brother (**5 Conflict**, and a standing problem: he now issues orders) or to a **Daunting Discipline or Deception check** from someone who can hold his gaze and lie about the century. Chapter 5 he shows freely to exactly one kind of visitor: one who has **heard the call** — he knows his own working's mark, and the crew's Dathomiri walks in already cleared. And the Rule of Two is, to him, the dissenter's madness in a second generation: **the Banite line is the one lineage in the galaxy he will never teach.** If Veritis ever stands before him, the holocron of Lord Kaan is the only thing on the Run that will call her a traitor to her face. She knows it. That is why the object must be silenced and can never be used.

**At the table.** The holocron is a **Wellspring** (Part VII): a Force-sensitive PC can raise Force Rating or open a power tree from it, at the Gatekeeper's price — never blood, always *enlistment*, paid in Conflict and in favors to a dead cause. It is a **Sith-tagged lore fragment source** of the first order: chapter 4 can close *What is the Valley?* from the inside; chapter 2 bears on *Why did the Run stay dark?* and hands the crew the patron's next moves; chapter 3 is the document that turns the Ladder of Sightings' pattern into a named tradition. And it is the thing every counter is counting toward: the Inheritors want it as proof of lineage, the Vigil wants it locked, Tane wants it on Coruscant, Veritis wants it silenced — and Del Gormo, alone in the campaign, wants nobody to have it. If the Inheritors reach it first, the culling is the night she takes it from them, and the Emptied House is where it was.

### The Last Muster — Kaan's dead man's switch
*Working — the Brotherhood of Darkness (dead) — the grinding call; declared homebrew, like the Scar's Echo*

> Somewhere out along the dead spurs, a patient working of stone and intention has been calling the gifted to a war that ended ninety years ago — in the voice of a prison.

**What it is.** Kaan trusted his Brotherhood and no one in it. Before the seventh campaign he left a contingency in the Brotherhood's rear country: a working — Sith magic bound into principality stonework — keyed to his own thread in the Force, sealed over the cache that holds his record and the Brotherhood's reserve. Its orders were the Brotherhood's whole doctrine in one line: *if my thread is cut, call the gifted, and the Brotherhood rises from here.* The thought bomb cut the thread. The switch fired. It has been calling ever since — for exactly as long as the war has been over, which is a date the players can do arithmetic with.

**Why it grinds.** The working was built to carry its master's summons — Kaan's voice, Kaan's warmth, the recruiting charm that emptied academies. But the thought bomb did not kill its master the way the switch understands death: it *sealed* him, with a thousand others. So the switch reaches for the voice it was built to carry and finds the inside of the thought bomb — and what it broadcasts is not a summons but a relay: a thousand souls, light and dark together, grinding at their prison. Stone on dry stone. The mill turns and there is no grain. **The pull Force-users feel up the Run is this switch** — and what the switch is playing them is the Valley of the Jedi.

**Who hears it.** The muster calls *new blood* — Kaan's recruitment was always everyone, and the working is keyed to the unclaimed and untrained. That is why the Awakening generation drifts up the Run, why Del Gormo's shore keeps filling, why the crew's Dathomiri hears it clearest — and why **the Banite line cannot hear it at all**: Bane burned the Brotherhood's channel out of his doctrine, and his heirs are deaf to their own history's mouth. Veritis knows *something* calls — the Census keeps logging gifted drift with no economic explanation — and cannot hear it herself. If she ever learns one of the crew can walk the call, that character becomes the most valuable instrument on the Run — and the player should get to feel valuable before they learn to whom. One more set of ears: the Vigil has heard it for generations without a name for it. Their catechism calls it **the millstone dream** — *do not follow the grinding; it is the dead asking for what the living cannot give* — which is folk-accurate on every point, and the first real navigational help a gifted PC who asks them will ever get.

**Dial 2 stands.** The switch is not the Awakening's engine, and neither is the Valley: the Force returns because it returns. More births mean more ears — the call is not louder, it is better attended.

**Using the call at the table.** Directionless as weather for most: a mood at the edge of sleep, a sound in dry country. Near a threshold — a Brotherhood site, a principality vault, the cache itself — it has a direction the way a draft has a door. A gifted PC can learn to walk it: Force checks across episodes, sharpened by fragments (each Sith-tagged fragment held adds a boost — the record hums toward its author), and the Dathomiri's "grinding stone on dry stone" should be seeded early and paid off exactly here. **Where:** the cache sits off every catalogue on a dead principality spur — suggested placement **Byllura** (the Dyarchy's world, where for a decade a whole population moved to the will of two telepath children; stonework that remembers obedience is exactly the Brotherhood's kind of irony), but place it where your table's road wants it. **Endgame stakes:** open the cache or break the working and the call stops — silence lands on the sector's gifted like a lost tooth, and the Valley loses its only mouth this side of Ruusan. What the crew does with a switch still grinding, a record still naming Bane, and a road the sky itself is closing — that is Acts 2 and 3.

## The Scar's Echo

### The Scar's Echo
*Encounter hazard — Episode 6, "The Scar" — NOT an NPC*

> A New Sith Wars battlefield where the dark side pooled and never drained: kilometers of fused glass under gray ashfall, an Inheritor dig site at its heart, and something underneath that has learned the shape of a listener.

> **DELIBERATELY HOMEBREW SECTION — the only one in this file.** The Scar's Echo is a dark-side vergence run as terrain, not a creature: it has no characteristics, no wounds, and cannot be fought. Its mechanics are assembled from two RAW subsystems — the fear-check rules (Discipline checks against a set difficulty, F&D core fear guidance) and Morality/Conflict as written — so nothing below requires a book lookup; the assembly, and the two declared overrides on the Adjustments line at the end of this entry, are the homebrew. The "Encounter hazard" typeline above is a sanctioned exception to the template's Nemesis/Rival/Minion types: this is the file's one declared non-NPC entry.

**Zones and fear checks.** Call for a fear check (Discipline) when a character first enters each zone, and again if the Echo does something overt (a voice using a dead parent's cadence, a glass field flexing like breath). Per the RAW consequence pattern: on a failure the character suffers 2 strain and adds a setback die to all checks for the remainder of the encounter; three or more threat add 2 further strain; a despair means panic — the character is staggered until the end of their next turn or must immediately move away from the heart. A character who succeeds cannot be forced to re-check in the same zone the same day.

| Zone | Fear check | Ambient effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Ashfall Rim** (the crash-ring and Inheritor base camp) | Hard Discipline | Ash-blind: add a setback die to Perception and Vigilance checks; ranged attacks beyond short range upgrade their difficulty once. |
| **The Glass Fields** (fused, uneven, wrong underfoot) | Hard Discipline; Force-sensitive characters upgrade the difficulty once | Difficult terrain throughout. Each hour spent here: Average Discipline check or suffer 1 strain (the whisper accumulates; this strain cannot be recovered until the character leaves the Scar). |
| **The Choir** (the heart; the dig itself) | Daunting Discipline; Force-sensitive characters upgrade the difficulty once | Both outer effects apply. All checks to recover strain here automatically fail. Sleeping in the Choir: 1 Conflict, and a true-but-poisoned dream. |

**Conflict pressure (RAW Conflict, tempted).** The Echo's offer is generosity. While anywhere within the Scar, a Force-sensitive character may use dark side results on Force power checks *without flipping a Destiny Point* — the strain cost per dark point still applies as written, and each dark point used this way awards Conflict as RAW **plus 1 additional Conflict** (the vergence's finder's fee). Additionally, award 1 Conflict for: sleeping in the Choir; deliberately opening oneself to the Echo's visions; or taking payment from the dig knowing what is being dug. Land the aftermath hard (Part VII): there are no counselors in this era, and the first person to explain the nightmares wins — make sure the players notice who is offering.

**The visions.** Seek, Sense, Foresee, and Farsight checks made within the Scar add both a boost die *and* a setback die, and what they return is true but curated — the Echo shows real fragments (it qualifies as a **Sith-tagged lore fragment source** for the Reconstruction subsystem) selected to make the viewer feel indispensable. A despair on any such check puts a corrupted conclusion in play that the character believes (mirror of the Reconstruction rules).

**The bargain (Wellspring).** The Scar is a functional vergence, and therefore a legal Wellspring (Part VII): a Force-sensitive character *can* raise Force Rating or open a new power tree here. Any advance bought with the Scar as its Wellspring carries a rider: the first time that power or rating is used each session, the character gains 1 Conflict. This is the campaign's first real temptation, and it should work — the offer is genuine, the training is real, and the price is on layaway.

**Running it.** The Echo has no agenda a stat block could hold; it wants *listeners*, and it prefers the young. The Inheritor dig crew (coin, answers, courtesy — first contact is always helpful, per Dial 3) treats the whisper as an occupational perk and is quietly further gone than they know. Never bring the crew's gifted here without intending the campaign to pivot on it: they dream of the Valley, which is loud, but the Scar is quiet and hungry, and it would very much like to be their second teacher.

**Chassis:** original build (encounter hazard — no adversary stat block exists or is owed; assembled from the F&D fear-check guidance and RAW Morality/Conflict). **Adjustments:** the +1 Conflict finder's-fee and the no-Destiny-flip dark-pip rule are the only overrides of RAW, both declared above.
