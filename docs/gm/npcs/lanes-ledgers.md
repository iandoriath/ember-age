# Lamplighters, Kajidics, the Guild & the Clans

Ninety years after Ruusan, the powers in this file are the ones the Withering could not kill: the guild that kept the beacons breathing, the Hutt clan-corporations that got rich on the dark, the one writ that still travels between systems, and the one people who never demobilized. None of them wants the old Republic back — but every one of them intends to be paid, chartered, or armed when whatever comes next arrives. The party will work for all four before they ever fight one.

**GM eyes only — this file carries Act 2–3 leverage (the Conclave of Charts, the kajidic succession) and one Dial 3 thread. Do not hand to players.**

- **Talents:** Adversary 1 (*upgrade the difficulty of all combat checks targeting her once*), Master Pilot (*once per round while piloting a starship, she may suffer 2 strain to perform any action as a maneuver*), Skilled Jockey 2 (*remove two setback dice from all Piloting checks*)
- **Abilities:** none
- **Equipment:** Heirloom heavy blaster pistol (Ranged [Light]; Damage 7; Critical 3; Range [Medium]; Stun setting), chartmaster's armored greatcoat (+1 soak, +1 defense), the *Meridian Candle* — her personal pre-Ruusan lane-runner (use the YT-2400 light freighter profile as printed in the Edge of the Empire Core Rulebook, p. 265).

**Chassis:** Smuggler Baron, Edge of the Empire Core Rulebook (adjustments: none).
**Using her:** Zel was the finest lane-runner of her generation before she took the master's desk, and she still certifies contested first-lightings from the *Candle*'s cockpit — which is why the pilot's talents live under the guildmaster's robes. Her neutrality is real and priced accordingly: she cannot be bought with scrip, only with permanence — the guild written into whatever charter, empire, or concord the Founding produces. She knows the relighting order decides which worlds matter for the next century, every faction knows she knows, and at the Conclave of Charts she will run the auction like an astrogation problem. Dial 3 threads: she has begun to suspect her own registry leaks — she does not yet know the mole answers to the Inheritors, and how she reacts when the party proves it is worth an entire session. What she has not begun to suspect is the money: the guild's coreward patron, whose relighting sequence she enforces as guild neutrality and whose survey teams she waves through, is a Sith front (see [GM Truths](../truths.md), the Patronage). If the party ever proves *that*, it is the end of her neutrality and possibly of her guild — play it as the worst day of her life.

- **Talents:** Convincing Demeanor 2 (*remove two setback dice from Deception or Skulduggery checks*), Durable 3 (*subtract 30 from Critical Injury results against him*), Nobody's Fool 3 (*upgrade the difficulty of Charm, Coercion, and Deception checks targeting him three times*), Resolve 2 (*whenever he suffers strain, reduce it by 2, to a minimum of 1*)
- **Abilities:** Awkward (*his bulk adds three setback dice to all Brawl, Melee, and Coordination checks he must make*), Ponderous (*he may never spend more than one maneuver moving per turn*)
- **Equipment:** none carried — the printed block's weapons (a Hutt-scale large-bore blaster pistol and a vibro-ax) are explicitly optional in print, and Sorulba goes unarmed; his enforcers, his ledgers, and time itself are the arsenal.

**Using him:** Sorulba's kajidic holds the fuel and scrap monopolies across three withered sectors, and his answer to reunification is *no, thank you* — every relit beacon on a lane he does not own is competition. Never stage him as a combat; stage him as terrain. He negotiates from total safety, pays promptly, keeps his word to the letter, and prices the letter — and because he personally remembers the Republic failing to pay its debts, he can contradict any human myth about the old days with an eyewitness sneer. His real vulnerability is at his own table: Jeeda's spreadsheet heresy, one bad season from a clan war.

He never draws first, always warns once, and with Cool 5 and Vigilance 5 he has never in sixty years been surprised by the person he warned. If the party wants a posting voided, the code allows exactly one path: satisfy the bond — and watching them try tells him who they are.

In a fight she is a Field Commander first — her soldiers act twice as often as anyone expects — and she withdraws the moment a contract stops paying for casualties. The way to move her is never ideology; it is the fine print, or a foundling in danger.

The witness rule almost never triggers around her because she never *performs* the Force — she just doesn't miss — which makes her invisible to the Vigil, the Inheritors, and the census-takers until the day it matters. Every faction that meets her wants her for something; run the party's dawning realization that she is the one gifted child in the galaxy who is already fine.
