# Session Three — The Drowned Resort

*The GM-screen module for hop 2. Run the session from this page, one scene at a time — every name is introduced and every number stated where you need it; marked names open stat blocks. **Branch-safe**: no particular Enarc outcome is assumed — collect what your table did in the carry-forward table and run everything else as written. This is Act 1's* physical *session (weather, water, climbing, a leviathan) — and its climax is not the beacon. It is what the beacon does to a grave.*

## What you need to know (even if you read nothing else)

**Where we are.** Hop 2 of the dark Run: **Aquilaris**, a Legends ocean world — resort seas over dead pleasure piers, flat land, no mountains, one hill. It was a Republic resort and seafood exporter until the Sith took it in 1042 BBY; it was drowned twice (a Sith massacre that threw a city into the sea, then a Hutt's storm-ships that flooded the whole landmass in 1032 BBY — see [The Dead Lords](../npcs/knight-errant.md)); and it was, for a while, the sector's refugee road: the **Seacroppers' Guild** ran survivors out through **underwater harvester stations** nobody else knew existed. Ninety years of the Withering did not drown it again. It did something quieter: the resort promenades sank, the harvester stations went dark one by one, and the people who stayed learned to live on the water and speak well of the hill. The guild's prize for Aquilaris posted the hour Enarc's certificate cleared — **6,000 scrip** — and the beacon mast is the old resort's **Grand Pier tower**, half a kilometer out in the bay, its top third above water and its machinery below.

**The twist of the night: the grave.** The highest point on the planet is **Tarrah Hill** — canonically "even pregnable to flooding" — and on it stands a stone with four words and no dates. It is the grave of **Kerra Holt**, the Jedi Knight who stayed in this sector 120 years ago and won, and her sister **Marenna**; the old four-armed keeper who lives on the hill and whom everyone speaks well of and never names is **@@npc:Master N'Kata Del Gormo@@**, her student. He has tolerated the Vigil's tape on this world's dead for ninety years because a tended grave is a safe one. **The relight ends that**: a lit corridor means prospectors, prospectors mean the wardens stop *tending* the twice-drowned dead and start *sealing* them — a manifest, not a tape — and the hill is next on the round. The crew will light the beacon, and the beacon will bring the Vigil to the stone — and the old keeper will *not* say what he has not said in ninety years: the crew will have to, or nobody will. **That is the climax; everything before it is the fuse.** Full scene: *The Hill on Aquilaris*, in [The Dead Lords](../npcs/knight-errant.md). Play the session so the players like him before they know him.

**The cast, one line each** *(stats in brackets)*:

## Scene 1 — The Water

**The jump.** Enarc to Aquilaris is a dead leg on a patron's timetable: **Hard Astrogation** (Osk's margins or rutter boost). Reversion in the outer system shows what the charts promised — a blue world — and then, closer, what they did not: no coastlines where the old atlas draws them. The land is a scatter of islands, and the islands are the tops of things.

**The approach.** No pickets, no court, no challenge — after Enarc, the silence is the point. The beacon mast reads cold; the old resort's **Grand Pier tower** stands half a kilometer out in the bay with its upper third in the air and running lights that have not run in sixty years. Beneath it, in clear water, the **promenades**: a drowned city of glass-roofed walks and pleasure piers, coral on the balustrades, the shadows of things moving under the glass. Give the players the bay from above and let them see the *shape* of the sunken resort before anyone speaks. Then the guild boats come out — a dozen submersibles surfacing in a ring around the ship like curious animals — and a woman on the lead hull calls up the only question this world asks strangers: *"Are you here for the light, or for the water?"*

**Landing.** There is one spaceport pad on **Capital Cay**, the highest island — a Seacroppers' town built on the roofs of a Republic-era port, three stories of it under the tide-line. **Guildmother Padgett Kreel** meets them on the pad. She wants the beacon lit (the guild has read the prize notice; 6,000 scrip is more than the whole Cay earns in a decade) and she is frightened of it, and she will say both in the same breath: *"Light it. Then tell me what comes."* The port is open; fuel is water-cracked hydrogen, cheap and slow; the guild's price is *help* — a hand on the nets, a look at the pumps, a story from outside.

**And on the ridge above the town**, on the one hill the sea never took, a figure with a staff is sitting in the evening light, watching the ship come in. Padgett, if asked: *"Old Gormo. He's on the hill. He's always been on the hill."* Nothing more — she means nothing more.

## Scene 2 — Capital Cay

**The town, seen walking.** Roofs for streets; ladders for stairs; nets drying on Republic signage; a chapel that was a customs house. Every third building has a **Vigil tape** across a door — grey, scripted, undisturbed — because this world's dead outnumber its living and the wardens have tended them for eighty years. The Seacroppers step around the tape without looking at it, the way you step around a neighbor's grave.

**Three things to do here, in any order** — each earns the town's trust and plants the fuse:

- **The pumps.** Capital Cay lives by three Republic-era pumps that keep the lower stories dry, and one is failing: **Average Mechanics** to diagnose, **Hard Mechanics** to fix with what the guild has, or **Average Negotiation** to talk the guild into cannibalizing a submersible for parts (Padgett hates it; she will do it). A fixed pump is the town's gratitude, and this is Mikrés's world — a Chadra-Fan who cannot swim being the only one who can keep the water out is the session's first honest hero-moment; let it be dry-land engineering, no diving, and let the town notice.
- **The elders' porch.** The old Seacroppers remember what their grandparents told them: a Republic charity that came with food and left with the town *addicted* — Grace Command, and the spice called Deluge — and the Jedi girl from this very town who dumped it in the sea. Nobody remembers her name. They remember *the crazy one who stole the submersible.* This is the verdict Question's first honest evidence, and it is the Republic's side of the ledger; if the Trooper is at that table, let the elders ask him, kindly, whose uniform that is.
- **The chandler on the roofs.** Between the pumps and the porch, a folding counter on a rooftop — *Sund & Daughter* — a young woman buying pump line and lamp cells off the drowned promenades from Seacropper divers, refurbishing them on the spot. A Seacropper mutters that the chandler girl *never rounds*. One line; say nothing. *(GM: Odri Sund — [Keepers](../npcs/keepers.md); note where the counter stood.)*
- **The eel-smoker on the hill road.** Old Gormo, selling smoked eel from a rack at the foot of the hill; four arms working, staff propped against the rack. He is *funny* — jokes about the tide, a riddle about which end of a net is the important one, a wrong answer about the beacon that turns out later to be exactly right. He asks the crew nothing. If a gifted PC stands near him, run the hearth-sense as *quiet* — the first place on the Run the grinding does not reach, as if something on this hill were louder than it. Do not explain it. Sell them the eel.

## Scene 3 — The Sounding

**The situation.** Nobody has been *inside* the Grand Pier tower in sixty years, and the beacon's machinery is below the waterline. Before anyone can light it, someone has to go down and look. Padgett offers a submersible and a pilot — **her son Joad**, sixteen, named for the boy who ran his father's submersible into the fjords in 1042 — on one condition: the crew takes a Seacropper's word about the water. *"You go at slack tide. You do not go at dusk. Nothing on the water at dusk."*

**The dive** — the session's first physical set piece, run as a heist through the drowned promenades:

| Leg | The water | The check |
|---|---|---|
| **The glass walks** | Down through the roofed promenades: coral, silt, the resort's last furniture, sixty years of the Deep's leavings | **Average Piloting (Planetary)** for the submersible; a **Perception** passenger spots the way the coral grows *away* from one channel — the Deep's road |
| **The pier root** | The tower's foot, where the beacon's capacitor stacks sit in a flooded gallery — machinery still sealed behind a Republic hatch | **Hard Mechanics or Skulduggery** to open a sixty-year hatch from a submersible's manipulators; **Hard Athletics** if someone swims it (2 setback: pressure, cold) |
| **The gallery** | Inside: dry pockets, drowned pockets, the capacitor stacks intact — and a **harvester-station relay** the Seacroppers wired into the beacon's power sixty years ago, still hot | **Average Computers** to read the relay: the beacon was never *dead* — it was *diverted*, feeding the harvester stations' life support for decades after the Withering. The Seacroppers kept the refugee road alive on the beacon's own power. |

**The Deep, first sighting.** On the way up, the water *flexes* — a wake the size of a street, a shadow under the glass walks, then nothing. Joad does not speak until they surface. Then: *"That's why we don't go at dusk."* No roll; it is weather. (@@npc:The Aquilaris Deep@@: hazard rules — roll the water, never the teeth.)

**The bill.** Relighting the mast means taking the beacon's power *back* from whatever the harvester stations still run on it. Padgett knows. She does not say it until they surface: *"Light it, and the stations go dark. We can live on the roofs. We have. Just — know that's the price."* Let the players sit with it. It is the whole verdict Question in a sentence.

## Scene 4 — The Hill

**Go up before the relight.** Something should draw them — Padgett sends the crew to ask "the old fellow" about the tides (he knows them to the minute); or the gifted PC follows the quiet; or they simply climb the only hill on the world to look at the bay. Tarrah Hill is grass and wind, a rack of drying nets, a hut, and at the top, **a stone**:

Marenna
Kerra
Sisters</pre>

No dates. No titles. No Vigil tape — the only untaped grave on Aquilaris — and the old eel-smoker sitting beside it, mending a net, who says, without looking up: *"They're not for sale. The eel is."*

**Run him as Star Wars, not a seminar.** He is a hermit: odd, funny, dangerous underneath, and playing dumb — jokes about the tide, a riddle about which end of a net matters, a wrong answer about the beacon that turns out later to be exactly right. He feeds them. He does not explain anything, ever. If they ask whose grave it is, he points at the stone with his staff: *it says.* If they ask who Kerra was: *"Ask her."* If they push: he goes back to the net. Nothing the players learn on this hill comes out of his mouth — it comes off the stone, off the warden's slate later, or off a **Hard Knowledge (Lore)** roll (the name *Kerra Holt* is in the Republic's Dark Age histories: a Jedi Knight who went into Sith space and did not come back — let that land in silence). The Force-sensitive should feel it for certain: the hill is the one silent place on the Run, and the silence has a shape.

**The tide table.** He gives them the relight's window without being asked: slack water at the Grand Pier, tomorrow, one hour before dusk. *"After that the water belongs to something else. Don't be on it."*

## Scene 5 — The Second Relight

**Why this scene is the session's engine, not its climax.** Vesta-9 lit itself; Enarc was the first beacon the crew lit with their hands. Aquilaris is the first they light knowing the *price* — and it is the physical set piece Act 1 promised: a mast half in the sea, a tide with a deadline, and a leviathan with a schedule. ## Scene 6 — The Tape Becomes a Manifest

**The next morning, the Vigil.** The chapter's circuit ship is on the pad at dawn — it always comes to a relight — and **Warden Ossa Tull** (or @@npc:Keeper Adressa Vhail@@) walks Capital Cay with a slate and a roll of tape, and something new: **a manifest**. The guild's prize notice has gone sector-wide; prospectors are a week behind; and on a world where the dead outnumber the living, the wardens have orders to convert every tended site to *custody* before the rush — relics catalogued, doors sealed under the deed, the necrology entered. She is correct, tired, and honest, and she explains it to the crew as the first outsiders who will understand: *"Yesterday we tended. Today we seal. Tomorrow they dig."*

**Let the players feel the change.** The tape the Seacroppers stepped around for eighty years now has a warden standing beside it reading what is behind each door; three houses on the Cay are opened, their war dead catalogued, closed under seal. The town does not object — the Vigil are the grey veils, harmless, and the necrology is kind. And the crew watches the round move, door by door, up the hill road.

**The warden's question.** Tull has the necrology on her slate — two names for Tarrah Hill, *Holt, Marenna* and *Holt, Kerra*, entered as war dead a generation ago by a warden who never climbed the hill — and one entry with no tape recorded against it. She asks the crew, because they have been up there: *"Is there a marker? Is there anything in the ground?"* This is the crew's first choice in the climax, and it comes before the climax: **what they tell her decides how she arrives at the hill.** A lie buys the old keeper a day. The truth brings her up with the manifest open.

## Scene 7 — The Hill on Aquilaris

**The climax.** The full scene — the claims, the stone, the saber's three roads, the recorder, and what travels — is written once, in [The Dead Lords](../npcs/knight-errant.md) (*Set piece — The Hill on Aquilaris*); run it from there. What this page adds is the *staging*, keyed to what the session built — and one rule above all: **the old man says nothing. The players say everything.**

1. **The warden climbs** — with the manifest open or closed, per Scene 6. The keeper is where he always is, beside the stone, mending a net. He does not stand. He does not speak.
2. **The warden asks — and not him.** Tull, reading: *"Holt, Marenna. Holt, Kerra. War dead. No tape recorded."* She looks at the old man; he looks at the net. So she turns to the only people on the hill with standing — the crew — and asks the question the whole session has been loading: *"You've been up here. Is there anything in the ground?"* Then she waits. **This is the scene.** Whatever a player says next is the line of the night; if nobody says anything, she tapes the stone, and he lets her.
3. **If they dig** — with the warden watching and the keeper not moving — they find what is in the hill: the saber, the recorder, the locket, and the second grave. What they do with a dead Jedi's blade in front of a grave-keeper is theirs. Do not resolve it for them.
4. **The arbitrated road** is the one this session was built for: the crew's own judgment, spoken here, is a Reconstruction on the verdict Question in everything but dice. If they hold three Civic fragments and this night (they do — the porch, the relay, the stone), let them roll it here, and let the verdict go on the paper.
5. **If the gifted PC ignites the saber** it is the campaign's first ignition: green, on a hill above a drowned city, in front of a warden of the grave-keepers — and an old man who, for the first time all session, looks up. The witness rule fires like a struck bell. Do not undersell it.
6. **What Tull does** depends on what the crew do. Sealed under deed, the hill becomes the Vigil's; the old man lets it happen, and is gone by morning. Left to him, the chapter files a *dissent* and comes asking, at the next hop, what else the crew saw. Given to the crew, both file it, and the Weeding hears about a Jedi's recorder on a freighter.
7. **The one line he keeps.** After — after the tape or the dissent, after the saber is lit or left — he finds whoever spoke, and asks: *"Why did you?"* What he does next follows from the answer.
