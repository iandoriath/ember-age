# GM Truths (Locked)

!!! danger "Players never see this page"
    The campaign's spine, on one screen — three locked truths. The *working* detail lives where you actually run it: the Sith line's full machinery and the holocron in [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md); the culling and the Emptied House set piece in [Vigil & Inheritors](npcs/vigil-inheritors.md); the Ladder of Sightings in [Faction Clocks](tools/faction-clocks.md); the Reconstruction truth ladders in the [Fragment Tracker](tools/fragment-tracker.md); the two Jedi in [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md).

## Truth 1 — Mendac is near, and almost invisible

A Sith Master and apprentice continue Bane's work — **Darth Veritis** and **Darth Mendac**, patient, hidden, interested in ruins only for what they remember. The Master is far away; the apprentice is *here* — a sector ahead of the advance, a whisper behind it, on the Run the whole campaign long — and the players will never see her unless they earn it. Their strategy in one line: *every true thing about the Sith, out of the galaxy and into her hands* — the genuine removed, the fakes left on the shelves, so that when anyone goes looking for what the Sith were, they find only what Veritis has left for them. The Run was Sith country in the wars and the Withering sealed its vaults; reunification is their harvest, and the party are their unwitting gardeners. Through the early acts, run Mendac as a **near absence** — fresh footprints, a warm cradle, a kind stranger nobody can describe; pattern and aftermath, never presence — but the absence is doctrine, not plot armor: the players can break it with filed evidence (the Ladder of Sightings), and an earned Act 3 confrontation, up to and including a duel, is on the table. Everything operational — the Patronage, the Weeding, the Scrub, the Census, the culling — is in [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md).

The campaign's Jedi question is not extinction but *what a Jedi is for* — the Temple's service (Master Seldra Tane, Act 2) or the hill's (N'Kata Del Gormo, on Aquilaris in Act 1, keeper of Kerra Holt's grave; the Verdanth swamp is where he goes later) — with the withdrawn lineages dead and only their objects left. Both Jedi, the Rade legacy, and the three-Masters codicil are in [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md).

## Truth 2 — The vergence at Ruusan

The Force ebbs and flows — it always has — but the last war did something to it. The thought bomb sealed a thousand souls, light and dark together, in one place, and a thousand souls bound in one place is a **vergence**: a knot in the Force with the mass of an army, and for ninety years it has been *pulling* — drawing the Force toward itself and away from the galaxy, so that the gift stopped manifesting normally. **The Ebb was the vergence feeding. The Awakening is the vergence leaking.** Nobody in the galaxy knows this (the Vigil's theology, the Inheritors' doctrine, and the Republic's science are all wrong), and the players learn it the way the campaign learns everything — in rungs, by testimony, up the truth ladder for *What died at Ruusan?* The Force-sensitive dream of Ruusan the way compasses find north: it is the heaviest thing in the Force, and everything gifted leans toward it.

The grinding pull the gifted feel up the Run is **the Last Muster** — Kaan's dead man's switch, firing since the day he died, reaching for its master's voice and finding the inside of the thought bomb instead ([Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md)). It is the vergence's only mouth this side of the galaxy: the souls grinding at their prison, relayed. More births mean more ears; the call has never gotten louder — the *leak* has.

**The endgame is the vergence.** Act 3's real question is what to do with a knot of a thousand souls that has been holding the Force down for a century — release it (and let the Force flood back, into a galaxy with no Order to meet it), draw on it (an empire's engine, or a republic's), seal it deeper (another age of Ebb, and a peace bought with the dead), or ask the souls what *they* want. Every answer changes what the Force is for the next thousand years, and the campaign has not decided for the players. Kaan's fourth chapter is the key; the Muster is the lock's sound; the road is closing.

## Truth 3 — The Sith lit Vesta-9, and made the Run go dark

The apprentice lit it — deliberately, per plan, and nothing the Mother would waste a thought on. And the biggest truth here is regional: **the Run withered because the Sith required time for their searches** — the line put the lane's lights out under the Ebb's cover and kept them out, clearing the old principality vaults of every incriminating page of the New Sith Wars before the galaxy came back to read them — and the worst page of all is **the holocron of Lord Kaan**, which names Bane and records the Rule of Two's birth (the catalogue hunts it vault by vault; it actually sits in the Last Muster's cache). The Run reopens now, in her order, because the clearing needed an economy — and the line could not be seen to open it. Three consequences run the whole campaign: **the Lamplighters are the Sith's scouting arm and do not know it** (the patron's money, the patron's relighting order); **an Inheritor observer filed the Vesta-9 census** on the crew; and **the guild leaks twice** (the patron bought the relighting order; the Inheritors' mole, Chartkeeper Denno Pike, stole it — Act 2's Conclave of Charts is rigged from two directions). The full doctrine — the kept night, why the Sith open a country they can already fly (discovery, not travel), the two-phase Scrub, the Hutt immunity, the Osk bedside, the holocron's chapters and Gatekeeper, and the Last Muster — is in [Keepers of the Flame](npcs/keepers.md).

## Marginalia

**A calendar gift:** Yoda's canonical birth falls in 104 AR — fourteen years after session one. He is not a relic of the old Order; he is downstream of your players. Whatever galaxy they build at the Founding is the one he will be born into — and the Order he someday joins is their legacy.

**Canon is your epilogue, not your cage:** Legends guarantees that a Republic and a Jedi Order stand a thousand years from now, and that Bane's line reaches its final two. It says nothing about what the rebuilding cost, what kind of Republic came first, or whose names history kept. If your players' Founding becomes the seed of the thousand years of peace, the record's silence about them is not a plot hole — it is what recoveries do to their founders.
