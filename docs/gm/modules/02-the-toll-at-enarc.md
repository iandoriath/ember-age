## What you need to know (even if you read nothing else)

**Where we are.** The crew is flying the first dead leg past Vesta-9 toward **Enarc** — hop 1 of the dark Duros Space Run. The Lamplighters' guild pays posted cash prizes for finding and relighting dead hyperspace beacons; that advance stalled for five years because Vesta-9 couldn't be found, then Vesta-9 lit itself (Session One), and the guild instantly posted the next prize: **Enarc, 8,000 scrip** — paperwork dated, suspiciously, *before* Vesta-9's certificate cleared. A relight only pays if a guild observer certifies it; the crew likely holds a **witness charter** (250 scrip per certified lighting) from Chartmistress Bel Nerra.

**The twist of the night.** Enarc was never empty. It is a three-lane crossroads — the Duros Space Run, the **Enarc Run** (down which the Naboo Admiralty's fleet lives), and the Hutt **Triellus** ring — and though it went dark like everything else, the Kajidics never stopped flying it. **Sorulba's kajidic** has taxed the junction for forty years from **the Weighbridge**, a toll station built into a pre-Ruusan customs hulk — and the beacon mast on its spine is *maintained, oiled, and deliberately dark*. The session's centerpiece: this is the campaign's **first beacon the crew lights with their own hands** (Vesta-9 lit itself). Make it physical, public, and unforgettable.

## Scene 1 — Arrival: The Scale Reads Your Name

**The jump.** Vesta-9 to Enarc is an uncertified dead leg: **Hard Astrogation** (Osk's page or margins add a boost die). Mid-run the lane crosses the Triellus's ghost: dark hulls on crossing vectors that do not answer hails — Hutt traffic that has never needed a beacon. No combat unless the crew forces it; the lesson is quieter: *the dark is not empty, and never was.*

**The hail.** The beacon mast reads cold on every band — then the Weighbridge lights them up with targeting sensors older than anyone aboard:

> "Enarc anchorage. Declare cargo and account. Berth is two hundred scrip, five percent of declared value, fuel at posted rates. Or come about and go back where you came from. The scale does not care which."

**Then the scale reads their name.** Hutt debt consolidates — paper bought and sold up the chain until it pools where the traffic is — and the crew's Bannistar paper got here before they did. Scale-Master Vopp reads it into the public record, flat as a manifest: Grell's signed percentage if it exists, the Kajidic lender's advance with interest, the dues-book entries, all of it, one number. *(If the table's bookkeeping is fuzzy, call the consolidated figure 700 scrip. If the crew somehow owes nothing, Vopp announces "NO ACCOUNT" — and heads turn, because clean crews are rare, and interesting, and about to be offered money.)* This is the session's stakes made personal in the first five minutes: every court tonight knows the crew's number, and three of them will offer to do something about it — Jeeda to restructure it, Vale to buy it (*"the fleet settles its friends' accounts"* — recruitment by refinancing), Skell to trade for it. Their debt is now a token on the table, and they are standing on the table.

**It seeds:** pay the berth toll, talk a waiver (**Hard Negotiation** — Vopp has heard everything), or turn around and end the session. Whatever they choose, they walk in already knowing the Weighbridge's first truth: everything here is weighed, including them.

## Scene 2 — The Weighbridge & the Cantina

**The station.** A customs hulk with a town grown through it: fuel bowsers like temple columns, a bazaar of black-lane goods, gun tugs on lazy patrol — and the beacon mast rising off the station's spine, polished, maintained, dark. Give the players a beat to notice: sixty dark years, and *this* beacon has no rust on it. Sorulba knows an asset when he owns one. The light is worth more unlit — until tonight.

**The three courts, in brief** *(each gets its scene below; this is the shape)*: Sorulba wants the beacon dark and will pay the crew **2,000 scrip and a black-lane rutter** to certify the corridor "unnavigable" or simply leave. Jeeda wants it lit with the certificate naming the kajidic **port authority of record** — fees legal and posted — and offers fuel at cost, clan protection, "a point of the port, for a year." Vale wants it lit with two clauses on the founding paper — a **naval customs house** and **protected-lane status**: settlers and trade genuinely welcome, *under the fleet's law* — and offers escort, fuel at naval rates, and naval certification. Each is a real offer from someone who keeps their word. The crew's guild paper is the tiebreaker none of them can print.

**The cantina beat** — run it before the court; it braids three threads on-screen with the crew at the center table:

- **The wager settles loudly.** @@npc:Captain Vess Marrow@@ pays or collects on the Vesta-9 race in front of the whole room — a round for the winner's table, toasts, the story retold with the crew in it. However the race ended, the port now knows their faces. *(If the rivalry went cold in Session One, she settles with cold courtesy instead, which the room also notices.)*
- **The letter of marque.** Mid-toast, Purser-Lieutenant Vale approaches — publicly, deliberately — and offers Marrow an **escort commission and naval registry**: the fleet wants its first famous privateer, and the *Larksong* is famous. Warm Marrow asks the crew what they think, and means it; cold Marrow lets them wonder. The crew watches their likable rival being courted by the nascent empire — and understands the same offer is one good deed away from *them*.
- **The corner booth.** @@npc:Captain Ferro Skell@@ watches it all, pricing everyone, and sends the crew a drink they didn't order — paid for, Vopp confirms later, out of an account holding *their* debt.
- **Background texture:** two of Kollen's marines walk the docks checking transponders they have no jurisdiction over. Vale is visibly annoyed at the timing. Nobody stops them. That is the Annexation, rehearsing.

**Vale's quiet question** — after the cantina, once, to the crew alone: *"Your guild posted the Enarc prize before your Vesta-9 paper cleared. Who does your guild bank with?"* The Admiralty noticed the too-fast prize too, and the fleet's investigators are competent, patient, and genuinely useful. **(GM only — the trap is structural:** handing the who-funds-the-guild thread to the Founding cadre arms them with a perfect enemy; "a hidden hand funds the reopening" is rally material whether or not anyone ever says the word *Sith*. The players can hand the fascists their cause and believe they are doing detective work. They are. That is the problem.)

## Scene 3 — The Court of the Anchor

**The situation.** @@npc:Sorulba the Anchor@@ holds court in a throne room he has not left in forty years. Stage him as terrain, never combat: he pays promptly, keeps his word to the letter, prices the letter, and can contradict any human myth about the old Republic with an eyewitness sneer. Before the crew is called, let them watch the machine work on somebody else — two petitioners ahead of them:

- A freighter captain surrenders his ship's papers against a debt, and Vopp files them without looking up. *(The room's lesson: this is what losing here looks like — quiet.)*
- A dockhand pleads for an extension; Sorulba grants it, doubles the interest, and the man *thanks him*. *(The room's other lesson.)*

**The Scales of the Anchor.** Disputes and offers at this court are settled by ceremony: the two sides place their offers on the great customs scales — symbolically, a token for each promise — and the room watches which pan falls. When the crew is called, Sorulba puts his 2,000-scrip walk-away token on his pan and asks, publicly, what they put on theirs. This is theater, and it is also binding: what is said at the Scales, before the room, *is* the negotiation. Let the players actually make their offers out loud — the court format forces participation, and the Hutts are grading the performance as much as the terms.

**The wager for the ledger.** The thing the crew should want from this room: the Weighbridge's ledgers — forty years of every hull the toll stopped, taxed, and fueled — the only independent witness on the Run to the question *Who lit Vesta-9?* Sorulba does not sell it. He *stakes* it. His custom for wayfinders: **dejarik** — holochess — against the house (Vopp plays for him; Sorulba watches), or, if the crew's navigator showed off anywhere on the station, a **jump-knot duel**: navigation puzzles solved head-to-head, the spacer's crossword, best of three. *(If anyone at the table met Tama Osk in Session One, let them feel the echo — the dying pilot solved these blind, faster than they could read.)* Stakes: his **ledger page** against something the crew genuinely fears losing — their rutter page, their debt-paper bought back from the consolidators, or their sworn testimony on his side of a future dispute. Mechanics, simple and tense: best of three opposed checks — **Knowledge (Education) or Astrogation vs. 3 difficulty** (Vopp is good), each PC may take one round, Advantage carries between rounds as boost dice, a Triumph wins the match outright, a Despair means the crew's stake is *examined* closely enough that Vopp learns something true about it. Let them lose a round before they win; let the room bet.

**What the ledger page says.** Two standing **"do not hail"** orders live in Sorulba's hand, written years apart, and keeping them distinct is the whole game. **The first is old, priced, and mundane:** the Naboo Admiralty's transits — grey hulls down from the Enarc Run, fuel bought, passage charged at a contract rate, every crossing *logged*; the Weighbridge has never hailed the fleet for the same reason a bank does not frisk its depositors. (A careful reader can date NAS *Steadfast*'s passage toward Vesta-9 in the transit pages — which superficially feeds the port's wrong answer from Session One, *the Admiralty lit it*, and actually refutes it: covert lighters do not pay tolls on the way to the crime.) **The second order is newer, unpriced, and unexplained:** six years ago, Run-lane entries stop — not because traffic stopped, but because the Weighbridge stopped hailing certain hulls, by standing order, after a boarding crew failed to report back and was never found. No contract. No rate. No log. There are ships you do not stop, and they are not the fleet's.

    The first order is commerce; the second is the shadow of the campaign's hidden actors (see [GM Truths](../truths.md), Dial 3 — the short version: a patron secretly steers the guild's relighting order, and its agents fly the dark Run unseen; the lost boarding crew stopped one of *their* hulls once). ## Scene 4 — The First Relight

**Why this scene is the centerpiece.** Vesta-9 lit itself. This is the first beacon the crew ever lights **with their own hands** — the campaign's whole premise, finally on-screen — so do not let it be one die roll. The mast has been oiled for forty years and *fired* for none of them: the capacitor stacks need manual sequencing at **three stations at once**, and the politics do not stop screaming while it happens.

**Getting leave (or not).** With a court's blessing — Jeeda's, Vale's, or a Scales bargain — the crew works openly, with power and dock access, in front of the whole Weighbridge. Without leave it is an infiltration of a Hutt station whose only guarded thing is the mast: vents, borrowed coveralls, a distraction in the cantina — build it from the party's ideas, and remember @@npc:Kajidic Enforcers@@ have Vigilance and Perception as group skills; the smart way past kajidic muscle has always been the ledger, not the knife.

**The three stations** — one or more PCs each; everyone gets their hands on the beacon:

| Station | Where | The work | On a Despair |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The mast head** | Two hundred meters up the spine, EVA, gun tugs sweeping | **Hard Athletics or Coordination** to make the climb, then **Average Mechanics** to seat the igniter by hand | The tool drifts. Somebody improvises, or somebody goes and gets it — narrate the long reach over nothing. |
| **The core room** | The old beacon heart, inboard | **Average Computers** to run the guild handshake (their charter papers carry the codes; Osk's margins carry older ones), then **Hard Mechanics** to sequence capacitors that have never once fired | Arc-flash: 4 strain, and the mast tries to fire *early*, half-sequenced — see below. |
| **The feed junction** | Down among the fuel bowsers, family territory | **Average Skulduggery or Mechanics** to re-route station power without tripping the family's alarms — or, with leave, **Average Negotiation** with the gantry boss, who wants a bribe and an autograph | The family grid *notices*: enforcers arrive to watch, arms folded, and every later check here is upgraded once. |

Run it as two or three interleaved rounds — cut between stations like a heist. PCs without a station support: the captain on comms (**Average Leadership** — hand a boost die to any one station per round), a lookout (**Average Perception/Vigilance** — downgrade one incoming complication), a face holding off the politics in the corridor (opposed social, see below). The relight completes when all three stations have succeeded.

| 3 Threat | Lieutenant Kollen arrives at the core room "to assist" — his presence upgrades the next check there once, and if the crew accepts the help, his signature is on the relight paperwork forever (a poison gift the Founding wing will spend). |

**If the mast fires early** (core-room Despair): the beacon lights *wrong* — a stuttering, off-pitch carrier that every nav board on the station hears as a wounded animal — and someone must re-seat the sequence **live**, at the mast head, while it cycles. One more **Hard Mechanics** with the mast humming under their gloves. Let it be scary; do not let it kill anyone.

**The firing.** When the last station goes green, read it slowly:

> The charge runs up the spine like something remembering how to breathe, and the mast catches — light rolling joint by joint to the head, sixty years of dark peeling off the hull plates. Below you the whole Weighbridge has stopped: dockhands, enforcers, two Hutts, a purser in navy grey, every upturned face lit the same color. And on every band, in the oldest voice in the sky, Enarc says its own name to the galaxy — because you lit it. First light anyone here ever gave *back*.

Then let the silence break into forty years of tally-marks worth of noise, and cut to the paper.

**Certification — the "Speak to it" stage.** @@npc:Chartmistress Bel Nerra@@ arrives a day behind the crew — in time for this scene — carrying the guild's certification packet and the Enarc prize paperwork *(dated before Vesta-9's certificate cleared; anyone who reads closely sees the advance was never resumed, it was **waiting** — say nothing, let a player find the date)*. The check to certify is **Average Negotiation or Charm**, upgraded once for her table manner — she *wants* to sign; the fight is over **whose clauses go on the founding paper of a three-lane junction**: Jeeda's *port authority of record*, Vale's *customs house + protected lane*, the guild's *charting authority* (if that fine print exists from Session One), and the crew's names as witnesses — again. The certificate is read for a century. Everyone in the room knows it.

## The Out

**Doors onward:**

- **Aquilaris, hop 2** — drowned resort world, first of the deep-Run stops: dead pleasure piers under warm seas. The next prize posts immediately. *Of course it does.*
- **The clan argument travels.** Whichever Hutt lost tonight writes letters; expect the winner's rivals — or the loser's collectors — at the crew's next port.
- **If the Admiralty finding exists** (Session One): the tender arrives at Enarc on schedule, and the junction becomes the Annexation's first test case with the crew's names in its founding file.
- **Skell keeps the ledger he lives by.** Whatever happened tonight went into it, priced.
- **If the gifted dream recurs** (optional; the era's pull toward the Valley, nothing local): the canyon again, closer — and this time somebody in it is facing the dreamer.

**XP:** 15–20; **+5** for a resolution that used ledgers, paper, or testimony where blasters would have been easier.

**The closing image**, in your own words: the junction lit or dark by the crew's own hand — and on the Weighbridge's oldest wall, under forty years of tally marks, Vopp chalks tonight's entry the way the toll has always recorded history. A hull. A weight. A price. Whatever the galaxy decides Enarc was worth, the Kajidics already wrote it down.
