# The Dead Lords — Knight Errant's Sith and What They Left Behind

*The Grumani sector's last century of the New Sith Wars is the most thoroughly documented Sith country in Legends — John Jackson Miller's* Knight Errant *(comic arcs* Aflame, Deluge, Escape*, 2010–12, and the 2011 novel), all set in **1032 BBY**, 122 years before session one. Its warlords are dead. Their worlds, institutions, machines, and unfinished business are the floor this campaign is built on, and the deeper the crew goes up the Run, the more of it they walk into. This file is the GM's brief: sourced from the Legends wiki (fetched, not remembered — every date and name below is as the record gives it), organized for play.* **GM only.**

!!! danger "The one rule of this file"
    **Inherit, don't rewind.** The 1032 BBY state of these worlds — Chelloa slag, Byllura's Dyarchy broken, the principalities carved up, Odion dead — is a fact of history the crew reads in ruins and testimony. Nothing here is alive to fight. What is alive is what these people *built* and never switched off: cloisters, corruptor forges, a rotation-state, a mind-network's stonework, a corporation, a plague-emptied hub. The campaign's own Sith (Veritis and Mendac, [Keepers of the Flame](keepers.md)) came *after* all of this and have spent ninety years erasing the true record of it — which is exactly why finding it matters.

## The floor: 1066–1000 BBY in one page

- **The Calimondra family.** Vilia Calimondra, Sith matriarch, married her way to an empire in the Grumani sector and bore seven children. In **1066 BBY** she set them the **Charge Matrica**: whoever conquered the most inherited everything. Her daughter Xelian declared war on her brother Chagras; all seven fought; by **c. 1050** only **Chagras** was left, and his **Chagras Hegemony** ruled the sector — while Vilia, alive, kept her own holdings and her own agents.
- **1042 BBY:** the Hegemony reached for the Republic. Odion (Xelian's elder son) sacked the University of Sanbra and massacred Aquilaris under cover of hunting the Jedi Vannar Treece — really to seize a research team hunting the **Helm of Ieldis**. **1040 BBY:** Chagras died of a nerve toxin (never solved; Arkadia and Vilia each accuse the other). Vilia declared the **Second Charge Matrica** among her *grandchildren*, and the Hegemony shattered into principalities: the **Daimanate** (Daiman, capital Darkknell), the **Odionate** (Odion), the **Arkadianate** (Arkadia, capital Calimondretta on Syned), the **Dyarchy** (the twins Dromika and Quillan, Byllura), **the Menagerie** (Malakite, incl. Skarpos), Lioko's and Trevayne's unrecorded holdings — plus the **Bactranate** (Ayanos Bactra, a Quermian "family friend," capital Jutrand) and a **Chagrasi Remnant** of systems that held out against everyone.
- **1032 BBY — the Knight Errant year.** Jedi Master Vannar Treece's Operation Influx dies on **Chelloa**; his student **Kerra Holt** stays in Sith space as a one-woman war. Daiman and Odion turn Chelloa into a magma sea with **kinetic corruptors**. Daiman baits Odion at **Gazzari** with a war-college ship and 1,700 children; Vilia orders the family to liquidate Bactra instead; **Jutrand falls to Odion**, Industrial Heuristics goes to Daiman at the family **Bequest**. Kerra breaks the Dyarchy's mind-network on **Byllura**; Arkadia annexes it, tries to have Vilia assassinated with a nerve-toxin hover chair, and loses her capital's war industry in the **Siege of Calimondretta**. **Zodoh the Hutt** floods **Aquilaris** and **Darkknell** with Stormdrivers and is executed by Daiman. Odion revives **Project Pandemonium**, finds the Helm on **Skarpos**, annihilates three armies with it, and dies at **Vanahame** when General Beld Yulan frees the cloister's children. The Odionate is carved up. Kerra Holt stays in the sector; the record never says what became of her.
- **1010–1000 BBY:** Kaan's Brotherhood of Darkness ends the Sith civil war and absorbs the warlords (the principalities are "the Brotherhood's inheritance" — Keepers). A **Battle of Enarc**, won by the Sith, is fought somewhere between 1004 BBY and Ruusan. Then the thought bomb, the Reformation, and the Withering.
- **Later canon that binds us:** Darkknell, Byllura, Jutrand, Sanrafsix, Nilash III are all *alive* in the Clone Wars (Separatist); Syned hosts a small domed Imperial outpost; Sanrafsix has 10–100 million people by 25 ABY. **Darken, never crater** — the map's rule holds.

## The Lords (GM cards — unstatted by design; they are history, not encounters)

### Lord Daiman, Creator of the Universe
*History — the Daimanate (dead, 1032 BBY floor) — the solipsist on Darkknell; run as inheritance, never as an encounter*

> "I was bored — and so I created the universe." He meant it. Every act in nature was a move for or against him; every other being was a puzzle his own mind had set. His eyes were two colors until he used the dark side, and then both were yellow.

**The man.** Xelian's spoiled younger son (born 1057 BBY). Believed, literally, that he had created reality twenty-five years earlier as a test of himself, that Odion was the "Destroyer" he had made to overcome, and that no one else existed. Tortured with Force lightning, mind-read casually, quartered a mercenary for asking for a fair fight. Cortosis-plated his own statues after a Jedi kept beheading them — and read each beheading as proof that she was *real*.
**The state.** Capital **Darkknell** (city **Xakrea**; fortress the **Sanctum Celestial**, a heptagon, flooded in 1032 — and *canonically a vergence*, per the FaD Core Rulebook); the **Black Fang** testing facility; the **Correctors** (Force-adept internal police in gilded armor and burgundy skirts); the **Encumbered** (what his subjects were called — extensions of his will; the "Faulty Encumbered" were publicly abused as spectacle); every cantina and public facility dismantled because his subjects existed to entertain *him*; statues and holoprojectors of himself on every world; **mobile munitions complexes** (interstellar war forges) feeding a fluid front. Aide: **Uleeta**, a Woostoid he tried to *animate directly* by will and could not. Enslaved Nilash III's telepathic tree-dwelling natives in a munitions plant with a plaza of thirteen statues.
**Fate:** alive at the end of the record (1032), inheriting Industrial Heuristics, Vellas Pavo, and part of the Odionate. Absorbed, presumably, into the Brotherhood. Unrecorded death.

### Lord Odion, the Destroyer
*History — the Odionate (dead, 1032 BBY) — the nihilist who felt every life as pain; run as inheritance*

> Life burned in his Force-sense like a headache that began the day his brother was born. Each death dimmed one light. He built a state to dim them all.

**The man.** Xelian's despised elder son. Hypersensitive to other beings in the Force; sought relief in killing and in **channeling one person's anguish into another's murderous frenzy**. Grievously burned at Chelloa; wore the scars as fuel. Ended reaching for the Helm of Ieldis to kill the galaxy and be alone.
**The state.** No formal ranks; species assigned to tasks (Givin navigate, Verpine engineer, hired Bothans spy). **Novitiates** (Force-sensitive dark acolytes), the **Lightning Guard** (jetpacks, no helmets, suicidal; annihilated on Chelloa), **Thunder Guard**. **Claimers** — Force-sensitive recruiters who hunt, *even in enemy territory*, for people who have embraced death and want to end on their own terms. **The Spike** — a modular mobile factory station that built kinetic corruptors. **The Death Spiral** — a hyperspace-capable siege tower of independently rotating armed rings (Odion's was destroyed at Gazzari; Chagras built *several* remote-controlled ones never accounted for). **Twelve cloisters** — planet-sized orphanages where every child in the realm was imprisoned and broken into workers and warriors; Vanahame's was under a spaceport on an airless moon. Capital/foundry **Jubalene** (millions of slaves). General **Beld Yulan** — an ex-Mandalorian mercenary whose children died of Candorian plague, converted by a Claimer, who turned at the end and led thousands of freed children to the Republic.
**Fate:** dead, Vanahame, 1032 BBY — the Helm overloaded on the children's joy. Territory partitioned (never itemized).

### Arkadia Calimondra
*History — the Arkadianate (1032 BBY floor) — the reasonable Sith; run as inheritance*

> Her city was warm, well-fed, bright-clothed, and rotated every citizen through a new profession before they could become good enough to threaten her.

**The woman.** Chagras's eldest daughter, logical, charismatic, frank — the one Sith Lord who nearly convinced a Jedi and *did* convince a mercenary. Believed a lifetime in one pursuit was obsolescence; smashed a suicided artisan's tool to prove it. Expert in nerve toxins (**Chagras's Blood**, from Synedian algae) — which is why Vilia can plausibly accuse *her* of the patricide.
**The state.** **Calimondretta** on ice-world **Syned** (blue newborn stars; sub-ice seas of algae that are both fuel and food — "without the plants, no civilization"), built into linked ice calderas; the **Promisoriums** (open, well-fed schools that indoctrinate versatility); the **Impound** (her prison, with torture); the **Citizen Guard**; flagship **New Crucible**; a museum; an **occupation-rotation** regime enforced without violence — *the only Sith state in Legends that ran on comfort*. Five hundred of her citizens deserted to the Diligence when Kerra broke out.
**Fate:** alive in 1032, capital damaged, expansion aborted, forces recalled from Byllura. Unrecorded thereafter.

### The Dyarchy — Dromika and Quillan, and Regent Saaj Calician
*History — the Dyarchy of Byllura (fell 1032 BBY) — a world run as one organism; run as inheritance*

> Fourteen-year-old twins who never left their rooms; a whole planet moving to their will through a network of enslaved Celegian minds; a Krevaaki regent who thought he was the author and was the puppet.

**The facts.** Chagras's youngest, conjoined through the Force ("no science or Sith alchemy could separate them"). **Quillan** never spoke, lived entirely in his mind, saw the future in detail, could see "even distant galaxies," and hurt at colors — everyone around him wore dark. **Dromika** was the voice, with Force persuasion strong enough to puppet the regent. Their **Unity**: Byllura's whole population made "almost a single-celled organism" through a **physical network of Celegian relays** — Arkadia's diagnosis: *dependent on a physical linkage, and a linkage can be attacked.* Capital **Hestobyll**, built on Republic-terraced river deltas below a plateau of waterfalls; the twins' quarters, **the Loft**. Calician built a battleship fleet crewed by Celegians as extensions of the twins. Kerra kidnapped Quillan; the network collapsed into a mass uprising; Arkadia's fleet took the world, freed the Celegians, executed Calician.
**Fate:** both twins in Vilia's custody, kept apart, on her secret garden world (Quillan) and "another of Vilia's worlds" (Dromika). Never mentioned again. Byllura survives to the Clone Wars.

### Ayanos Bactra and the Bactranate
*History — the Bactranate (liquidated 1032 BBY) — the Sith who ruled by corporation*

**The facts.** A Quermian "old family friend," not blood. Preferred bureaucracy to armies; held three of the sector's four superconductor concerns; owned **Industrial Heuristics** (arrow logo), which built **arxeums** — mobile war-colleges of nine city-block towers, nine engines, students recruited young and kept for life (units near Alphoresis, Gazzari — destroyed — and Tergamenion). Ruled **Jutrand** (ecumenopolis, hundreds of corporate HQs; razed by stone mites in the Hundred-Year Darkness and rebuilt) and **Sarrassia** (Sarrassian iron; a 2,500-year religious war between the Grumani Hierophants and Spumani Crusaders that he ended by fiat). Kept his bankers; Odion fed three of them to a rancor.
**Fate:** fled "into hiding at a Quermian retirement colony." Death unrecorded.

### Lord Malakite and the Menagerie
*History — the Menagerie (1032 BBY floor) — the embodiment of perversion*

**The facts.** Vilia's grandson; saw himself as *perversion* to Daiman's creation and Odion's destruction. Sith alchemy on an industrial scale: **Mutates** (red-skinned twisted sentients as infantry), **Menagerie dragons**, winged **Night-Soarers**; disdained technology, swarmed it with numbers, ran a large-scale breeding program to replace losses, lured enemies into tar pits and lit them. Held **Skarpos** (stormy, trenched, the **Morbollon Mesa** with a Hierophant temple). Fled the Helm with his life; answered Vilia's call against Odion.
**Fate:** alive 1032. Unrecorded thereafter. *(The Feral Run's beast file has no Sithspawn — deliberately; if the crew ever meets a Mutate's descendant, it is this man's.)*

### Vilia Calimondra, the Matriarch
*History — the Calimondra family (alive 1032 BBY) — the grandmother who ran the war as a family meeting*

> "A family is defined by its shared illusions, as much as by its blood."

**The facts.** Alive, powerful, on a **secret retreat world of gardens** reached by coordinates she sends over secure channels; her own legions (two of Trandoshan slave-warriors given away as a consolation prize); her own seers; a spy network including the Bothan **Narsk Ka'hane** (a head implant for coded orders, a Coruscant-made **Cyricept Mark VI stealth suit** with a hidden tracker). Convened the **Bequest** — the family holo-conference around a pylon that doubled as a star map — to divide dead men's assets. Kept Kerra Holt alive on purpose as "a learning experience" for her grandchildren — *"One day, the Sith will turn upon the Republic — and we again will face the full roster of Jedi Knights. My grandchildren need to at least know how to deal with one."* Her seers sensed Arkadia's plot "through unknown means." Encouraged the family to attack *each other* so no successor could ever rise.
**Fate:** the record ends with her alive and in custody of both Dyarchy twins. **She is the Brotherhood's problem, not ours** — but her *retreat world*, her *archive of the family*, and her *seers* are the best-documented Sith holdings in the sector that nobody in the campaign has yet claimed to have scrubbed.

## The heroes' loose ends (GM hooks the record leaves open)

- **Kerra Holt stayed — and, in this campaign, won.** After Vanahame she remained in the Grumani sector to continue her crusade, and the record never mentions her again; nor does it put a single Calimondra in Kaan's Brotherhood. **Ruling (Ian, 2026-08-28):** the sociopaths never joined anyone — Kerra outlasted the Charge Matrica and ended the dynasty before the Brotherhood swept through, which is why Kaan inherited an *emptied* country whose vaults nobody had read. She fought it out on **Verdanth** — the world of seventeen lords, where a one-Jedi war ends — and taught one swamp-child what she knew (Del Gormo; [Keepers of the Flame](keepers.md)). She found her **sister Marenna** at last — born in captivity after the massacre, cloister-raised and broken — and brought her *home*, to Aquilaris, and could not save her. When Kerra's own time came her student carried her home too, buried the sisters together on the one dry hill their drowned world has — and stayed. He has kept his master's old home ever since. Two names on one stone. She is the root of the Rim's Jedi — a second origin, never the Temple's — and everything she left is on Aquilaris: *The Hill on Aquilaris*, below. **(Ian, 2026-08-28: the grave is on her homeworld — "she deserves it" — and Del Gormo is there, defending it; the Verdanth swamp is where he goes later, in time for a crashed youngling some decades after 104 AR; Yoda is born in 104 AR and a Master by about 204.)**
- **Beld Yulan's transports** carried thousands of freed cloister children to the Republic in 1032 — and the Republic was adjourning the Rim two generations later. Their grandchildren are the Rim's Awakening generation; their family stories are the Civic fragments about the cloisters.
- **Jarrow Rusher's Brigade** (eight battalions named for ancient weapons; troopship *Diligence*, a converted Devaronian liner with detachable cargo-pod clusters; Rusher walked with Arkadia's stolen double-bladed lightsaber as a cane) went back to the Republic to refit. A mercenary tradition on the Run that keeps **Telettoh's maxim** — *no Sith aboard, ever* — is his living legacy, and a natural ancestor for the Mandalorian clans' contracts.
- **Narsk Ka'hane's Mark VI stealth suit** — Coruscant-built, Vilia-modified with a hidden tracker — is the single most valuable *object* in the record with an unknown fate.
- **Jenn Devaad and Grace Command** — see the Republic's sin, below.

## The Republic's sin: Operation Deluge

**The record.** During the Dark Age, Republic Intelligence (Baron Lemayne of Alderaan) ran **Grace Command**, a mercy charity that flew relief into Sith space — as a front for **Operation Deluge**: deliberately distributing an addictive spice that *neutralized desire* to Sith populations, to weaken the warlords' armies without spending Republic ships. It worked: Daiman culled half his own defense force as addicts. It also addicted the Aquilarians who had welcomed Grace Command as liberators. Captain **Jenn Devaad** (Devil Squadron, ex-slave of Zodoh) turned on the operation and began dumping the spice and smuggling refugees out through Aquilaris's underwater harvester stations, which became the sector's refugee hub.
**The wider policy, for the record.** The Dark Age Republic held a defensive cordon of Core and Colonies, deactivated every hyperspace relay beyond it (denying the Sith real-time route data — and its own people any way home), ordered corporations to withdraw behind the line, and officially called the whole war "a series of protracted military engagements against an illegal organization." Jedi served three months of law-enforcement patrol, nine months on the frontier, and sixteen days of free travel — the loophole Treece's volunteer "knight errant" raids lived in, with the Chancellor's secret consent and no regular forces. **The Republic did not send a warship into the Grumani sector in 1032.** It sent a drug.

**Why it matters here.** The Republic's Rim policy has a *precedent* the Senate would rather nobody remembered: it once treated the Rim's people as a chemical weapon. Ninety years on, the verdict Question — *should the Run be reconnected at all?* — has its darkest evidence here: the Republic's side of the atrocity ledger — and the Trooper who kept a suspended garrison's oath for three generations deserves to learn what the writ he kept once did. **Aquilaris (hop 2)** is where the memory lives: the harvester stations, the Seacroppers' Guild, and grandparents who remember being *helped*.

## Set piece — The Hill on Aquilaris (hop 2, first and only pass)

> Tarrah Hill — the one piece of Aquilaris the sea has never quite kept — a stone with four words cut plain, no Vigil script; a warden with a roll of grey tape and a manifest; and an old four-armed eel-smoker who has lived on this hill longer than anyone can remember, sitting beside the stone, saying nothing.

**The stone** (a prop worth printing — the players should read it before anyone explains it):

<pre style="text-align:center;font-family:inherit;letter-spacing:.2em;line-height:1.9">HOLT
Marenna
Kerra
Sisters</pre>

No dates, no titles, no *Jedi*. The old man cut it, a lifetime ago — and never left. The Vigil's necrology entered the two names as war dead a generation ago without knowing what either was, and he let them, because a taped grave is a safe grave and a grave-keeper who does not ask is the best guard a secret ever had. Nobody living has asked whose it is.

**The rule of this scene (Ian, 2026-08-28): the keeper does not speak for her. He cannot.** He is a hermit with no name, no order, no paper, on a world where the Vigil holds the deed — and a Jedi Master with a speech would rob the table of the only thing this scene is for. When the manifest comes up the hill he does not argue and he does not draw. He sits by the stone and says nothing, because ninety years of saying nothing is the only defense he has ever had, and it is about to fail. **The crew are the only people on that hill with standing.** The warden asks *them*. The keeper looks at them. Every line that matters — *whose grave is this, and who does she belong to* — comes out of a player, or nobody says it and the tape goes on. If the crew say nothing, the hill is sealed and the old man lets it happen; the Vigil is not wrong, and he knows it. He is not a voice in the argument. He is what the argument is *about*.

**Why tonight.** The crew relights Aquilaris's beacon (the Run pulls on toward Darkknell; there is no return, so the scene is now or never). A lit corridor means prospectors, and prospectors mean the Vigil stop *tending* and start *sealing*: a world drowned twice is a graveyard end to end, and the wardens take custody of its war dead before the rush — relics catalogued, sites closed, the hill next on the round. Tolerating a tape is one thing; a warden with a manifest asking what is *under* the stone is another. The crew are there for the beacon and walk into it.

**The claims — and who can make them.** The Vigil (Vhail on her circuit, or the chapter's warden) hold the deed: a war grave, two Holts on the necrology, custody of relics by the Council's last act — *veneration without practice*, and this is the most venerable object on the world. Against that there is only the crew: two beacons certified, a grave stood at, names on paper the warden respects. The old man holds everything true — he buried her, she taught him, the practice she taught did not stop — and none of it counts, because he will not say it. Vhail, if she is the warden, is living the crack (keys or children) at one grave: she has read *Marenna* and *Kerra* on her slate for years without asking who they were. Nobody in the scene is wrong.

**How the name gets out.** Never from him. From the **stone** (four words the players read for themselves); from the **warden's slate** (two Holts, war dead, no tape recorded); from a **Hard Knowledge (Lore)** roll (*Kerra Holt* is in the Republic's Dark Age histories — a Jedi Knight who went into Sith space and did not come back); from what is **under the stone** if the crew dig. That he was her student is theirs to work out or not; he never confirms it. If asked directly he says the one thing he says: *"Ask her."*

**What is in the hill** (Threads rows below): Kerra's **green lightsaber** — the campaign's first ignition in a hand that is not a costume's (the Inheritors' red blade across the water is theater); if it ever leaves the grave, it lights in a player's hand or not at all; her **recorder** — the testimony she recorded for Chancellor Genarra and never delivered: the Calimondra family, the Bequest, the vaults, Grace Command (fragments for *Who lit Vesta-9?* and the verdict Question); her parents' **locket**; and **Marenna** — the sister she found too late, buried beside her. Nobody hands the crew any of it. They find it, or they don't.

**The saber's three roads.** *Dug up* — by the crew, with the warden watching and the keeper *not intervening*; what they do with a dead Jedi's blade in front of a grave-keeper is theirs. *Taken* — off the hill without a word, which the Vigil call desecration and the old man calls nothing at all, so the wardens come asking and the Weeding hears. *Arbitrated* — the crew's own judgment, spoken at the hill, is a Reconstruction on the verdict Question in all but dice: the first paper of the campaign that is not paper. Whatever they take is carried up the Run; whatever they leave, the Vigil seal, and the old man sits back down.

**The one line he keeps.** Afterward — after the tape or the dissent, after the saber is lit or left — he finds whoever spoke, and asks a question, not gives an answer: *"Why did you?"* What he does next is keyed to what the crew did, not to the answer (there is no right one): the hill sealed, he is gone by morning, up the lane to the swamp he was born in; the hill left to its keeper, he stays; the saber in a player's hand and any answer given at all, he offers, without ceremony, to show the gifted PC how she held a blade, whichever way he is bound afterward. He is a Wellspring the crew can choose to come back to, not a voice that outranks theirs.

**Consequences travel, not the grave.** If a Jedi's recorder walks off Aquilaris, the true Sith line wants it more than anyone alive — it is the record they exist to erase — and the Weeding meets the crew *ahead*, at the next hop, never behind. A saber that leaves in the Dathomiri's hand writes her lineage in one stroke and makes the Temple's *too old to train* absurd in public. A crew that sides with the Vigil hands the grave-keepers a Jedi to keep — and the keys-or-children crack its cleanest example: they have been reading a cloister child's name — Marenna's — for ninety years.

## Where the thought bomb was engineered

**The canon chain, untouched.** Legends is firm on the ritual: it descends from Vitiate's Ritual of Nathema; **Revan** transcribed it into his holocron; **Bane** found the holocron on Lehon in 1000 BBY and "used the transcription as inspiration for the actual thought bomb"; he fed the working to Kaan and lied that the Brotherhood would survive it. Many Sith Lords in a circle, hands joined, chanting into one consciousness; a mental *shell* — the record calls it "the first construct of the bomb" — into which the dark energy is channeled; a clap to detonate; every Force-sensitive in radius torn into a silver ovoid of perpetual torment; a Force nexus at ground zero. Three Dark Jedi died making it and others went mad; to attempt it "was widely considered an act of suicide."

**The seam, and ours in it.** The record never says who turned a *description* into a *working* — who built the shell. In this campaign it was Kaan's people, from a dead Grumani lord's archive: **Odion's Project Pandemonium** — a decade of research into binding a crowd's anguish into dark-side energy and projecting it, whose only finished product was a Helm that drove three armies to kill each other and *overloaded on joy*. The Brotherhood inherited the principalities; it inherited Pandemonium's papers with them; and the thing that makes the Ruusan bomb **seal** rather than merely kill is the Helm's own behavior scaled to a thousand souls. Nobody alive knows this: the Brotherhood died with it. But it is written down — Pandemonium's notes and the Brotherhood's engineering annotations — in a dead nihilist's archive somewhere in the vaults the Scrub is emptying, and it is the only place in the galaxy the bomb's *key* is described. That is why the true Sith line's hunt for the record is not about embarrassment. It is about the lock.

## Seeds this file adds (GM-placeable; Act 1–2)

1. **The Thirteenth Statue.** A Daiman statue — cortosis-plated, headless — turns up as Memory Market provenance. The head was taken by a green lightsaber. Whoever authenticates the cut authenticates a Jedi who never left. *(Civic fragment for the verdict Question — what the Run had that nobody sent.)*
2. **The Cloister That Wasn't Opened.** One of Odion's twelve, on a dead spur, sealed under its spaceport since 1032 — children's bubble-cells, a control room, a lights-out switch. Ninety years empty. Or not: the Vigil taped it and never went in; the Scrub did. The Unbroken Seal's cruelest venue.
3. **The Promisorium Still Teaching.** Syned's rotation-state survived the Withering because it was *built* to survive change — a Five Systems that rotates its councilors annually and will rotate the crew's certification clerk out of the job mid-negotiation. Its founding portrait is a woman with a double-bladed saber-staff.
4. **The Celegian Relay.** Under Hestobyll, the Unity's linkage still hums — nothing on the end of it. The Last Muster's cache uses it; a gifted PC who touches it hears the switch *and* remembers being a city.
5. **The Spike.** Odion's corruptor factory was never given a fate. A modular station drifting dark on a spur, its floors decentralized, its labor droids (one reprogrammed by a Jedi, once) still waiting for orders — with the machines that turn a world into a magma sea still on the line. The Admiralty would want it. So would the Founding cadre's enemies.
6. **The Bequest Pylon.** Vilia's holo-pylon star map — the family's own chart of who held what — is the single best navigational fragment in the sector for the Scrub's *catalogue*: it is the catalogue. Her garden world is the one Sith holding nobody has claimed to have cleared.
7. **Grace Command's Ledger.** An Alderaanian charity's shipping manifests from 1032, still in a Republic archive under seal — the verdict Question's darkest rung (the Republic's side of the atrocity ledger), and the Trooper's inheritance.
8. **Devaad's Route.** The refugee road out of the sector ran *through Aquilaris's harvester stations* — a hidden lane the Lamplighters never charted because it was never a beacon lane. Osk's grandmother may have flown it.

## Where the record disagrees with itself

A fresh GM will meet these; pick one and stay consistent. Chelloa's evacuees: **60,000** vs **63,000**; its towns: ten vs "only seven remaining." Odion's cloisters: "at least twelve" vs thirteen (twelve *besides* Vanahame). Vanahame: moon vs planet. The Holts' trail ended at Sarrassia in **1038** vs **1039 BBY**. Narsk's suit: Mark **VI** (most sources) vs Mark IV. Jubalene: Odion's "capital" vs "temporary throne world" vs Treece's "unclear whether there even is one." Chagras's death: 1040 (most) vs 1042. Skarpos: "stormy, trenched" vs "bleak desert." Malakite's fliers: Night-Soarers vs Stenaxes. Zodoh's weapon: Mandalorian axe vs vibroblade. Uleeta: Woostoid vs Woostri. The Gazzari students: "hundreds" / "about a thousand" / 1,700 recruited / **1,717 aboard the *Diligence* including 560 crew.** Yulan: "Mandalorian" vs "Tarro" mercenary. And one outright error to ignore: the Arkadianate article places the Helm's discovery on Gazzari (it was Skarpos) and Arkadia's prison at the Loft (that is the Dyarchy's palace; hers is the Impound).
